\section{Exact computational witnesses and reproducibility}\label{sec:evidence}

The companion programs are bounded C++17 algebra checks. Every executable
installs a Windows job limit of 64 MiB process memory and 10 process
CPU seconds before starting algebra, and refuses to continue if the
limit installation fails. Fixed arrays bound every instance. The checks
use neither a growing dense transform matrix nor an enumeration of the
enormous role graph. Compilation is separate from these runtime limits.

The evaluator witness uses the ordinary pairwise Pascal core on its
small instances. It independently compares the resulting full map
with Horner evaluation, the radix extraction with monic polynomial
division, and the actual transpose with literal binary Vandermonde
columns. Its local Karatsuba/module/Gram implementation is compared
against an independent bit-serial ambient field implementation.
Both radix roundtrips and the distinct inverse transpose are checked.

The full product witness composes native input embedding, affine shift,
the new prefix evaluator, pointwise multiplication, the weights
\eqref{eq:omega}, and the decoder \eqref{eq:decoder}. It compares
against a separate schoolbook carryless product and reduction. Arbitrary
ambient covectors exercise evaluator and decoder transposes and the
complete fixed-operand product adjoint; tests do not restrict those
covectors to primal images or to smaller subfields.

The saved reports in \path{witnesses/code/} were produced by the bounded
programs in the reviewed Pascal/Taylor source packet on 30 October 2026.
They are historical receipts copied with their exact sources, not runs
made for this focused release. The first covers $K=1,\ldots,5$ and records
1,481,563 assertions, including 47,376 local/full-field phase comparisons.
The second covers $K=1,\ldots,4$ and records 784,218 assertions. The
included \path{witnesses/verify-witnesses.ps1} script can reproduce these
finite checks under the stated per-process guard. The witnesses use
ordinary finite Pascal cores; they neither execute nor certify the
asymptotic fast-H2 machine or the $O(n\log n)$ theorem.
