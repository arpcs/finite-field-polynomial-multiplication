\section{Sibling fusion, local scalar costs and evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Evaluate the outer prefix before translating the inner rows}
After Lemma~\ref{lem:radix}, scale digit $t$ by $c_l^t$ and, for
each inner monomial coefficient, evaluate the outer polynomial on
$W_r$. This produces the degree-$<M$ polynomials
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outer}
 g_v(X)=\sum_{t<\ell}c_t(X)c_l^tv^t,\qquad v\in W_r.
\end{equation}
For $x\in W_m$, Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilator} gives
$f(x+u^lv)=g_v(x+u^lv)$. Hence translate $g_v$ by
$\delta_v=u^lv$ and evaluate the result on $W_m$.
For $v=0$ the translation is the identity. For every $v\ne0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase}
 \operatorname{Taylor}_{\delta_v}
 =\operatorname{diag}(\delta_v^{-s})\,
   \mathcal P_M\,
   \operatorname{diag}(\delta_v^t).
\end{equation}
This is the coefficient identity
$[X^s]g(X+\delta)=\sum_{t\geq s}\binom ts g_t\delta^{t-s}$.

Thus there are two scalar actions per coefficient in the translation,
plus the separate outer scale. There is no scalar action at every
Pascal butterfly level. Evaluating \eqref{eq:outer} first is important:
$\delta_v$ is now a public field element, so no symbolic two-variable
coefficient box is expanded when $r\ll m$. The zero row bypasses
\eqref{eq:phase}, and its transpose bypasses it by the same identity
map. In particular no inverse of zero is requested.

\subsection{Native child fields, even for large ambient payloads}
At the node with macro power $a$, all phases and scales belong to
\[
 B_a=\F_2[T]/\Phi_{9a}(T),\qquad d=6a=\Theta(k).
\]
This field has $\F_4$ dimension $3a$, regardless of whether $m=a$ or
$m=2a$. It would be incorrect to demand a subfield of dimension $3m$
in the second case. Let $h_0=3A/a$; the powers of three divide, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:module}
 T=Y^{h_0},\qquad
 \Phi_{27A}(Y)=\Phi_{9a}(Y^{h_0}),\qquad
 E=\bigoplus_{j<h_0}Y^j B_a,\quad D=h_0d.
\end{equation}
This chart is the literal coefficient permutation $i=j+h_0t$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform short scalar action]\label{lem:scalar}
Put $\alpha=\log_2 3$ and $\rho=\alpha-1<1$.
Multiplication by a public element of $B_a$ on an arbitrary $E$ word,
and its ordinary binary transpose, cost $O(Dk^\rho)$, uniformly even
when $D/k$ is arbitrarily large.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Pack the $d\times h_0$ coefficient rectangle in
$O(D\log\min(d,h_0))$ time. A fixed-tape construction recursively
bisects the shorter dimension, transforms its two parts and merges
corresponding row fragments from two sequential streams. At every
depth the total moved volume is $O(D)$ and there are
$O(\log\min(d,h_0))$ depths. The inverse and the actual transpose
permutation have the same cost. Record-length counters use amortized
ripple updates; parameter initialization is at most the volume of the
current stream, not a $\log D$ charge per bit.

Binary polynomial Karatsuba multiplication~\cite{KaratsubaOfman62} costs $K(d)=O(d^\alpha)$ on fixed tapes.
Pad once per local product to the next power of two. Three half-size
products, sequential XORs and depth-first parking give
$K(2s)\leq3K(s)+O(s)$. Parent storage is parked locally and not crossed
by a descendant. The native modulus is $T^{2s}+T^s+1$, with $d=2s$.
A raw product split into four aligned $s$-bit blocks reduces by
\[
 A_0+T^sA_1+T^{2s}A_2+T^{3s}A_3
 \longmapsto (A_0+A_2+A_3)+T^s(A_1+A_2).
\]
This needs $O(d)$ sequential moves and XORs. Apply the same scalar
to the $h_0$ components in \eqref{eq:module}. The total cost is
\[
 O(D\log d+h_0d^\alpha)=O(Dk^\rho).
\]

For the transpose, let $K_d$ be the coefficient-residue Gram matrix
for $\lambda(a)=[T^{d-1}]a$ modulo that native modulus. Its entries
are one exactly when $i+j=2s-1$ or $3s-1$.
Let $J_d$ reverse $d$ coordinates and let $N$ copy the last $s$
input coordinates into the first $s$ outputs, zero elsewhere. Then
\[
 K_d=J_d(I+N),\qquad N^2=0,\qquad
 K_d^{-1}=(I+N)J_d.
\]
Associativity of $\lambda(ab)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-transpose}
 M_c^{\T}=K_d M_c K_d^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Thus two explicit linear-cost maps and one actual scalar product
compute the transpose, with the same bound. Transpose the packing
permutation literally. This proof uses no inverse-Frobenius rule and
no trace-adjoint substitution for an ordinary coordinate transpose.
\end{proof}

The positive and negative powers in \eqref{eq:phase} are generated
sequentially in the $d$-bit field, for each outer label $v$. There
are $O(M\ell)$ such short products. Their time
$O(M\ell d^\alpha)$ is at most $O(M\ell Dk^\rho)$.
One field inversion per nonzero $v$ may use any fixed polynomial-time
algorithm in $k$: $M\geq4^{k/2}$ absorbs this per-label setup.
Fixed small $k$ uses direct fixed-subfield maps on each native word.
Likewise the basis powers, native indices and inverses of the scales
$c_l$ are public short-field computations. No generic ambient-field
product is used at a small recursive node.

The separate outer scale in \eqref{eq:outer} is charged as well.
Generate the $\ell$ powers $c_l^t$ with $O(\ell)$ products in $B_a$,
then apply them to the $M\ell$ native $E$ words using
Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar}. The total is
\[
 O(\ell d^\alpha+M\ell Dk^\rho)=O(M\ell Dk^\rho).
\]
The ordinary transpose uses the same local Gram formula. With an
external batch of $B_0$ independent lanes the data-action cost is
multiplied by $B_0$, while the public powers are generated once.

\subsection{The full recurrence and all transposes}
Choose a fixed
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
 \max(\beta,\gamma,\rho)<\theta<1.
\end{equation}
Let $F_D(k)$ be the time to evaluate an arbitrary degree-$<4^k$
polynomial over $E$ on $W_k$, with the fixed top width $D$.
Radix extraction, outer scaling, the shared Pascal/Taylor layer,
public setup and rectangular reorientation together cost
$O(4^kDk^\theta)$. Equations~\eqref{eq:outer}--\eqref{eq:phase}
therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 F_D(k)\leq4^rF_D(m)+4^mF_D(r)+C4^kDk^\theta.
\end{equation}
The $4^r$ inner evaluations are identical maps on separate coefficient
lists, after the distinct translations have been performed. They can
be streamed as one batched map, with the batch index an opaque payload
coordinate. The same applies to the outer evaluations. Scalar actions
remain componentwise on the original $D$-bit field words. This batching
does not reinterpret a list of words as a larger field and does not
reload a parent array once for every child. Copying into and out of
the local child I/O interval contributes to the displayed node toll.

More precisely, strengthen the interface to
$\operatorname{Eval}(k,D,B_0)$, with stream shape
$[4^k][B_0][D]$ and volume $V=B_04^kD$. After radix extraction the
shape is $[\ell][M][B_0][D]$. The outer call is
$\operatorname{Eval}(r,D,B_0M)$, consuming the whole current array.
Perform the $v$-dependent translations, reorient the two local fields
$[\ell][M]$ to $[M][\ell]$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:rectangle}, and call
$\operatorname{Eval}(m,D,B_0\ell)$ on that same whole array. The
external lane remains an identity factor and the field degree remains
$D$. Thus the uniform time satisfies
\[
 T_D(k,B_0)\le T_D(r,B_0M)+T_D(m,B_0\ell)
       +CVk^\theta.
\]
Normalizing by $V$ gives the same recurrence as
\eqref{eq:recurrence}. Only descriptors are suspended on the parent
stack: all current data belong to the active child, so there is no
inactive sibling array to park or replay. Header initialization is
charged once per call, and amortized ripple counters charge record
boundaries to their scans. The local reorientation depends on $k$,
not on the size of the external lane.

\begin{lemma}[All-prefix recurrence]\label{lem:recurrence}
The recurrence \eqref{eq:recurrence} implies
$F_D(k)=O(4^kDk)$ with constants independent of the top width $D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Normalize by $4^kD$ and denote the resulting cost by $T(k)$.
If $k=a+r$, expand the power-three child $a$ into three $a/3$
children using its two macro steps. The new children have heights
$a/3,a/3,a/3,r$. If $k=2a+r$, first split $2a$ into two $a$
children and expand both, obtaining six $a/3$ children and $r$.
All bounded cases are included directly. In each case the child
heights $x_i$ sum to $k$, each is at most $k/2$, and only a bounded
number of tolls $O(k^\theta)$ has been introduced. Since
\[
 \sum_i x_i^\theta
 \geq(k/2)^{\theta-1}\sum_i x_i
 =2^{1-\theta}k^\theta,
\]
induction with the upper potential $C_0k-Lk^\theta$ succeeds after
choosing $L$ to dominate the toll divided by
$2^{1-\theta}-1$, and $C_0$ to cover the fixed bases.
\end{proof}

The ordinary transpose reverses inner evaluations, the two actual
scalar transposes in \eqref{eq:phase}, the Pascal transpose, outer
evaluations, scales, and radix extraction. Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar}
and the explicit shift/carry transposes give the same recurrence on
unrestricted covectors. The inverse also has the same bound: invert
the children and scales, use the same characteristic-two translation,
then the separately proved synthesis \eqref{eq:radix-inverse}.

Evaluation on the affine grid $Y+W_K$ is one initial translation by
$Y$ followed by this evaluator. Its diagonal factors are native
$Y^i$ and $Y^{-s}$ actions. Reduce their exponents modulo $27A$;
cyclic shifts in a $27A$-slot representation and an aligned fold cost
$O(D)$ per word. Its Pascal call is covered by
Contract~\ref{con:tensor}. It follows that affine evaluation and its
ordinary transpose both cost $O(bDK)$. This global affine shift is
performed once, not at every recursive node.
