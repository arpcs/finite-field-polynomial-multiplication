\begin{abstract}
We construct a deterministic algorithm for multiplying two ordinary
degree-$<n$ polynomials over $\F_2$ in
$\Oh(n\log^{1-1/1302}(n+2))$ bit time on a machine with a fixed finite
alphabet and a fixed finite number of sequential tapes. The construction
uses a finite family of paired Fourier phase operators, an integer
composition of jobs of different ranks, and an interchange algorithm
obtained from a linear-time-per-address-bit seed. A tensor algorithm on
mixed-radix address arrays then supplies the kernels needed by a
cyclotomic Chinese remainder reduction. The bound includes input and
output conversion, public setup, coefficient arithmetic, tape movement,
workspace restoration and all $2n-1$ ordinary product coefficients.
The technical appendices give the finite word, recursive schedules and
cost substitution. Exact finite certificates accompany the finite
identities and rank inequality.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Background and contribution}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Background and contribution}
Fast polynomial multiplication over finite fields builds on a substantial
body of work in arithmetic complexity and structured Fourier transforms.
Harvey, van der Hoeven and Lecerf~\cite{hvhl} obtained the fixed-prime
bound $\Oh(n\log n\,8^{\log^*n})$; Harvey and van der Hoeven~\cite{host}
subsequently obtained $\Oh(n\log n\,4^{\log^*n})$ and developed the
cyclotomic coefficient-ring methods used here. Their later theorem gives
$\Oh(n\log n)$ over each fixed finite field under an explicit Linnik
hypothesis~\cite[Theorem 1.2]{ffnlogn}. Van der Hoeven and Larrieu's Frobenius
FFT~\cite{frobenius} gives a complementary approach that exploits
finite-field symmetries, with applications to binary polynomial
multiplication. These results provide important arithmetic and transform
foundations for the present work.

The earlier controlled-CRT manuscript~\cite{release} states a saving
$\kappa=2^{-38}$ in the logarithmic exponent for ordinary binary
polynomial multiplication. The present revision states $\kappa=1/1302$
for the same coefficient input/output problem and deterministic
fixed-tape model. It retains the cyclotomic host and controlled-CRT outer
framework while developing the paired-phase construction, integer
mixed-rank compiler and movement bootstrap used for the improved bound.
The finite-network and local Fourier methods of OpenAI~\cite{swap,local},
Sussman's mixed-job construction~\cite{sussman}, and the finite
precompilation and five-stage constructions of Colkitt and
contributors~\cite{mixed-precompile,five-stage} supply essential
antecedents. The contribution studied here is their adaptation and
composition with fully charged ordinary binary polynomial input and
output. The cited final integer-multiplication and complex-arithmetic
bounds are not premises of this theorem.

\paragraph{Relationship to the companion papers.}
The Pascal/Taylor companion~\cite{pascal-companion} gives a separate
binary $\Oh(n\log(n+2))$ construction. Its complete multiplier is not a
subroutine of this proof. The general finite-field
companion~\cite{general-companion} combines the earlier controlled-CRT
binary result with an independent odd-characteristic construction.
The present proof does not invoke that combined conclusion, so this
dependency is not circular. This revision concerns $\F_2$; the other
selected field statements retain their own bounds, sources and evidence.
The improvement is an asymptotic claim, with no practical speedup or
exhaustive priority determination asserted.

\section{Statement, model and proof dependencies}
Input consists of two ascending coefficient lists of length $n$ over
$\F_2$. Output consists of all $2n-1$ ordinary coefficients, including
zeros. One deterministic machine has a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed
finite number of one-dimensional sequential tapes. Heads move one cell
per step. Shape descriptors are ordinary binary integers; address
coordinates are public positions and never input-payload controls.
The two input tapes contain the end-marked bit strings
$a_0\cdots a_{n-1}$ and $b_0\cdots b_{n-1}$, with heads at their
first symbols. All work tapes and the output tape are initially blank.
At termination the output tape contains the end-marked string
$c_0\cdots c_{2n-2}$, where
$c_i=[X^i](\sum_j a_jX^j)(\sum_k b_kX^k)$; its head is returned
to the first symbol. Inputs may be preserved by copying them once.
Counting, copying, final head returns and erasure of marked work
intervals are included in the time bound.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is such a machine multiplying ordinary degree-$<n$ polynomials in
$\F_2[X]$, for every $n\ge1$, in
\[\Oh\!\left(n\log^{1-1/1302}(n+2)\right)\]
bit time. The constants and finite direct-branch cutoff are effective
and fixed independently of $n$.
\end{theorem}

The time bound counts all transitions of the machine. A subroutine's
volume is the number of bits in its current input array, including
spectator coordinates, retained padding and coefficient lanes. Traversal
of suspended data is charged to the caller that performs it.

The cyclotomic host and principal-root construction is the deterministic
construction of Harvey and van der Hoeven~\cite[Propositions 3.4,3.8]{host}.
Short auxiliary products use classical fixed-tape integer multiplication
\cite{ss}; they do not call the algorithm being constructed. The outer
reduction develops the algorithms of~\cite{release}, with its costs
derived from the kernels proved here in Appendix~\ref{app:outer}.
The finite-network and local Fourier methods have antecedents in
\cite{swap,local}; mixed-rank composition and finite precompilation are
developed in~\cite{sussman,mixed-precompile,five-stage}.
These works are used through the constructions stated below, rather than
through a transfer of their final integer or complex-arithmetic bounds.

The proof is organized into three steps. First a finite all-input word
and an integer composition argument produce a uniform rank-$N$ template.
Its rational address interpretation gives a fast interchange procedure.
Its characteristic-two interpretation, implemented on every active and
idle address pattern, gives the native tensor kernels. Finally a
controlled Chinese remainder and Fourier reduction returns the ordinary
coefficient list. Appendices~\ref{app:finite-unit}--\ref{app:outer}
provide the full constructions and their cost interfaces.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
A native array is an ordinary lexicographic stream indexed by a Cartesian
product of integer ranges. A productive port has arbitrary independent
input and receives its specified operator. A paid query is a recursive
kernel invocation charged on its complete input stream. Owned arrays
are auxiliary streams initialized within the program; their queries
are charged, and they receive no independent productive credit. A dirty
address coordinate may have any initial value and must be restored at
the stated completed subroutine boundary.
\section{The finite native word and integer composition}
Let $\eta^2+\eta+1=0$ in $\F_4$. For $M\in\F_3^{e\times e}$,
define an operator on $\F_4$-valued functions on
$\F_3^e\times\F_3^e$ by
\[K_M=\mathcal F^{-1}\operatorname{diag}_{\xi,\zeta}
 (\eta^{\xi^TM\zeta})\mathcal F.\]
Here $\mathcal Ff(\xi)=\sum_x\eta^{\xi^Tx}f(x)$, and its inverse
uses negative exponents. In dimension $m=2e$, its normalizing scalar is $3^{-m}=1$ in
characteristic two. Fourier notation proves identities; these transforms
are not invoked as fast subroutines. We have $K_MK_L=K_{M+L}$.
With $U_Af(x)=f(A^{-1}x)$, conjugation by
$A=\operatorname{diag}(G,G^{-T})$ sends $K_M$ to $K_{GMG^{-1}}$
and fixes $K_I$. The separate reflection
$\operatorname{diag}(I-2P,I)$ inverts $K_P$ and fixes $K_Q$ if $PQ=0$.
A rank-$e$ paired job uses $2e$ active trits, not $e$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite all-input unit]\label{lem:unit}
There is an effective finite native program, valid at every column
multiplicity $f$. For a fixed positive integer $C$, its productive
and paid profiles are respectively $CT$ and $CP$, where
\[T=\{8:18910,22:240,36:240\}\]
and
\[P=\{1:27445,2:10400,3:2200,4:4340,5:7760,6:8120,7:765\}.
\]
Every public role has arbitrary independent input. Owned work is
initialized, queried and disposed without productive credit. At
$s_0=1-1/1300$, $\sum_rP_rr^{s_0}<\sum_kT_kk^{s_0}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the 120 integer root lines $2e_i\pm2e_j$ and
$(1,\pm1,\ldots,\pm1)$ with even sign parity. Each has norm eight;
distinct inner products are $0,\pm4$. Seven coordinate-match bases
and eight simplex-code cosets partition them into fifteen orthogonal
bases. The explicit two-tree rectangle list in Appendix~\ref{app:finite-unit} partitions
all 7560 directed orthogonal contacts once. Its source/target size
histogram is
\[\begin{gathered}
(1,1):1624,\quad (1,2):800,\quad (1,4):232,\\
(2,1):752,\quad (2,2):108,\quad (2,4):20,\\
(4,1):208,\quad (4,2):16,\quad (4,4):22.
\end{gathered}\]
The binary fitting $B$ has an explicit nine-center factorization. At
root scale $\alpha=v/2$, choose the integral E8 simple-root Gram $S$.
Its three arms have lengths $1,2,4$, so
\[\det S=30(2-1/2-2/3-4/5)=1.\]
For $d_s=(\alpha_s\cdot\beta_i)_i$ the entries are integral, and
$B_{st}=1+d_s^TS^{-1}d_t$ modulo two. Thus $(1,d_s^T)$ and
$(1,S^{-1}d_t)^T$ give $RG=B$. The rectangle incidences give $JV=B+I$.

On each rectangle, grow source flags from leaves to root and target
flags from root to leaves. All XOR contacts are then in a common frame.
A side of dimensions $(a,b)$ has paid jumps $a,8-a-b,b$ and its own
rank-eight endpoint on arbitrary data. Each main flag path has jumps
$1,2,4$. Nine owned centers are gathered at the full frame, queried on
disjoint rank-seven and rank-one pieces, scattered and erased. The
resulting raw main call is $D\operatorname{Lower}(Q^{-1})$ with
$Q=K_P$, $D=K_{I-P}$; arbitrary incoming side dirt cancels exactly.

For $a^3=1$ the five integral transvections
$I+\lambda_i u_i\phi_i$ in Appendix~\ref{app:finite-unit} satisfy
$T_5\cdots T_1=\operatorname{diag}(a,a,a^2,a^2)$ in
$\mathbb Z[a]/(a^3-1)$. At stages four and five their supports lie in
banks zero and two. Apply the complements only on these two banks at
those stages. The full endpoints are then rank $36$ on two banks and
rank $22$ on two banks. Helpers remain independent rank-eight outputs.
This removes exactly 480 inactive rank-seven calls, not their productive
rank mass alone. Including the 45 split owned queries gives the displayed
invoice per cover row, with normalized target/paid rank masses 165200
and 165080. The finite cover multiplies both profiles by the same $C$.

The native identities use commuting projectors and the literal chronology
in common frames, at every column multiplicity $f$. They do not distribute
tensor powers through sums. Appendix~\ref{app:finite-unit} specifies the
five directions, frames and full invoice. Directed integer bounds for logarithms and exponentials
give, after division by $36^{s_0}$,
\[\text{paid}\le\frac{46549475589303981235}{10133099161583616}
 <\frac{46549523863948677520}{10133099161583616}\le\text{target}.
\]
The certificate evaluates exact finite inequalities, not growing-size
timings. Its polynomial-ring multiplication also records all intermediate
bank matrices, and its frozen-list verifier exports every rectangle.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite uniform column body]\label{lem:body}
For any fixed $s_0<z_0<z<1$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there are effective fixed integers $N,B,P$
and an all-input column template for $K_{I_N}$ on $B$ independent arrays
using $P$ rank-one queries, including owned queries, with $P/B<\varepsilon N^z$.
The same mixed construction has a rational order-three address
interpretation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\widetilde T_k=CT_k$ and $\widetilde P_r=CP_r$ be the actual
integer unit multiplicities. For a target composition $a$ put
$c(a)=\prod k^{a_k}$ and
$u(a)=\binom{|a|}{a}\prod\widetilde T_k^{a_k}$. Removing the last
colored letter gives $u(b)=\sum_k\widetilde T_ku(b-e_k)$.
At top rank $n$ choose $G$ maximal
with $36\,8^G\le n$. An ordered paid path has multiplier $\rho$; the
target of type $k$ at child $b-e_k$ matches the pending rank-$r$ request
since $k\rho r c(b-e_k)=\rho r c(b)$. Its ambient fits because $r<k$.
Unmatched sources satisfy $n/36<s\le n$; only terminal paid exits
remain, at rank at most $E(n)=7(n/36)^{\log_8 7}=o(n)$.

Finish an entire matched child class before resuming its paid parent
round. Ordered paths and live owned banks have separate tags. Integer
replication clears every smaller batch denominator. Complete each
source's disjoint $n-s$ complement before using its rank-$s$ endpoint.
With $L(n)=P_n/B_n$ the literal construction gives
\[L(n)\le\sum_sw_sL(n-s)+\sum_ex_eL(e),\qquad
\sum_sw_s=1,\quad \sum_ex_ee^{s_0}\le\sum_sw_ss^{s_0}.\]
Set $\delta=z_0-s_0>0$. Then
\[\sum_ex_ee^{z_0}\le(36E(n)/n)^\delta\sum_sw_ss^{z_0},\qquad
n^{z_0}-\sum_sw_s(n-s)^{z_0}\ge z_0 36^{z_0-1}\sum_sw_ss^{z_0}.\]
Strong induction gives $L(n)\le A n^{z_0}$. Taking $N$ so that
$A N^{z_0-z}<\varepsilon$ supplies any required fixed margin.
Rational bounds give an effective cutoff. Splitting, whole-job
bases and the all-input unit commute with replacing each coordinate
slot by $f$ columns, so the body is valid at every $f$.
The rational interpretation uses the actual root projectors $vv^T/8$
and $I+(R-I)\otimes P$, $R=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Its native identities use the same annihilating projectors and scalar
XOR word; rational matchers are frozen before a residue prime is chosen.
\end{proof}

\section{Ordinary movement from a linear seed}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:movement}
For every fixed rational $s_0<t<1$ there is a uniform equal-chunk interchange
machine costing $\Oh(V(u+1)^t)$ on
$[P_0][2^u][G_0][2^u][B_0]$, with arbitrary positive prefix, gap,
suffix and payload. Aggregate rectangles of address width at most $L$
cost $\Oh(VL^g)$, $g=1/(2-t)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The normalizers and cost induction are summarized here; the complete
row, range and tape constructions are in Appendix~\ref{app:movement}.
Freeze Lemma~\ref{lem:body}, then choose
prime $q\ge5$ avoiding its finitely many denominators and witnesses.
A generic rank-one address update has split rank two. With $E,F$ the
first two head and last two tail selectors, write
\[\begin{aligned}
M-I&=\binom E{B_1}(A_1,DF^T),\\
H&=A_1E+DF^TB_1,\\
K&=(-B_1+FD^{-1}H)E^T+FD^{-1}A_1(I-EE^T).
\end{aligned}\]
Then $KE=-B_1+FD^{-1}H$, $DF^TK=A_1$, and
\[L(K)ML(-K)=I+\binom E{FD^{-1}H}(0,DF^T).\]
Only four coarse fields remain. No $H^{-1}$ is used. Gaussian lowering
over the local ring uses unit pivots and at most 43 smaller swaps; use
64. Bad charts use $32N^2$; their fraction is at most $16N^2/q$ by the
cleared polynomial bounds and equal high-lift counts.

For a completed output $M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X&B_1\\C&D_1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
with $M^2+M+I=0$ and invertible $B_1$, lower adapters give
$[0,B_1;-B_1^{-1},-I]$, then $J_A=[0,A;A^{-1},0]$, $A=-B_1$.
The identities $J_AL(-A^{-2})J_A=U(-I)$ and
$J_I=\operatorname{NegHead}L(I)U(-I)L(I)$ remove the full matrix weight
with two bodies. On bad output rows the bodies still run; $J_IM$
corrects $M^2$. Choose $N$, then $q$, so the normalized child $z$-moment
is $<3/4$. The normalized stopped recurrence is
\[T(n,w)\le\sum_jc_jT(\lfloor n/N\rfloor,w)+C_Nn+C_N(w+1)^b,
\qquad T(n,w)=\Oh_N((w+1)^b)\ (n<N),\]
where $b\le1$ is the predecessor ordinary movement exponent. Both strict
moments give $\Oh_N(n+n^z(w+1)^b)$.

Fixed right matchers act affinely on high matrices over each fixed low
residue type; predecessor swaps and fixed-unit residue lanes price
them. Variable unit pivots use the charged smaller children. The
complete q-adic row stock is reserved once, with
$\Oh_N(w\log(s+2))=o(s)$ high digits when $w=\lceil s^{1-z}\rceil$.
No new child reapplies the ordinary wrapper or root padding. Crop the
private row pad after a full body, then finish predecessor high/remainder
swaps before cropping the original binary box.

The ordinary exponent update is
$b_{j+1}=z+(1-z)(b_j-z)$, starting from $b_0=1$ by explicit four-stream
bit-pair exchange. Each fixed successor calls its predecessor, never
itself at atom width $w$. Thus $b_j=z+(1-z)^{j+1}\to z$. The number
of tape families and bootstrap passes is fixed before input varies.
Finally Euclidean equal-block exchanges above threshold $a$ cost
$\Oh(VLa^{t-1})$ and the last short field costs $\Oh(Va)$; take
$a=\lceil L^{1/(2-t)}\rceil$. Each whole native aggregate range is
padded once and cropped after its completed rectangle.
\end{proof}

\section{Full-mask endpoint execution}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:endpoint}
Let $M=\prod_i n_i$, $L=\lceil\log_2(2M)\rceil$, and let the native
radices $n_i$ be even, with $L^3\le n_i\le R_0$,
$\log R_0=\operatorname{polylog}L$, and $V=DM$, $D\ge1$.
For fixed rational $s_0<t<\ell<1$ the canonical adjacent-pair
$H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ tensor
costs $\Oh(VL^{\max(\ell,g)}\operatorname{polylog}L)$.
Its inverse and literal ordinary bit transpose have the same bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The weighted eight-update tile word of Appendix~\ref{app:native-endpoint}
flips chosen endpoint bits while restoring its dirty auxiliary. Dock
only the selected native factor of $u$ digits, padded to its next binary
range, through its actual gap. A high prefix pads whole opposite-factor
rows; a low prefix pads each low-factor block. Both cost $\Oh(V)$ and
expose $\Oh(u\log R_0)$ bits, not the whole bank. Private pads survive
the eight updates and exact repair and are cropped before a phase child.

The quotient-margin bad set $\mathcal B$ has fraction $\Oh(u/L^3)$.
The ideal $T$ preserves quotient labels, so $T(\mathcal B)=\mathcal B$.
The packed $P$ fixes the private invalid tails and agrees with $T$ on
$\mathcal B^c$ in the same padded domain. Both are bijections, hence
\[P(\mathcal B)=\Omega\setminus P(\mathcal B^c)
=\Omega\setminus T(\mathcal B^c)=T(\mathcal B)=\mathcal B.\]
This derives the repair-slot invariant rather than assuming it. At a bad output
$y$, sort the whole record by key $T(P^{-1}y)$. Stage a complete item
before selecting its stable radix bucket. With $K$ bad items, $b=\Oh(L)$
key bits and opaque width $R_{\rm op}$, this costs
$\Oh(Kb(R_{\rm op}+b)+V+M_{\rm rec}L^C)$, including all head returns.
Refill only marked holes. Five existing endpoint banks and the native
suffix provide dirty selectors, a dock, $\Oh(\log h)$ role bits and
$R_{\rm op}=2^{\Omega(L)}D$, which pays all fixed metadata polynomials.

Pair endpoint bits into four-state digits; idle state 3 is kept in place.
Dirty-selector echoes implement disjoint active-rank SUM, negation and
swaps. All masks remain in the array. For a paired rank-$e$ call select
the first $2e$ active trits, using prefix
$\min(h,\lceil C(2e+\log(L+2))\rceil)$. Correct only completed logical
routes with restored dirty selectors. The exceptional eligible prefix
fraction is exponentially small in the full physical universe, without
conditioning its distribution on eligibility. The ideal route preserves
the mask defining this exception; the truncated route is identity there
and equals the ideal elsewhere. The same destination-key repair therefore
applies before any linear combination forms a phase.
Every child inherits its parent's mask-only enable predicate, conjoined
with its local eligibility test. All its charts and translations use
that predicate; a disabled parent has identity endpoints on every role.
For the fixed body of Lemma~\ref{lem:body}, each query has actual volume
$V/B$, including owned queries. Reserve rows divisible by
$B^{\lceil\log_N(h+2)\rceil}$ once. Children retain every physical mask
and opaque lane. Parking/restoring siblings costs $\Oh_N(V)$ at their
parent; descendants never scan parked ancestors. Thus
\[F(e;L)\le(P/B)F(\lfloor e/N\rfloor;L)
+\operatorname{polylog}L\bigl((e+\log(L+2))^t+\operatorname{polylog}L\bigr).
\]
Unrolling uses $(P/B)/N^t<1$ after choosing the body exponent below $t$;
it gives $\Oh((e+1)^t\operatorname{polylog}L)$.

For $C=\eta I_3+\eta^2J_3$ the Fourier multiplier is $\eta^{\xi^2}$.
The explicit matrix
\[J=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&0\\1&0&1&1\\1&2&0&2\\0&2&1&2\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{satisfies}\quad\sum_i(Jx)_i^2=x_0x_2+x_1x_3.
\]
Since $\mathcal FU_Af(\xi)=\mathcal Ff(A^T\xi)$,
$U_{J^T}C^{\otimes4}U_{J^T}^{-1}=K_{I_2}$. Chronologically apply
$U_{J^T}$, then $K_{I_2}$, then its inverse chart to obtain $C^{\otimes4}$.
For each mask with $a$ active trits, expand $\lfloor a/4\rfloor$ in
base $N$. Move each consecutive $4N^j$-trit group by two active-coordinate
reflections, execute one paired rank-$2N^j$ job with that chart, and undo.
Process the at most three leftover active trits directly. All active
counts, including zero, are covered; no sector is gathered.

The exact positive $\F_{16}$ word in Appendix~\ref{app:positive-word}
has 384 forward $(C\oplus[1])$ letters and 392 monomials and equals
$H\otimes H$. Tensor its letters over a block, not separately per axis.
Monomial scalars are generated at source addresses; all four F16 bit
planes and pads survive the full word. Completed blocks are binary,
fix every borrowed index, and cost the displayed bound including
aggregate layouts. Polylogarithmically many blocks cover the axes.
The literal completed operator is symmetric; the same algorithm is its
bit transpose. Its inverse is two forward applications since $H^3=I$.
\end{proof}

\section{Ordinary transfer and explicit exponent}\label{sec:outer}
The explicit outer algorithms of~\cite{release} have the following
parameter ledger. With $\theta=\max(\ell,g)$, generic/ragged local
permutations, controlled tensors, the balanced CRT and local Fourier
words cost $\Oh(VL^\theta\operatorname{polylog}L)$. To verify the
substitution, adjacent matching slots use two $HJ$ blocks and unit
diagonals; ragged fragment topology is independent of switch settings.
Controlled scalars are read only at completed subset-kernel boundaries,
with the current shuffle gauge inverted. Their complete per-record
generator cost is $G(R_0,L)$, so the scalar bill is $MG$, not $MDG$.
The local formal triadic convolution DAG and dirty Toeplitz replay have
only polylogarithmically many such slots. Growing coefficient arithmetic
uses the independent short-product backend. No algebraic step uses
the numerical value of the older published exponent. The callsite table
and sequential generator bounds are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:outer}.

For completeness the ordinary boundary is explicit. The cited host
constructs $2n<N_*<4n$ and its root data in $N_*^{o(1)}$ time. Enlarge
its divisor to $\alpha'$ until $D'=\varphi(\alpha')\ge(R_0+L_0+5)^{64}$,
where $L_0=\lceil\log_2N_*\rceil$, $R_0=2^{\lceil\log_2L_0\rceil^2}$.
Then $m'=N_*/\alpha'$, $m'D'=\Theta(N_*)$ and
$1-D'/\alpha'=\Oh((\log L_0)^2/L_0^3)$. Root transport is the explicit
unital old-variable map $Y\mapsto U^J$ followed by the appropriate
$J$th power; new input words use a variable rename, not that substitution
a second time.

For the native grid, $M=m'\prod_{p\mid m'}(1+1/p)$ and
$\prod_{p\mid m'}(1+1/p)=1+\Oh(L_0^{-2})$. Since
$\alpha'>L_0^{64}$ and $\log\alpha'=o(L_0)$, its parameter satisfies
$L=\lceil\log_2(2M)\rceil\le L_0$ and $L=\Theta(L_0)$ for all sufficiently
large inputs. Thus $n_i=p_i+1>L_0^3\ge L^3$ and the endpoint hypotheses
hold. Ragged CRT and local Fourier slots keep these same full axes.

Map $X\mapsto Y^cZ$, $c=(m')^{-1}\bmod\alpha'$. An ordinary coefficient
index $k=r+m'j$ goes to $(r,cr+j\bmod\alpha')$. Split rows by
$f=\Phi_{\alpha'}$ and $\psi=(Y^{\alpha'}-1)/f$ and retain both streams.
For $uf+v\psi=1$ reconstruct as
$(A_fv\psi+A_\psi uf)\bmod(Y^{\alpha'}-1)$. The large stream uses
native axes $p_i+1$, the full dummy grid, and three complete DFTs with
CRT-idempotent local root exponents. The complement, with
$d=\deg\psi$, packs $Y^jZ^i\mapsto T^{2di+j}$, reduces rows and folds
$Z^{m'}=1$. Its classical cost is $\Oh(N_*(\log L_0)^3/L_0)=o(N_*)$.
Setup, scalar arithmetic and top maps cost $N_*\operatorname{polylog}L_0$
or $N_*^{o(1)}$. The complete bill is
\[\Oh(N_*L_0^\theta(\log L_0)^A)+N_*\operatorname{polylog}L_0+N_*^{o(1)}.
\]
Every private crop occurs at its proved completed boundary. Inverse
maps, all cyclic coefficients, stream erasure and head returns are
included. Since $2n-2<N_*$, the first $2n-1$ returned cyclic coefficients
are exactly the ordinary product.

To prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, put $u_0=1300/1301$,
$z=(s_0+u_0)/2$ and $d=1-z$. Two fixed movement bootstraps give
$t=z+d^3$. Since $u_0-z=1/3382600$ and
$d^3<1300^{-3}<1/3382600$, we have $t<u_0$. Therefore
\[g=1/(2-t)<1/(2-u_0)=1301/1302.\]
Choose $t<\ell<g$. Then $\theta=g$ and the positive fixed gap to
$1301/1302$ absorbs every fixed secondary logarithm. All choices are
made before input length varies. The finite initial range uses the
same fixed direct branch, completing the proof.

The ordinary fixed-operand transpose is also explicit. If $b$ is fixed,
$(T_b^Tc)_i=\sum_jb_jc_{i+j}$ is coefficient $n-1+i$ of
$(\sum_jb_jX^{n-1-j})(\sum_{k<2n-1}c_kX^k)$. Run the ordinary algorithm
at length $2n-1$, emit its full product and crop indices $n-1$ through
$2n-2$. Reversal, padding, cropping and returns add $\Oh(n)$.

\paragraph{Computational certificates.}
The accompanying exact certificates verify the finite field identities,
orthogonal rectangle partition and strict rank inequality used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:unit}. The asymptotic conclusion follows from the
recursive constructions and charged interfaces, not from finite timing
tests. Their scope is distinct from independent mathematical review or a
complete formal proof of the main theorem.
