\section{Integer composition of different-rank jobs}
\label{app:mixed-compiler}
We prove the column-template claim used by Lemma~\ref{lem:body}.
The argument is a specialization of the mixed-job and finite
precompilation mechanisms of~\cite{sussman,mixed-precompile}.
All jobs here have the exact all-input word of
Appendix~\ref{app:finite-unit}.

\subsection{Interfaces and whole-job matching}
A rank-$r$ paired job is $K_{I_r}$ on $2r$ selected trits and identity
on every spectator. Its $f$-column version is $K_{I_r\otimes I_f}$.
Disjoint completed jobs split by
$K_{I_{a+b}}=K_{I_a\oplus0}K_{0\oplus I_b}$.
An equal-rank idempotent is matched by bases of its image and kernel:
the coordinate permutation $\operatorname{diag}(G,G^{-T})$ conjugates
it to the required canonical job and preserves $K_I$. Every such
normalizer is a fixed finite slot map lifted by $I_f$.

For the rational address interpretation, replace the phase by
$I+(R_3-I)\otimes P$. Equal-rank completed jobs are similar by
rational bases. Inverse $R_3$ modules are rationally similar to
$R_3$, and spectators are matched as whole fixed spaces. There is
no assumption that the rational matcher group is finite. The finite
constructor records its finitely many right-route symbols for the
later residue cover.

\subsection{A finite integer flow}
Let $T_k$ and $P_r$ in this appendix denote the actual integer profiles
$C T_k^{\rm norm},C P_r^{\rm norm}$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:unit}, so
$r\le7<8\le k\le36$. For a target-type multi-index $a$ put
\[
 c(a)=\prod_k k^{a_k},\qquad
 u(a)=\binom{|a|}{a}\prod_k T_k^{a_k}.
\]
Regard its $u(a)$ copies as colored words of composition $a$; the
letter of type $k$ has $T_k$ possible colors. Removing the last
letter gives the bijection
\begin{equation}
 u(b)=\sum_{k:b_k>0}T_k u(b-e_k).
 \label{eq:colored-flow}
\end{equation}

For top rank $n\ge36$, take $G$ maximal with $36\,8^G\le n$.
At depth $d\le G$, retain each ordered paid-round path and its
multiplier $\rho$, the product of its preceding paid ranks. The
class of composition $a$, $|a|=G-d$, contains $u(a)$ units at column
multiplicity $\rho c(a)$ whenever $36\rho c(a)\le n$.
For a pending rank-$r$ request at parent composition $b$, the
child of type $k$ has column multiplier $\rho r c(b-e_k)$ and
productive rank
\[
 k\rho r c(b-e_k)=\rho r c(b).
\]
Its ambient fits because $r/k<1$. Equation~\eqref{eq:colored-flow}
matches every request to a distinct whole child output, including its
color. Missing parent slots become new independent source arrays.

Every such source has rank $n/36<s\le n$. Indeed a missing parent
fit gives $36\rho_{\rm parent}c(b)>n$ and hence
$s>rn/36\ge n/36$; the child's fit supplies the upper bound.
At depth zero maximality of $G$ gives the same lower bound.
Only depth-$G$ requests remain unmatched. Their rank is at most
\begin{equation}
 E(n)=7^{G+1}\le7(n/36)^{\log_8 7}=o(n).
 \label{eq:mixed-exits}
\end{equation}
An ordered path is not merged with another path merely because
their multipliers coincide.

The schedule is chronological and acyclic. Suspend a class at its
next paid round, execute the entire matching child class, restore
its routes and resume the parent. A pending owned increment is a
child input of its actual current value; it is not counted again as
an independent source. Each suspended owner has a distinct tag, and
its erase occurs only after return.

\subsection{Clearing denominators and paying complements}
Inductively suppose a rank-$e<n$ template uses $B_e$ independent
inputs and $P_e$ rank-one queries. A direct template has $B_e=1$,
$P_e=e$; set $B_0=1,P_0=0$. Let $S_s$ be the source counts in the
finite flow and $t_e$ its terminal exit counts. Replicate the entire
flow by a common positive multiple of every $B_{n-s}$ and $B_e$
that it uses. All smaller-template batch counts are now integers.
Complete each source's disjoint rank-$(n-s)$ complement before its
rank-$s$ flow endpoint, and complete each exit by the smaller
rank-$e$ template. These are actual paid source and exit jobs.
Every original source then receives the full rank-$n$ endpoint.

Writing $S=\sum_sS_s$, $w_s=S_s/S$, $x_e=t_e/S$ and
$L(n)=P_n/B_n$, this construction gives
\begin{equation}
 L(n)\le\sum_s w_sL(n-s)+\sum_e x_eL(e),\qquad
 \sum_s w_s=1.
 \label{eq:mixed-recurrence}
\end{equation}
The strict unit moment, multiplied by each unit's column multiplier
to the power $s_0$, is preserved under repetition. Matched internal
requests cancel against their productive endpoints, leaving
\begin{equation}
 \sum_e x_e e^{s_0}\le\sum_s w_s s^{s_0}.
 \label{eq:mixed-boundary-moment}
\end{equation}
This is an inequality on the finite constructed flow, including all
owned requests; it does not promote a smaller helper to a full output.

Choose $s_0<z_0<z<1$ and put $\delta=z_0-s_0$. By
\eqref{eq:mixed-exits} and $s>n/36$,
\[
 \sum_e x_ee^{z_0}
 \le(36E(n)/n)^\delta\sum_s w_ss^{z_0}.
\]
For $v=s/n\ge1/36$, concavity gives
$1-(1-v)^{z_0}\ge z_0v\ge z_0 36^{z_0-1}v^{z_0}$.
Consequently
\[
 n^{z_0}-\sum_s w_s(n-s)^{z_0}
 \ge z_0 36^{z_0-1}\sum_s w_ss^{z_0}.
\]
The first coefficient tends effectively to zero. Choose an effective
cutoff where it is smaller than the second coefficient, and enlarge
$A$ so that $L(n)\le A n^{z_0}$ on the finite direct range. Strong
induction in \eqref{eq:mixed-recurrence} proves the same bound for
every $n$. Each complement and exit rank is strictly smaller than
$n$. For any prescribed fixed positive rational $\varepsilon$, take
$N$ with $AN^{z_0-z}<\varepsilon$. Then
$P_N/B_N<\varepsilon N^z$.

The common cover multiplicity changes the integer color counts and
the effective constants $A,N,B_N,P_N$. It leaves the strict unit
moment, source-rank thresholds and exit exponent unchanged.
All replication factors, slot maps and word lengths are fixed finite
integers once $N$ is fixed. Induction through the constructor applies
at every column multiplicity $f$, because the unit, splitting and
whole-job matching were proved at every $f$. Its setup can therefore
be encoded in one fixed machine, or generated at a fixed charged
cost absorbed by the finite direct cutoff. No advice depending on
the eventual ordinary input is used.
