\begin{abstract}
For each fixed finite field $\F_q$, ordinary polynomial multiplication
has deterministic bit complexity $O_q(n(\log(n+2))^{1301/1302})$ on
a fixed finite-tape machine. For each fixed odd field $\F_{p^e}$, the
stronger bound $O_{p,e}(n(\log(n+2))^{319/320})$ follows from the
cyclotomic-host reduction proved here. The characteristic-two branch
uses the paired-phase binary theorem~\cite{PairedPhase} and an explicit
fixed-basis reduction costing a constant number of binary products
plus linear coefficient conversion and recombination. Both branches
return all $2n-1$ ordinary coefficients and charge setup, arithmetic,
tape movement, output and cleanup. The common exponent is the larger
of the two field-specific exponents; the sharper odd-field exponent
is not asserted in characteristic two.
\end{abstract}

\section{Prior work and source lineage}
For each fixed prime $p$, Harvey, van der Hoeven and Lecerf obtained
$O_p(n\log n\,8^{\log^*n})$ bit operations~\cite{HVHL17}.
Harvey and van der Hoeven improved this to the unconditional
$O_p(n\log n\,4^{\log^*n})$ benchmark on deterministic fixed tapes
and supplied the cyclotomic hosts used here~\cite{HVH19Cyclo}.
Fixed-degree basis reduction extends that comparison to every
individually fixed finite field. Their later uniform finite-field
$O(n\log q\log(n\log q))$ theorem assumes a Linnik constant
$L<1+2^{-1162}$~\cite[Theorem 1.2]{HVH22Finite}.
Harvey's October 2026 discussion identifies the fixed-field problem
as a separate issue from the new integer-multiplication claims
\cite[Section 4]{Harvey26Context}; it is context, not review of this work.

These developments established the algebraic and bit-complexity
framework on which this paper builds. The $n\log n$ scale is a
natural next benchmark for the unconditional fixed-field problem:
the earlier uniform theorem reaches that scale with its stated
number-theoretic assumption, while the unconditional cyclotomic
algorithm retains the iterated-logarithmic factor. Our claims fix
the field and address that remaining gap through the inherited hosts,
new transfer accounting and accompanying binary constructions.

The source lineage is explicit. OpenAI's finite-network and tape
interchange method~\cite{OpenAI26} is an antecedent. The selected odd
transfer imports the precise stage-two machine theorem
\cite{Stage2Machine26}, not an end-to-end integer multiplier.
The native supplement retains community chart, PR6030 phase-word
invoice~\cite{CommunityPhase26}, and contributor
attributions~\cite{Community26}; related mixed-recursion and
exact-complex RAM work is credited separately~\cite{Sussman26}.
General fixed-matrix tensor acceleration in Alman's lookup-table
model~\cite{Alman23} is prior art, with a different access model.
The supplement uses classical integer arithmetic
\cite{SS71,BrentZimmermann10} and classical linear/quadratic
Clifford descriptions~\cite{DehaeneDeMoor03}; its specific
community-derived coordinate constructions retain their own credit.
These classical and community developments provide essential
antecedents to the construction.

The stronger interchange construction uses the distinct PR589 BIT record
family at the pinned revision in~\cite{CommunityBIT26}; it is not the
raw-CU phase word. OpenAI's exact-complex DFT construction~\cite{ExactDFT26}
is also a method-level antecedent for local kernel networks and Fourier
layers. Its stated exact-complex arithmetic model is distinct from the
finite-field bit model considered here. The present source comparison
does not establish priority against every possible finite-characteristic
adaptation.

The contribution recorded here is the odd-field host transfer and
its combination with the accompanying binary construction, under the
stated field and machine quantifiers. The companion baseline note
\cite{GeneralBaseline} instead combines the accessible binary route
with the odd proposition below to obtain $O_q(n\log(n+2))$.
These are related results with shared foundations.

The odd proposition does not use the binary multiplication theorems or
the combined all-field theorem. The paired-phase binary proof likewise
does not invoke the combined conclusion. The composition below is
therefore independent of that conclusion.

The odd-field construction uses the mixed-BIT and odd-ring phase
arguments in the native supplement. Its direct quotient selector,
three-call local word and charged native relocations provide the
exponent $319/320$. The characteristic-two construction supplies
exponent $1301/1302$ through Theorem~1 of~\cite{PairedPhase}.
The earlier controlled-CRT construction~\cite{ControlledCRT} remains
a separate binary result; it is not a premise of the all-field
composition in this paper.

\section{Model and scope}
The input consists of the ordinary coefficient lists of two polynomials
of degree less than $n$ over a fixed field $\F_q$, in a fixed polynomial
basis. The output is the full ordinary coefficient list of their
product. Time counts bit operations, including encoding, setup,
movement, decoding, and output, on a deterministic machine with a fixed
finite alphabet and a fixed finite number of sequential tapes. Constants
may depend on the fixed field. No growing-field uniformity, practical
cryptographic speedup, or combined workspace bound is claimed.

The logarithm is base two; changing its base changes only constants.
All statements concern ordinary univariate multiplication. Group
convolution, transformed products, integer multiplication, and
multivariate products are different problems and are not used as output
substitutes.

\section{The odd-characteristic theorem}
\begin{proposition}[Odd-field ordinary product]
\label{prop:odd}
For each fixed odd prime $p$ and positive integer $e$, ordinary
degree-$<n$ polynomial multiplication over $\F_{p^e}$ is deterministic
and takes
\[
  O_{p,e}\!\left(n(\log(n+2))^{319/320}\right)
\]
bit operations on a fixed finite-tape machine.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Construction and dependency closure]
Appendix~\ref{upd:transfer} gives the current complete transfer. Its
native inputs are the equal-field interchange and odd-ring phase
theorems in \path{proofs/stronger-odd-20261102/native-source.tex.txt}.
The relevant labels are \path{thm:mixedbitswap} and
\path{thm:mixedbitinteger}.
The supplement states the phase conclusion separately from the original
integer application. The named external machine input is the stage-two
interchange theorem at the pinned source in~\cite{Stage2Machine26}.
The new source supplies its additional finite-word, integer-flow,
precompilation and delayed-cover arguments. These are written proof
dependencies, not inferences from finite test counts.

For fixed $p$, the unconditional cyclotomic host~\cite{HVH19Cyclo}
gives $N=\Theta(n)$ with $N>2n-1$. Enlarge the conductor only after
fixing the generator degrees. With $L=\lceil\log_2N\rceil$ and
$D=\varphi(\alpha)$, the literal coefficient algebra
\[
 B=\F_p[I,U]/(I^2+1,\Phi_\alpha(U))
\]
has $D$ Gaussian lanes. Its $p^D$-power Frobenius fixes both generators;
thus $f=\mu^{p^D-1}$ is an exact idempotent with $(1-f)\mu=0$ for
every local coefficient $\mu$. Explicit canonical-$U$ unit witnesses
give a three-$C$ word for every required shear, including zero divisors.
The Fourier factorization and arbitrary-dirty exact-width printer
retain every port and dummy until their completed boundaries.

Native aggregate relocations are repriced with the proved lossless
binary-transpose corollary, rather than the older shuffle exponent.
Diagonal table construction and application, both coefficient CRT
components, the normalized inverse and ordinary reconstruction are
separately charged. Small ring products use independent classical
arithmetic~\cite{SS71,BrentZimmermann10}. The resulting prime-field cost is
\[
 O_p\!\left(n(\log(n+2))^\gamma
                    (\log\log(n+4))^K\right),\qquad
 \gamma=1-\frac{3125805}{10^9}=0.996874195.
\]
Since $319/320-\gamma=805/10^9>0$, the fixed secondary power is
absorbed. A fixed basis uses $e^2$ serial prime-field products and
$O_{p,e}(n)$ conversion and recombination work, returning all $2n-1$
coefficients. The proof and source map are in
\path{proofs/stronger-odd-20261102/}; the older transfer sources remain
historical support for version 0.1.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed binary-basis extension reduction]
\label{lem:binary-extension}
If ordinary multiplication over $\F_2$ on degree-$<n$ inputs has bit
cost $T(n)$, then for each fixed $s\ge1$, ordinary multiplication
over $\F_{2^s}$ in a fixed polynomial basis has bit cost
$O_s(T(n)+n)$ on the same fixed-tape model.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an $\F_2$-basis $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_s$ of $\F_{2^s}$ and its
structure constants before $n$ varies.
Write the input coefficient sequences as
$f=\sum_{i=1}^s\omega_i f_i$ and $g=\sum_{j=1}^s\omega_jg_j$,
where $f_i,g_j\in\F_2[X]$ have degree less than $n$. A sequential
scan of the fixed-width coefficient encodings produces these $2s$
binary streams in $O_s(n)$ time. All basis indices and constants
$c_{ijk}\in\F_2$, defined by
$\omega_i\omega_j=\sum_k c_{ijk}\omega_k$, are fixed finite-control
data. Initializing any working copies of their tables has constant
field-dependent cost.

Execute the binary multiplier serially on fresh operand copies for
each of the $s^2$ pairs $(f_i,g_j)$. Each call returns all $2n-1$
ordinary product bits, including zeros. Scan that product into the
$s$ coordinate accumulator streams, adding it to coordinate $k$
exactly when $c_{ijk}=1$. Fixed-width encoding and decoding,
operand copying, accumulator initialization, the $O(s^3n)$ additions,
temporary-stream erasure and head returns together cost $O_s(n)$.
The accumulator streams are scanned coefficient by coefficient to
write all $2n-1$ output coefficients in the prescribed basis and
return the output head to its first symbol.

The binary calls charge their own arithmetic and tape movement. If
a supplier does not already restore its private work tapes, mark
their visited intervals on a fixed number of auxiliary tracks.
Updating the endpoint marks has constant simulation overhead;
clearing and resetting the intervals after a call takes
$O(T(n)+n)$ time, since only that many cells can have been visited.
This normalization does not assume linear-size binary scratch.
The paired-phase supplier~\cite{PairedPhase} already includes its
workspace cleanup in its stated bound. The number of streams,
tracks, tapes and serial calls is fixed once $s$ and the supplier
machine are fixed. The total cost is therefore $O_s(T(n)+n)$ on
one fixed finite-alphabet, fixed finite-tape machine, as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed characteristic-two fields]
\label{cor:paired-phase-extension}
For each fixed $s\ge1$, ordinary degree-$<n$ polynomial multiplication
over $\F_{2^s}$ in a fixed basis takes
$O_s(n(\log(n+2))^{1301/1302})$ deterministic bit operations on a
fixed finite-tape machine, returning all $2n-1$ coefficients.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:binary-extension} to Theorem~1
of~\cite{PairedPhase}. Since $1301/1302>0$, the linear wrapper cost
is absorbed in the displayed bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-fixed-field bound]
\label{thm:all-fields}
For every fixed finite field $\F_q$, ordinary degree-$<n$
multiplication in a fixed polynomial basis takes
\[
 O_q\!\left(n(\log(n+2))^{1301/1302}\right)
\]
deterministic bit operations on a fixed finite-tape machine.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $q=p^e$. If $p=2$, Corollary~\ref{cor:paired-phase-extension}
gives the stated bound using the paired-phase binary theorem and
the fixed-basis reduction.
If $p$ is odd, Proposition~\ref{prop:odd} gives the stronger exponent
$319/320$. Since
$1301/1302-319/320=491/208320>0$, its logarithmic factor is bounded by the one
displayed here for $n\ge2$; finitely many smaller lengths are absorbed
in the field-dependent constant. Both branches use ordinary
coefficient representations and the fixed finite-tape bit-time model.
The field and basis are fixed before $n$ varies. The machine, tape
count and constants may depend on them but are independent of $n$.
Neither branch invokes the combined all-field conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unconditional fixed-field baseline]
\label{thm:baseline}
Every fixed finite field has an unconditional ordinary multiplication
algorithm in $O_q(n\log(n+2))$ bit operations on the stated machine.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
This follows immediately from Theorem~\ref{thm:all-fields}, because
$(\log(n+2))^{1301/1302}=O(\log(n+2))$. The independent Pascal-transform
route for characteristic two is also recorded in
\cite{SimpleBinary}. The odd fields follow from Proposition~\ref{prop:odd}.
\end{proof}
\section{Verification and limitations}
The supplementary material supplies the native finite compiler,
its recurrence, the cover-reserve calculation, and the finite native
records. Saved finite-check reports support their stated finite
identities; the asymptotic claims use the written proofs. The
characteristic-two claim is imported from the paired-phase binary
preprint~\cite{PairedPhase}, whose result retains its own proof scope.
Lemma~\ref{lem:binary-extension} charges its fixed-extension wrapper;
the all-field theorem combines that corollary with the odd proposition.
This composition is a consequence of the two written component
theorems, not a new inference from finite computations.

Complete Lean proofs of the odd proposition and the combined theorem
remain unfinished. The finite evidence and checked supporting components
do not establish the complete asymptotic algorithms.
