\begin{abstract}
For every odd modulus \(P\ge3\), put \(s=\lceil\log_2P\rceil\) and
\(A_P=(\mathbb Z/P\mathbb Z)[i]/(i^2+1)\). A fixed-alphabet,
fixed-tape machine applies the tensor phase kernel \(C^{\otimes k}\)
to \(N=2^k\) records of \(\ell\) lanes in
\(O(N\ell s(k+1)^{1-c_*}(\log_2(k+2))^C)\) bit steps, where
\(c_*=3125805/10^9\) and \(C\) is an effective fixed constant. The
corresponding Walsh transform and inverse over \(\mathbb Z/P\mathbb Z\)
satisfy the same bound. The proof gives the finite word, its mixed
binary-field interchange supplier, and the charged phase compiler.
Its external Stage2 machine theorem is identified as an imported
dependency. Bounded checks address only their stated finite data and
interfaces. No end-to-end Lean verification is claimed for this source.
\end{abstract}
\section{Statements and scope}
Let \(P\ge3\) be odd, \(s=\lceil\log_2 P\rceil\), and
\[
 A_P=(\Z/P\Z)[i]/(i^2+1).
\]
Store its elements as pairs of canonical \(s\)-bit residues; zero divisors
are allowed. On two coordinates put
\[
 X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 C=aI+bX,\qquad a=\frac{1+i}{2},\quad b=\frac{1-i}{2},\qquad
 H_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All divisions here are by the unit 2. Set
\[
 \tau=1-2^{-36},\quad \sigma=1-2^{-32},\quad
 \lambda=1-2^{-37},\quad \gamma=1-2^{-38}.
\]
Thus \(\sigma<\tau<\lambda<\gamma<1\).
The direct construction below uses \(\gamma\). The later explicit
mixed construction proves the stronger exponent
\(\gamma_{\rm mix}=\lambda=1-2^{-37}\) in
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixedphase}, with its finite-certificate scope recorded
in the accompanying verification note.

\begin{theorem}[Phase tensor]\label{thm:phase}
There is one deterministic machine with a fixed finite alphabet and a
fixed finite number of ordinary one-dimensional tapes that applies
\(C^{\otimes k}\) to \(N=2^k\) records, each containing \(L\) independent
\(A_P\)-elements, in
\[
 O(2^kLs(k+1)^\gamma)
\]
bit steps, uniformly in \(k\ge0,L\ge1\), and odd \(P\ge3\).
Inputs and outputs have the same lexicographic address order.
Shape preparation, all data movement, padding, restoration and cleanup
are included. The \(A_P\)-matrix transpose has the same algorithm
because \(C\) is symmetric.
\end{theorem}
\begin{remark}[Coordinate transpose]
The literal \(\Z/P\Z\)-coordinate transpose on the two stored
components is different. Multiplication by \(i\) has matrix
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
whose transpose changes its sign. Thus the coordinate transpose is
\(J C^{\otimes k}J\), where \(J\) conjugates every Gaussian
coefficient. The two conjugations cost \(O(2^kLs)\); this transpose
therefore has the same bound too. Neither convention asserts an
\(\F_2\)-linear transpose of the canonical binary encodings.
\end{remark}
\begin{theorem}[Walsh specializations]\label{thm:walsh}
For each fixed odd \(P\), \(H_0^{\otimes k}\) over \(\Z/P\Z\)
costs \(O(2^k(k+1)^\gamma)\) bit steps. Uniformly for
\(P=2^w+1\), it costs \(O(2^k(w+1)(k+1)^\gamma)\).
These bounds also hold for the matrix transpose and inverse over the
coefficient ring, and allow \(L\) independent lanes at a linear factor \(L\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}[Exact Boolean integer outputs]\label{cor:integer}
The complete integer Walsh spectrum of a Boolean truth table, and the
integer XOR convolution of two \(0/1\) vectors of length \(N=2^k\),
can be computed in \(O(N(k+1)^{1+\gamma})\) bit steps.
\end{corollary}

In particular, \(P=3\) gives the Fourier transform on
\((\Z/2\Z)^k\) with coefficients in the fixed field \(\F_3\),
at \(O(N(k+1)^\gamma)\) bit cost. Its inverse is
\((-1)^k H_0^{\otimes k}\). Three such transforms and \(N\)
single-symbol products give XOR convolution over \(\F_3\) at the
same cost, by the character identity proved below.

The bounds concern bit algorithms with address computations.
They are not bounds for unrestricted linear arithmetic circuits, and
canonical odd-residue encodings do not define an \(\F_2\)-linear map.
The ordinary fixed-field application is supplied by the accompanying
host-transfer appendix; this supplement proves the phase-transform
services used there. Growing cyclic NTTs, faster matrix exponents, and
practical implementation are outside its scope.
Alman's lookup-table method gives \(O(N\log N/\log\log N)\) bit
operations for fixed-field tensors \cite{Alman}; the access models should
be kept explicit when comparing that result with the present fixed-tape
construction. The finite motif and interchange method are attributable to
the September 2026 source \cite{Source}; our required local chain is
reconstructed below.
\section{Streams and equal-field interchange}
We count every tape-head step. A fixed number of tracks, streams and
finite program tables only change constants.
\begin{lemma}[Elementary streams]\label{lem:streams}
An \(R\)-bit record can be copied, reversed or erased in \(O(R)\) time.
A specified fixed-radix address digit can move past arbitrary intervening
fields in \(O(V)\), where \(V\) is total volume. A cyclic rotation of a
later coordinate by an offset determined by preceding coordinates costs
its volume plus explicitly charged offset preparation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An \(R\)-countdown has amortized \(O(R)\) trailing-zero work; keep a
highest-one mark and zero flag, and return its head to the low end.
Initialization costs \(O(\log(R+1))\le O(R)\).
Split by the fixed number of digit values and merge in the new order;
relative order of all other coordinates is preserved. A rotation splits
each complete target fiber into tail and prefix blocks and concatenates
them in that order. Count blocks rather than a bit displacement times
record size. A fixed number of nested counters amortize initialization
against their ensuing complete ranges.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Interchange]\label{lem:swap}
For all positive surrounding dimensions, two equal \(u\)-bit address
fields in
\([B_0]\times[2^u]\times[B_1]\times[2^u]\times[B_2]\)
can be exchanged in \(O(V(u+1)^\tau)\) time on a fixed-tape machine.
The surrounding fields and all attached payload bits are included in \(V\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We give the finite reconstruction in Appendix~\ref{app:swap}.
It uses only a fixed binary scalar motif, rational address projections,
elementary streams and strictly smaller interchanges.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact short-field routing}
\begin{lemma}[Frozen mask router]\label{lem:router}
Suppose \(M\) complete address records have an opaque \(R\)-bit suffix,
\(p=\lceil\log_2(2M)\rceil\), and \(V=MR\).
Let \(T,W\) be disjoint \(u\)-bit address fields, and \(g\) a fixed
polynomial-time function of address controls excluding both.
There is an exact map \(T\gets T\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}g\) restoring
arbitrary dirty \(W\), with cost
\[
 O\bigl(V(u+1)^\tau\log^2(p+2)+Mp^c+V\bigr)
\]
for a fixed \(c\). All controls must precede a dock for the updated
field, or be put there by the explicitly charged interchanges.
The opaque suffix is never a control.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For integers \((a,w)\) and frozen bit \(z\), perform eight enabled
unit updates. Their target axes are
\(a,w,a,w,w,a,w,a\), signs
\(-,-,+,+,+,+,-,-\), and tests on the other axis's parity are
\(0,0,0,0,1,1,1,1\).
Four parity cases give
\[
 (a,w)\longmapsto(a+z(1-2(a\bmod2)),w).
\]
The quotients under division by two are restored.
Put \(K=\max(6,\lceil\log_2(p+2)\rceil^2)\).
Process target positions in \(K\) residue classes, omitting the highest
selected position for a direct route. Every other selected bit has a
complete \(K\)-bit lane inside the actual fields.
Pack each update into one whole-field offset, substituting
\(z=g_j(\text{controls})\).
If all lane guard quotients lie in
\([10,2^{K-1}-11]\), bounded intermediate displacements cannot cross
a lane boundary. The bad fraction is at most
\(\delta=\min(1,80u2^{-K})\).
The ideal XOR preserves guards. The packed word is a bijection agreeing
off the bad set; therefore it preserves that set too.

Dock each updated field after all controls using Lemma~\ref{lem:swap},
rotate there by whole records and undo the docking.
Offset preparation is polynomial in \(p\), with at most \(M\) fibers.
Extract bad records, compute ideal composed with packed inverse on their
keys, stably radix-sort their \(p\)-bit destinations and refill the holes.
This costs
\(O(V+Mp^c+\delta Mp(R+p))\), paying for every copied payload bit.
Since \(u\le p\), \(\delta p=O(p^2 2^{-K})\) is bounded and tends to zero.
The \(K\) classes and direct omitted bits give the stated bound after
enlarging \(c\). For \(u<K\), use at most \(K\) direct controlled-bit
routes: move the bit to the dock, evaluate its mask, exchange its two
record blocks if enabled, and move it back.
No padding to a multiple of \(K\), free scatter, or discarded exception
is used.
\end{proof}

\section{A finite odd-ring motif}
Let \(H=24\), let \(\mathcal T\) be its triples, and join distinct
triples whose intersection has size zero or two. Put
\[
 v=\binom H3=2024,\qquad z=\binom{H-3}3+3(H-3)=1393.
\]
An invocation has two \(v\)-element banks \(x,y\), one side role per
ordered neighboring pair, and \(H+1\) center roles.
For side arrays \(d\) and center arrays \(c\), define
\[
 V_{(S,T),T'}=[T=T'],\quad
 J_{S,(S,T)}=-\frac{|S\cap T|-1}{2},
\]
\[
 G_{j,T}=[j\in T],\quad G_{*,T}=1,\qquad
 R_{S,j}=[j\in S]/2,\quad R_{S,*}=-1/2.
\]
\begin{lemma}[Arbitrary scratch restoration]\label{lem:scalar}
The chronological program
\[
 y\!-=Jd;\ y\!-=Rc;\ d\!+=Vx;\ c\!+=Gx;\
 y\!+=Rc;\ y\!+=Jd;\ c\!-=Gx;\ d\!-=Vx
\]
adds \(x\) to \(y\) and restores every initial scratch value.
Three stages, forward \(X\to Y\), inverse \(Y\to X\), forward
\(X\to Y\), send the data banks to \((-Y,X)\) and fix scratch.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\((RG)_{S,T}=(|S\cap T|-1)/2\).
The side term cancels it for distinct even intersections, it is zero
for distinct odd intersection, and the diagonal is one.
Thus \(RG+JV=I\), over \(\Z[1/2]\) and hence over every \(A_P\).
The target increment is
\(-Jd_0-Rc_0+R(c_0+Gx)+J(d_0+Vx)=x\).
The three bank additions give
\((x,y)\mapsto(x,y+x)\mapsto(-y,y+x)\mapsto(-y,x)\).
\end{proof}
Use these stages on the three triple-coordinate fibers of data banks
indexed by \(\mathcal T^3\), with distinct scratch roles per invocation.
Their counts are
\[
 m=H^3=13824,\quad n_* =v^3,\quad I_*=3v^2,\quad
 W=2n_*+I_*(vz+H+1),\quad L_*=I_*(H+1)H .
\]

\subsection{Common labels and every residual}
Work in \(F=\F_2^H\) with the dot product.
Triple indicators have norm one, and neighboring indicators are
orthogonal. At stage \(j\), let \(A=F^{\otimes(j-1)}\),
\(P_0\) its earlier triple tensor line, \(B=P_0^\perp\),
and \(Q_0\) the later triple tensor line.
Write \(a_0=\dim A\), \(f_0=H^{3-j}\), and suppress tensoring by \(Q_0\).
The incoming/outgoing physical labels are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X:&\ A\otimes\langle t\rangle\to A\otimes F,\\
 Y:&\ B\otimes\langle t\rangle\to
 (B\otimes F)\op(P_0\otimes t^\perp).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed the latter is the next stage's earlier complement, so labels
match between stages. Sources are \(U_a=\langle
t_{a_1}\otimes t_{a_2}\otimes t_{a_3}\rangle\) on \(X_a\),
zero elsewhere. Sinks are full on \(X_a\) and scratch, \(U_a^\perp\)
on \(Y_a\).

The eight common gate labels, in physical order
\(Y\)-side, \(Y\)-center, \(X\)-side, \(X\)-center,
\(Y\)-center, \(Y\)-side, \(X\)-center, \(X\)-side, are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \ell_0&=B\otimes\langle t\rangle,& \ell_1&=B\otimes F,\\
 \ell_2&=(B\otimes F)\op(P_0\otimes\langle t\rangle),&
 \ell_3&=A\otimes F,\\
 \ell_4&=B\otimes F,&
 \ell_5&=(B\otimes F)\op(P_0\otimes t^\perp),\\
 \ell_6&=A\otimes F,& \ell_7&=A\otimes F.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Stage two reverses and inverts the forward schedule with logical banks
exchanged; its physical incidence order remains exactly this order.

\begin{lemma}[Residual geometry and count]\label{lem:rank}
Every edge joins comparable nondegenerate labels, and every nonzero
orthogonal residual has an orthonormal binary basis. The sum of residual
dimensions is
\[
 S_*=Wm-2n_*+2L_*,\qquad S_*/W<m^\sigma.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Appendix~\ref{app:labels} lists every residual. Only center time-3 to 4
edges decrease, losing \(H\) dimensions on each of \(H+1\) center
roles per invocation. Sources have total dimension \(n_*\), sinks
\(Wm-n_*\); common gate labels make signed dimensions telescope.
Absolute dimensions add \(2L_*\).

Nondegeneracy follows from orthogonal direct summands.
Every nonzero residual contains a unit: outside one triple support,
outside the at-most-six-point union of two neighbor triples, or outside
an earlier/later tensor line support \(3^a<H^a\).
Tensoring those units and placing one in a nonzero orthogonal summand
preserves norm one. A nondegenerate nonalternating binary symmetric
space has an orthonormal basis: split unit lines; an alternating plane
\(a,b\), orthogonal to a retained unit \(u\), is absorbed by the three
orthogonal units \(u+a,u+b,u+a+b\).

Exact counts are
\[
 n_*=8291469824,\quad W=34666942577344,\quad L_*=7373836800,
\]
\[
 S_*=479235812353937408,\quad Wm-S_*=1835266048.
\]
They satisfy \((Wm-S_*)2^{32}>10Wm\), \(m<2^{14}\).
Since \(\ln m<14\ln2<10\) and \(e^{-x}>1-x\),
\(S_*/W<m^{1-2^{-32}}\).
\end{proof}

\section{Phase frames and one-child corrections}
For a nondegenerate binary label \(U\), let \(P_U\) be its orthogonal
projection, \(q_U(x)=\wt(P_Ux)\pmod4\), and define algebraically
\[
 C_U=2^{-m}H_0^{\otimes m}\operatorname{diag}(i^{q_U(x)})
                         H_0^{\otimes m}.
\]
No square root is executed; this is an identity over \(\Z[i,1/2]\).
The defining Walsh matrices are not executed by the algorithm. Only
the factored directional edge kernels are run; source frames define
logical inputs rather than a free physical preprocessing operation.
If \(V=U\op E\) and \(\mathcal B\) is an orthonormal basis of \(E\),
orthogonal weights add modulo four, giving
\[
 q_V(x)-q_U(x)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal B}\wt(v)[v\cdot x]\pmod4.
\]
Each \(\wt(v)\) is odd, so \(C_VC_U^{-1}\) is one
\(aI+bX_v\) or its inverse per basis vector; decreasing edges reverse
the sign. Here \(X_v f(x)=f(x+v)\).

Replace each scalar wire by an array. Apply the frame change on every
edge and the scalar gate pointwise. Common frames commute with scalar
gates. Defining the logical input as \(C_{\rm source}^{-1}\) of the
physical input requires no physical preprocessing. Telescoping yields
the sink frame times the inverse source frame on every routed role,
including arbitrary scratch.

For the exceptional \(X_a\to Y_a\) route, the binary generator \(u_a\)
has weight 27. Its endpoint is \(C^{-1}\) on those 27 coordinates and
\(C\) elsewhere. With \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\),
\[
 C=iZC^{-1}Z,\qquad C^{-1}=-iZCZ.
\]
For \(f\) columns, preapply the corresponding \(Z\) mask and postapply
\(i^{27f}\) times that mask. Negate physical \(X\) outputs and undo the
bank exchange. Every original row now receives \(C^{\otimes mf}\).
An inverse directional child uses one forward child, the two \(Z\)
wrappers and scalar \((-i)^f\). No extra child is added to \(S_*\).

For a nonzero direction \(v\), a fixed binary invertible change sends
\(v\) to the first coordinate. Conjugating one \(f\)-axis child implements
the directional tensor on \(f\) columns. Fixed row additions/exchanges
compile this change. A phase mask in original coordinates must be a
dual functional \(\ell\) with \(\ell(v)=1\); keep it in the child frame
or pull it back correctly. A primal vector is not generally its dual
after a nonorthogonal basis change.

\section{Uniform layout and time}
Write \(k=4h+r_0\), \(r_0<4\), and handle leftovers directly.
Before forming any row or suffix parameter, branch on a fixed cutoff
\(h<h_0\) and use direct kernels there. The cutoff is chosen to cover
all later uniform inequalities and is independent of \(s,L\).
Every ceiling of a logarithm used to choose a width or depth is computed
by integer powers, bit lengths and comparisons. Rational exponents are
used in the analysis, not as unit-cost real operations.
For one target \(h\)-bit block choose another as a dirty tile and leave
two as spectators. Constantly many \(h\)-bit interchanges put them in
that order. Let
\[
 j_*=\lceil\log_m\max(2,h)\rceil,\qquad
 r=\lceil\log_2(W^{j_*})\rceil.
\]
Move the first \(r\) bits of the third block into the row position,
using \(O(r)\) digit moves. Since \(W<2^{45}\), \(m>2^{13}\),
\(r<4\log_2h+46\), and eventually \(r\le h/2\).
The exact layout is
\[
 [r_0\text{-prefix}][\text{row }r][\text{target }h]
 [\text{tile }h][\text{spectator }2h-r][L\text{ lanes of }2s\text{ bits}].
\]
Its opaque suffix width and key width satisfy
\[
 R=2^{2h-r}L2s\ge2^{3h/2}L2s,\qquad p=2h+r+O(1)=O(h).
\]
Thus \(R\) dominates every fixed polynomial of \(p\), uniformly in
\(s,L\ge1\). Smaller bounded \(h\) uses direct kernels with constants
independent of \(s,L\).

Pad the row count once to a multiple of \(W^{j_*}\), by less than two.
For this block call, \(V\) now includes that single padding and is
below twice its incoming volume. Recursive children do not pad again;
temporary padding inside an interchange is removed before it returns.
At depth \(j\), split whole rows into \(W\) equal roles; child volume
is exactly \(V/W\) and opaque suffix \(R\) is unchanged.
The entire physical target and tile ranges stay present as keys even
when the selected subwidth is one. Do not crop artificial rows during
a role program; they may be nonzero internally. Each complete program
acts independently on every original row and restores role classes,
so padded zeros return before cropping.

At selected width \(e=m^j\), divide the target interval into \(m\)
slots of \(f=e/m\) bits. Use the last \(f\) tile bits as the dock,
immediately before the opaque suffix. A row addition updates a target
slot by a disjoint source slot; its frozen mask excludes target and tile.
Lemma~\ref{lem:router} gives
\(O(V(f+1)^\tau\log^2(h+2)+V)\), with metadata absorbed by \(R\).
Recompute the mask after each completed row operation.
All basis changes, scalar gates, parity masks, copies and role merges
therefore form an additive fixed node toll.

The recursive procedure \(\operatorname{Pow}_j\) is distinct from this
public four-block routine. Its input retains the complete physical
\(h\)-bit target and tile, a selected interval of width \(m^j\),
the same opaque suffix, and a supplied row pool divisible by \(W^j\).
Its contract applies the selected tensor independently to every supplied
row and fixes every other address field. It introduces no new padding
or block decomposition. The base \(\operatorname{Pow}_0\) is one
selected-bit kernel. An internal call uses the framed role program and
calls only \(\operatorname{Pow}_{j-1}\) on its slot, with the inherited
row pool divided by \(W\). Induction proves its contract, including
arbitrary scratch and full row restoration. Invoking the public
four-block routine recursively would not justify the displayed recurrence.

Canonical ring addition/subtraction, sign, halving and multiplication
by \(i\) cost \(O(s)\) per word. Local \(s+O(1)\)-bit workspace prevents
overflow; reduction after every gate keeps width independent of depth.
Word counter resets cost \(O(\log s)\le O(s)\), and lane counters
amortize. A modulus or local descriptor copied once at a node costs
\(O(s+\operatorname{poly}(h)+\operatorname{poly}(\log(L+1)))\), fitting
its retained volume. No multiplication of growing words appears inside
the phase routine.

Normalize time by the current logical \(V\), writing \(F_j(h)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} and the exact one-child wrappers give
\[
 F_0(h)\le c_0\log^2(h+2),\qquad
 F_j(h)\le (S_*/W)F_{j-1}(h)
       +c_1(m^j+1)^\tau\log^2(h+2).
\]
Because \(S_*/W<m^\sigma<m^\lambda\) and \(\tau<\lambda\),
putting \(a=S_*/W\), expansion gives
\[
 F_j(h)\le C\log^2(h+2)\left(a^j+
 m^{j\lambda}\sum_{t=0}^{j-1}(a/m^\lambda)^t\right)
 =O(m^{j\lambda}\log^2(h+2)).
\]
The denominator \(1-a/m^\lambda\) is a positive fixed constant.
Partition \(h\) into existing consecutive base-\(m\) power groups;
their costs sum to \(O(h^\lambda\log^2(h+2))\).
Whole-block layouts cost \(O(Vh^\tau)\); row moves and padding add
\(O(V\log(h+2))\). Since \(\tau<\lambda<\gamma\), these terms
and the fixed logarithmic powers fit \(O(Vh^\gamma)\). Restore layouts and
process all four blocks. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:phase}; its local
machine lemmas are reconstructed above and in Appendix~\ref{app:swap}.

\subsection{Storage and descriptors}
Use \(W\) fixed role tapes, fixed reusable work/I/O tapes and stacks.
Before a child, append its current inactive siblings to a parking stack,
copy only its \(V/W\) active stream into child I/O, and clear the
current role intervals. Restore siblings by popping backward into prepared
destinations. This costs \(O(V)\) per child of the current node; the
number \(S_*\) is fixed, so it is an additive node constant.
Descendants never cross an ancestor's parked stream.
Separate fixed banks for phase, router and interchange families avoid
copying outer phase data inside a SWAP call.
Only current visited work intervals are erased.

Descriptors list fixed-order fields, selected interval and offset,
original \(h\), word/lane sizes and collapsed spectators. They do not
accumulate every ancestor's subdivisions. Materializing a dimension
\(2^h\) takes \(h+1\) bits, so the conservative shape-header length is
\(O(d)\), where
\[
 d=h+\lceil\log_2(s+1)\rceil+\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil+1.
\]
For every fixed \(c\),
\(d^c\le C_c(2^{3h/2}+s+L)\le C'_cR\), uniformly in
\(s,L\ge1\). Thus even per-record fixed-polynomial work on these
size headers is absorbed by its own opaque record; a child's retained
volume also pays once-per-call setup. The modulus VALUE is a separate
\(s\)-bit operand: it is only copied, compared, added or halved in
\(O(s)\) per coefficient operation, never subjected to an unpriced
polynomial-time preprocessing in \(s\).
Keys have width \(O(h)\), not \(\log V\) when external words are large.
All small-child routing still pays its own metadata with the same \(R\).

\section{Walsh and exact outputs}
With \(D=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)\),
\[
 H_0=(1-i)DCD,\qquad (1-i)^2=-2i.
\]
Apply \(D\) tensors blockwise in the same layout, computing weight modulo
four on the current target key; other blocks stay opaque.
This costs linear volume plus
\(O(V(h^\tau+\log(h+2)))\) layout overhead, within \(O(Vh^\gamma)\).
For fixed \(P\) the global factor \((1-i)^k\) is one fixed-ring action.
Base-ring inputs are embedded with imaginary component zero. Since the
target Walsh matrix has base-ring entries, the final imaginary component
is zero and can be removed in linear volume; no field-basis conversion
or irreducible-polynomial search is needed.

For \(P=2^w+1\), write \(t=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) and
\[
 (1-i)^k=2^t(-i)^t(1-i)^{k\bmod2}.
\]
Reduce \(t\) modulo \(2w\) for the power of two, separately modulo four
for the Gaussian phase. Sharing a reduced exponent is wrong when \(w\)
is odd. Use \(2^w=-1\): a shift \(r<w\) of a canonical
\(x\le2^w\), split at bit \(w\), reduces by low-minus-high, followed
by at most one addition of \(P\). Its range is
\([-2^w,2^w-1]\). This and the remaining signs/phases cost \(O(w)\).
The inverse scalar \(2^{-k}\) is another signed shift.
The two exponent residues are prepared once from the header by ordinary
integer arithmetic. Its fixed-polynomial cost in
\(\log(k+2)+\log(w+2)\) is absorbed by \(2^k(w+1)\).
Symmetry and \( (H_0^{\otimes k})^2=2^k I\) prove the transpose and
inverse assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:walsh}.

For a Boolean table \(f:\F_2^k\to\{0,1\}\), first form
\(u_x=(-1)^{f(x)}\). Its integer spectrum is
\(W_f(t)=\sum_x(-1)^{t\cdot x}u_x\), the unscaled Walsh matrix
applied to \(u\). Choose \(w=k+2\), \(P=4N+1\).
Each integer spectrum entry is in \([-N,N]\), so centered lifting
recovers it uniquely. For XOR convolution
\(h(t)=\sum_x a(x)b(t\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}x)\), the characters
\(\chi_s(x)=(-1)^{s\cdot x}\) satisfy
\(\chi_s(x\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}y)=\chi_s(x)\chi_s(y)\).
Writing \(\Wal=H_0^{\otimes k}\), consequently
\(\Wal h=(\Wal a)(\Wal b)\) pointwise over any of
the odd coefficient rings. Two forward Walsh transforms, point products
and a third Walsh transform therefore suffice after division by \(N\).
Here
\(N^{-1}=-4\pmod P\), and each final count is in \([0,N]\).
Classical integer multiplication \cite{SS} prices the \(w+1\)-bit
point products at
\(O(w\log(w+2)\log\log(w+4))\); one low-high fold reduces each modulo
\(P\). The high part includes the possible bit at position \(2w\):
canonical operands can equal \(2^w\), and their product can equal
\(2^{2w}\). Low-minus-high still lies in \([-2^w,2^w-1]\), so one
addition of \(P\) suffices. This is lower order than
\(N(k+1)^{1+\gamma}\).
Input/output conversion costs \(O(Nw)\), including dense integer words.
Stream each paired word into a separate fixed integer-multiplication
bank while keeping the main array cursors at the current point. This
adds \(O(Nw)\) copying, rather than rescanning a parent array per product.
This proves Corollary~\ref{cor:integer}.


\section{Finite address and native-interface lemmas}
This section records the complete finite-tape and native-word dependencies used by the phase compiler and the selected routing statements.
\begin{lemma}[Whole-array point operations]\label{lem:rampoint}
Fix a finite number \(R\) of roles and a rational matrix
\(Q\in\Q^{R\times R}\). Its pointwise action on \(R\) arrays of
\(A\) arbitrary complex entries has a fixed program in the public RAM
syntax with \(O_R(RA)\) charged work and allocation. Zero rows and
row replacement by a multiple of another row are allowed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Prepare each fixed rational coefficient \(p/q\) from \(0,1\), sums,
differences and inversion of the nonzero integer \(q\). Build a fresh
array. Each output entry reads the old \(R\) input entries at its
address and forms their fixed finite linear combination using prepared
scaling. This costs \(O(R^2A)=O_R(RA)\). No output callback reads
the partially built array, no array update primitive is assumed, and
no multiplication of two input-dependent complex values is used.
For a zero row, the callback returns zero. Replacing a row implements
COPY; a zero row implements CLEAR even when its old value is nonzero.
The fresh array and the copied unchanged rows are included in the cost.
All indices are bounded by the array size, up to fixed constants.
\end{proof}

Fix a label dimension \(m\), exact invertible complex address matrices
\(T_a\) on \(\{0,1\}^m\), and column count \(f\ge0\). Write
\(\mathcal L_f(T)\) for \(T^{\otimes f}\) in slot-major addresses.
This is an identification of coordinates; it does not require an
executed shuffle. Tensor multiplication gives
\(\mathcal L_f(TS)=\mathcal L_f(T)\mathcal L_f(S)\).

\begin{lemma}[Chronological frame lifting]\label{lem:ramframe}
Suppose a finite chronological word consists of exact frame transitions
\(T_bT_a^{-1}\), rational point operations whose nonzero contacts have
identical exact frames, and zero initialization or erasure of owned roles.
Lift only the address frames to \(f\) columns, retaining every scalar
coefficient. At every time and role \(r\), the physical value is
\[
 \mathcal L_f(T_{\mathrm{current}(r)})\,x_r,
\]
where \(x_r\) is the logical scalar-program value. This holds for all
\(f\), arbitrary complex inputs, and independent spectators and lanes.
If the logical endpoint is a matrix \(L\), the physical endpoint on
public role \(r\) is
\[
 \sum_{s\ \mathrm{initially\ public}} L_{rs}
 \mathcal L_f(T_{\mathrm{final}(r)}T_{\mathrm{initial}(s)}^{-1})\,v_s.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct through the word. A transition follows from multiplicativity of
\(\mathcal L_f\). A scalar contact follows by distributing the common
complex-linear frame through its finite linear combination. In particular
an outer coefficient \(q\) stays \(q\), not \(q^f\).
An erased role is zero in every linear frame, including when its old
value was dirty. Initially zero owned roles contribute no independent
input to the endpoint sum. Substitute the logical endpoint and initial
frames to obtain the displayed formula. This is an all-column proof;
it does not infer such a proof from testing \(f=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Frame changes at an overwrite]\label{lem:overwriteframe}
In Lemma~\ref{lem:ramframe}, a point matrix \(Q=(q_{rs})\) may
have incoming frame labels \(a_s\) and outgoing labels \(b_r\).
It suffices that
\[
 q_{rs}\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_{a_s}=T_{b_r}.
\]
There is no condition on the old frame of a destination whose old
value has zero coefficient in that output row. In particular
\(x_r\gets qx_s\), \(r\ne s\), may reset the destination to the
source's frame without a paid transition. A zero output row may
choose any outgoing frame. The point-work charge remains linear
in actual bit volume for the fixed dyadic word over every odd
\(A_P\), or in array volume in the charged complex RAM model.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The physical output row is
\[
 \sum_s q_{rs}\mathcal L_f(T_{a_s})x_s
       =\mathcal L_f(T_{b_r})\sum_s q_{rs}x_s.
\]
Only the nonzero coefficients enter this equality. For a zero row,
the physical output is zero in every frame. Thus the induction and
endpoint formula of Lemma~\ref{lem:ramframe} hold with labels before
and after each point operation. Whole-array row replacement is the
already charged operation of Lemma~\ref{lem:rampoint}; no old
destination value is transformed. Over an odd Gaussian residue
ring the same identity and fixed dyadic point arithmetic hold.
All lanes and spectators stay opaque. This is a sufficient rule;
it does not assert that it captures every additional freedom from
correlations between the current logical values.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Pricing the rank-zero adapters.}
For completeness, put \(B=2^f\). For fixed \(m\ge2\), the complete
address volume \(V\) is at least \(B^m\ge B^2\). Without a bitwise
RAM primitive, construct XOR, weight modulo four, and binary dot-product
tables by successive fresh arrays, using
\[
 \begin{split}
 X_\ell(2a+e,2b+d)&=2X_{\ell-1}(a,b)+(e+d)\bmod2,\\
 A_\ell(2a+e)&=(A_{\ell-1}(a)+e)\bmod4,\\
 D_\ell(2a+e,2b+d)&=(D_{\ell-1}(a,b)+ed)\bmod2.
 \end{split}
\]
Each callback reads only the preceding whole table. The geometric sum
of work and allocation is \(O(B^2)\). A fixed binary affine map on
\(m\) packed slots and a fixed quadratic phase use \(O_m(1)\)
lookups per address. For the phase
\(q(z)=\sum_i a_i z_i+2\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}z_i z_j\), its column sum is
\(\sum_i a_i A_f(x_i)+2\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}D_f(x_i,x_j)\bmod4\).
Thus such adapters cost \(O_m(V)\), including fresh output arrays.
For \(m=1\), only identity/complement addresses and the \(O(B)\)
weight table are needed. A constant phase is retained as \(i^{cf}\).
The local construction assumes a legitimately prepared \(i\); the
full supplied-root interface must still provide it.

These adapters fit the public word language: an additive permutation
and a translation give the affine map. Coordinate phases and the
integer identity \(2uv=u+v-(u\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}v)\) on bits
give each quadratic cross term from parity phases. Inverse phases use
three copies. Their zero recursive rank does not mean zero RAM work.
An exact finite identity \(T_bT_a^{-1}=A C_r B\), with such adapters
\(A,B\), lifts to one rank-\(rf\) child and linear adapter work.
The exact identity, including its absolute phase, must be certified;
a rank histogram alone does not supply it.

\subsection{Reconstructing the finite native and controller interfaces}

exact representatives, dyadic phases and charged implementation matter here.

For \(H=\F_2^9\), put \(L_0=\{(0,z)\}\),
\(F=C^{\otimes9}\), and
\[
 L_U=\{(u,u+v):u\in U,\ v\in U^\perp\}\qquad(U\le H).
\]
This Lagrangian has
\[
 9-\dim(L_U\cap L_V)=\dim U+\dim V-2\dim(U\cap V).
\]
Indeed the intersection has \(u\in U\cap V\) and
\(v\in(U+V)^\perp\). Choose a binary \(G\) taking the first
\(r=\dim U\) coordinates to \(U\). The symplectic matrix
\[
 K_G=\begin{pmatrix}G&0\\G+G^{-T}&G^{-T}\end{pmatrix}
\]
has a monomial Gaussian-dyadic lift: its address factor is \(G\)
and its lower shear is the symmetric matrix \(I+G^{-T}G^{-1}\).
The latter is implemented by a quadratic diagonal phase.
It fixes \(L_0\) and \(FL_0\), and takes \(L_V\) to \(L_{GV}\).
Consequently \(T_U=K_G C_{E_r}K_G^{-1}\) has preimage
\(T_U^{-1}L_0=L_U\). To make the choice depend only on the subspace,
scan its nonzero vectors in increasing binary-integer order, retaining
a vector exactly when it increases the span; extend this basis by the
standard coordinate vectors in order. Use the following consistent overrides:
\[
 T_0=I,\quad T_H=F,\quad T_{\langle q\rangle}=C_q,
 \quad T_{q^\perp}=FC_q^{-1}\quad(q\cdot q=1).
\]
Here \(C_q=((1+i)I+(1-i)X_q)/2\) and \(X_q\) translates
addresses by \(q\). Directly, the last two overrides have preimages
\(L_{\langle q\rangle}\) and \(L_{q^\perp}\), respectively.
In dimension nine the override classes do not conflict. Equal subspaces
use the same representative at every scalar contact.

\begin{lemma}[Exact transition lowering]\label{lem:clifflower}
The transition \(T_VT_U^{-1}\) factors as \(A C_r B\), with
charged monomial Clifford adapters \(A,B\), where
\(r=\dim U+\dim V-2\dim(U\cap V)\). Its exact scalar phase is
included. Thus a nested transition uses one child of rank
\(\lvert\dim V-\dim U\rvert\) at every column count.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a symplectic matrix with upper-right block of rank \(r\), the
image of \(L_0\) has an \(r\)-dimensional address projection.
A binary address change takes it to the first \(r\) coordinates;
its purely vertical kernel is the remaining coordinate space.
Subtract those kernel vectors from the other basis vectors.
Isotropy makes their remaining vertical coefficient matrix symmetric,
so a lower shear removes it. Partial Fourier swaps on the first
\(r\) coordinates then take this Lagrangian to \(L_0\).
The remainder stabilizes \(L_0\) and is monomial: the joint eigenspaces
of the diagonal Paulis are single coordinate lines. Its Clifford
condition makes its varying phase quadratic, with fourth-root values
up to a constant phase. Use \(SCS=((1+i)/2)H_0\) for each Fourier
swap; these are exact unitary Gaussian-dyadic matrices.

The remaining constant is also a fourth root. A unit-modulus
\((a+bi)/2^k\) has \(a^2+b^2=2^{2k}\); when \(k>0\), both
\(a,b\) are even. Descent leaves only \(\pm1,\pm i\).
Thus every adapter belongs to the already charged affine/quadratic
class, including its absolute phase. The upper-right rank of
\(T_VT_U^{-1}\) is the codimension of \(L_U\cap L_V\), by
symplectic invariance. This proves the claimed count. Exact address
identities lift by Lemma~\ref{lem:ramframe}; rational role coefficients
are retained outside their tensor powers.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Direct stage geometry, including its translation]

\[
 E\equiv\sum_k\operatorname{rowwt}_k(G)y_k
 -2\sum_{k<\ell}\Bigl(\sum_iG_{ki}G_{\ell i}\Bigr)y_ky_\ell
 \pmod4.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Explicit finite-word products and coefficient closure]
\label{lem:wordproduct}
Let \(A,B\) be fixed words on binary label spaces of dimensions

per ordered pair of paid letters. Its point coefficients belong to

In particular dyadic coefficients stay dyadic under finite powers,
target completion and paid-block splitting. The resulting word can
return every owned row to zero even when the input words promise only

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the tensor product of the two binary label spaces, with one label
for each coordinate pair. For each live role \(b\) of \(B\), choose
a fixed basis beginning with its supplied target family in \(V_b\), of dimension
\(t_B(b)\). Regard those active \(B\)-coordinates as columns of an
\(A\)-instance, with all inactive \(B\)-coordinates as spectators.
At original column count \(f\), this instance has \(t_B(b)f\)
columns. Introduce roles \((x,b)\) for every role \(x\) of \(A\).
Copy each point letter of \(A\) onto these roles separately for each
\(b\), retaining every scalar coefficient unchanged.

Lift an \(A\) affine or phase letter to these active coordinates and
conjugate by the fixed chosen \(B\)-basis. It is a fixed binary affine
map or fourth-root quadratic phase on the product labels. Quadratic
phases remain quadratic under binary linear substitution modulo four,
and a sum over a fixed number of columns has the same form. Such maps
have the finite phase/permutation descriptions given above; their actual
machine costs are still charged when the product word is executed.

When a paid \(A\)-letter has rank \(r_A\), target role \(x\), and
direction space \(U\), run one complete copy of \(B\), at column count
\(r_Af\), across the existing roles \((x,b)\). Its point letters
couple those roles at common addresses. Supply fresh initially zero
scratch for the owned \(B\)-roles, tagged by this paid-letter position.
Choose a fixed \(A\)-basis beginning with the paid letter's actual
direction family in \(U\) to identify its active columns, and restore
this basis afterwards. A paid \(B\)-letter of
rank \(r_B\) becomes one paid kernel on the independent tensor
directions \(U\otimes V\), of rank \(r_A r_B\). All other lifted
letters are point or monomial letters. The \(B\) certificate makes its
endpoint, on each role \((x,b)\), exactly the required
\(U\otimes V_b\) kernel. If its certificate leaves private scratch
unconstrained, append a point matrix that is identity on every live
row and zero on every private owned row. This explicit \texttt{CLEAR}
does not change a live output or add a paid call. It uses only
coefficients zero and one, and costs one charged whole-array scan.
The public \texttt{GLiveKernelM} contract indeed constrains only the
live outputs; scratch restoration is not inferred from that contract.

Consequently, for every \(b\) the whole program simulates \(A\)
on its active \(B\)-columns, even though a paid step batches all
\(b\)'s through one \(B\)-word. Independence of the \(B\) endpoints
justifies that batching for arbitrary inputs. The \(A\) certificate
therefore gives independent targets on
\(U_a\otimes V_b\), of dimension \(t_A(a)t_B(b)\), and clears
all \(A\)-owned rows after the analogous final point \texttt{CLEAR}
if needed. Fresh \(B\)-scratch was already explicitly cleared.
This proves the endpoint at every \(f\), not just at one column.

Every point letter is a finite role copy or embedding of an original
point letter, with identities on unused rows. Basis conjugations are
monomial address maps; they introduce no new rational denominators.
The point coefficient ring is thus preserved. Completion appends only
paid kernels on extending independent directions, and splitting
partitions such directions; both also preserve this ring. Repeating
the product proves the power statement constructively. This supplies
the product/rank contract recorded in the public \texttt{Power.lean}
with an explicit coefficient-ring argument. The tape construction may
use this product recipe directly; literal equality with the vendor's
product word is not required.

The bounded \texttt{finite\_word\_product\_probe.cpp} checks all
3276 live basis columns of a four-label example at zero, one and two columns,
over moduli 3, 5 and 9. It includes private scratch, two point
coefficients summing to one, and unequal coordinate selections in the
two factors. Raising point coefficients to the column count fails in
1536 cases; omitting scratch clearing fails in 819. All 3276 columns
also pass with a live-only factor endpoint whose dirty private scratch
is erased by the explicit product-boundary \texttt{CLEAR}. A further 768
columns of a six-label rank-two example check nonstandard direction
families and reject an untracked same-span basis change in every case.
Its 13637638 exact assertions attack this construction without expanding
the powered word.
\end{proof}

For the proposed raw unit, Lemma~\ref{lem:rawdyadic} and the explicit
product now give a dyadic strictified word. All its point denominators
remain powers of two; the monomial factors use only signs and \(i\).
It can therefore be executed modulo the same requested odd \(P\) at
every depth. The general odd-denominator mechanism below remains useful
for other rational words, but this instance needs no modulus growth.

\begin{lemma}[Clearing odd denominators without modular inverses]
\label{lem:oddclear}
Let \(W\) be a fixed finite word of rational point matrices,
Gaussian monomial maps and paid dyadic kernels. Suppose its productive
endpoint maps are independent dyadic kernels. Write each point matrix
on all roles as \(A_j/(2^{a_j}q_j)\), with \(A_j\) Gaussian-integral
and \(q_j\) positive and odd, and put \(Q=\prod_j q_j\).
For every odd requested modulus \(P\ge3\), the word can be evaluated
using only odd working moduli dividing \(PQ^2\). Every productive
answer is returned modulo \(P\). This includes \(\gcd(P,Q)>1\).
Apart from the paid kernels and address movement, each point operation
costs \(O_W(s+1)\) bit steps per record when \(P\) has \(s\) bits.
The working modulus has at most \(s+2\lceil\log_2 Q\rceil+O(1)\)
bits, independently of the column count.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Initially multiply all live inputs by \(Q\), leave owned rows zero,
and use modulus \(M=PQ^2\). Keep a product \(R\) of the odd point
denominators not yet consumed; initially \(R=Q\) and always
\(M=PQR\). After a prefix of the word, the true scaled state is
\(QW_{\rm prefix}x\in R\Z[i,1/2]\), and the stored state is
its reduction modulo \(M\). This is an invariant over the dyadic
localization, where reduction modulo every odd integer is defined.

At a point with denominator \(2^a q\), apply its integral numerator
matrix and perform \(a\) modular halvings. The result represents
\(qQW_{\rm next}x\) modulo \(M\). It is componentwise divisible
by \(q\): the true value is in \(q\Z[i,1/2]\), and \(q\mid M\).
Divide the canonical residue components exactly by \(q\), then
replace \(M,R\) by \(M/q,R/q\). The quotient represents
\(QW_{\rm next}x\) modulo \(M/q\), proving the invariant.
Unchanged rows of a point matrix have their numerator multiplied by
\(2^a q\) too. Thus this division is global, including unchanged
and owned rows. Dyadic kernels and Gaussian monomial maps preserve
the invariant without changing the modulus.

At the endpoint \(M=PQ\). Independence and the dyadic endpoint
identity make each productive answer a multiple of \(Q\) in this
ring. Its canonical components are therefore divisible by \(Q\);
exact division returns the requested answer modulo \(P\). There is
no division by a nonunit inside a fixed residue ring. Fixed integral
linear combinations, modular halving and exact division by fixed odd
integers each take linear bit time. The extra modulus width is fixed.
The bounded \texttt{odd\_denominator\_lift\_probe.cpp} attacks this
construction on four Gaussian values with shared denominator/modulus
factors, endpoint cancellation and distinct role-owner moduli;
its 445929 assertions support, rather than replace, this proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Streaming fixed quadratic phases]\label{lem:streamphase}
Fix \(m\ge1\) and
\(q(z)=c+\sum_i a_i z_i+2\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}z_iz_j\pmod4\).
On complete lexicographic address ranges \([2^f]^m\), a fixed-tape
machine applies the columnwise phase \(i^{\sum_{t<f}q(z_t)}\) to
\(V\) Gaussian records of \(s\) bits per component in
\(O_m(V(s+1))\) bit steps. A fixed number of complete spectator ranges
may occur before, between and after the selected fields. The same
statement holds for the \(u\)-phases over \(\mathcal G_P\).
It uses no random table lookup or growing-field permutation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(B=2^f\), and fix the selected fields preceding the last one.
For the last field \(y\), the exponent is
\[
 q_0+a_m\operatorname{wt}(y)+2\langle z,y\rangle\pmod4,
 \qquad z=\bigoplus_{i<m:b_{im}=1}x_i.
\]
Here \(q_0\) includes \(cf\) and every term involving only preceding
fields. Reading their \(f\)-bit counters a fixed number of times
computes \(q_0,z\) in \(O_m(f+1)\) bit steps. No per-record scan
of those counters is used.

Generate the four-valued phase row in increasing binary order of \(y\).
Start with \(S_0=[i^{q_0}]\), and, for bit \(j\) from low to high, set
\[
 S_{j+1}=S_j\mathbin{\Vert}
     \bigl(i^{a_m+2z_j}S_j\bigr).
\]
Two alternating tapes implement this by sequential copies, fixed-symbol
phase changes and rewinds. The sum of their lengths is \(O(B)\).
Clearing the previous row also costs \(O(B)\). The recurrence proves
every phase exactly, including its constant, for arbitrary growing \(f\).
Since \(f+1\le2B\), the prefix work is absorbed by its complete row.

Read this row alongside the data. Repeat each phase for the complete
suffix spectator range, applying it by signs and component exchanges
in \(O(s+1)\) steps per record. Prefix spectators only repeat the row
construction. Binary counters and fixed-field nested countdowns have
linear total update/reset work on complete ranges; copying a counter
of length \(\log C\) before repeating \(C\) records is also absorbed.
Thus all control scans, row copies, rewinds and record operations have
the asserted bound with a fixed alphabet and tape count. For \(f=0\)
the tensor phase is one and the machine copies the data. This diagonal
construction does not implement an affine address permutation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Streaming guard classification]\label{lem:guardstream}
Fix \(K\ge6\) and a residue class \(\rho\pmod K\).
In a lexicographic scan of \([2^f]^3\), the guard-bad predicate of
Lemma~\ref{lem:router} can be maintained in
\(O(2^{3f}(K+\log(f+2)))\) bit steps, with fixed tapes and alphabet.
It does not require a fresh polynomial-time address computation at
every record. Counter initialization and reset work are included.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a complete lane starting at \(j=\rho+aK\), the guard quotient has
\(K-1\) bits. Its forbidden values are below 8 or above
\(2^{K-1}-9\). Equivalently, its upper \(K-4\) bits are all zero
or all one. Store each lane's bad flag on a track aligned with its
binary coordinate counter, and maintain the number of bad lanes in
a separate \(O(\log(f+2))\)-bit counter with a zero flag.

An increment changes a low prefix of \(\ell\) bits. Recheck only
the lanes meeting that prefix, scanning at most \(O(\ell+K)\)
counter cells including returns. Update the bad-lane counter only
when a lane flag changes. Over a full binary cycle, the sum of changed
prefix lengths is \(O(2^f)\), and the number of rechecked lanes is
\(O(2^f)\). Thus the total is
\(O(2^f(K+\log(f+2)))\). Initializing lane markers and flags takes
\(O(f\log(f+2))\), absorbed by the same bound. The two guarded
coordinate counters are nested complete cycles; the remaining coordinate
uses an ordinary amortized counter. Summing proves the claim.
Reading the maintained zero flags costs constant work per record.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform fixed-tape affine adapters]\label{lem:affinetape}
Fix \(m\ge3\) and an affine bijection of \(\F_2^m\). Applied to
each column of complete slot-major ranges \([2^f]^m\), it permutes
arbitrary opaque payload bits on fixed tapes in
\[
 O_m\bigl(V(f+1)^\tau\log^4(f+2)\bigr)
\]
steps, where \(V\) is total bit volume. The bound is uniform in payload
width \(R\ge1\), and allows complete prefix and suffix spectators.
It borrows an existing third coordinate as dirty workspace and restores
it; no address axis is appended. Together with
Lemma~\ref{lem:streamphase}, the bound prices every fixed monomial
adapter used above.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Binary elimination writes the fixed linear map as a fixed number of
coordinate swaps and shears \(Y\gets Y\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}X\).
Translations complement whole coordinate fields and cost linear volume
by reversing their blocks while preserving each block's internal order.
Choose a third field \(Z\) for each shear. Constantly many interchanges
from Lemma~\ref{lem:swap} put these three equal fields first and restore
their positions afterwards. All other selected fields are opaque suffix.
It suffices to handle \(M=2^{3f}\) records of any width \(R\ge1\).

Use the guarded eight-update word of Lemma~\ref{lem:router}, with
\(K=\max(6,\lceil\log_2(3f+2)\rceil^2)\). There are \(K\)
residue classes and at most \(K\) incomplete sites, handled by direct
single-bit shears. Each packed update is a frozen-control rotation of
a full \(2^f\)-value target line. Its offset is computed once per
two-field control fiber in \(\operatorname{poly}(f)\) bit steps.
Across the \(2^{2f}\) fibers this preparation is \(O(V)\), since
every fixed polynomial in \(f\) is bounded by a constant times \(2^f\).
Opaque copying and rotation are linear in their actual bit volume.
The interchange charge over all classes is
\(O(V(f+1)^\tau K)\); direct incomplete sites cost \(O(VK)\).

The good set is preserved by the ideal selected-bit shear. The packed
bijection agrees with it there, so it preserves the complement too.
The exact correction is supported on at most
\(\delta M\) records, \(\delta=O(f2^{-K})\), in each class.
Lemma~\ref{lem:guardstream} classifies the complete stream using
maintained guard flags. Only bad records receive their \(3f\)-bit
destination keys and a polynomial-time computation of the packed inverse
and exact correction. Stable binary radix sorting and reinsertion cost
\[
 O\bigl(V+\delta M\,\operatorname{poly}(f)
          +\delta M f(R+f)\bigr)=O(V).
\]
Indeed \(R\ge1\), and \(f2^{-K}\) absorbs every fixed polynomial
in \(f\); remaining bounded widths use direct shears. Marking and
merging holes are complete sequential scans, not lookups.
Across all classes the additional guard-control work is
\(O(MK(K+\log(f+2)))\), bounded by the displayed conservative
\(\log^4\) allowance. All counters, key copies, rewinds and payload
bits have been charged. Prefix spectators repeat this operation on
complete fibers; suffix spectators are included in \(R\).

The original coordinate shears and swaps compose to the fixed affine
map. Each shear restores its arbitrary \(Z\). For \(f=0\), copy the
single coordinate fiber. The result uses the earlier interchange
exponent \(\tau\); it does not make these adapters linear-time or
give them the stronger complex-RAM exponent.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Unequal-field transposes from equal-field interchanges]
\label{lem:unequalswap}
Two adjacent complete binary address fields of widths \(a,b\ge0\)
can be exchanged on fixed tapes, preserving arbitrary opaque payloads
and complete spectators, in
\[
 O\bigl(V(a+b+1)^\rho\bigr),\qquad
 \rho=1-3\cdot2^{-38}<1,
\]
where \(V\) is total bit volume. No extra address field is appended.
More generally, an equal-field interchange exponent \(0<\tau<1\)
gives the bound \(O(V(nT^{\tau-1}+T+1))\), where \(n=a+b\)
and any integer threshold \(1\le T\le n\) is allowed. For
\(\tau=1-1/q_0\), where \(q_0\ge2\) is a fixed integer, a
balanced threshold gives the stronger exponent
\(\rho_*=q_0/(q_0+1)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Empty fields require no movement. Otherwise set \(n=a+b\) and choose
the least integer \(T\) with \(T^4\ge n^3\). While both active
widths are at least \(T\), exchange equal adjacent end pieces of width
\(u=\min(a,b)\) using Lemma~\ref{lem:swap}. If \(a\ge b\), write
\(A=A_1A_2\), \(|A_2|=b\), and change
\(A_1A_2B\) to \(A_1BA_2\). Freeze the correct suffix \(A_2\)
and continue on \(A_1B\). If \(b>a\), change \(AB_1B_2\) to
\(B_1AB_2\), freeze the correct prefix \(B_1\), and continue.
The active width drops by at least \(u\), so over the entire schedule
\(\sum u\le n\). All frozen pieces are collapsed into one prefix
and one suffix spectator; descriptors need no growing list of pieces.
The total interchange cost is at most
\[
 O\left(V\sum u^\tau\right)
   \le O(VnT^{\tau-1}).
\]

Once the shorter active field has fewer than \(T\) bits, move its
bits individually across the other field. Move a short right field
first-to-last, or a short left field last-to-first. The fixed-digit
split/merge construction of Lemma~\ref{lem:streams} costs \(O(V)\)
per bit and preserves both fields' internal order. This costs \(O(VT)\).
Threshold preparation, Euclidean counters and their updates are
polynomial in \(n+1\), absorbed by the complete \(2^n\)-record
range. Unrelated spectator counters use amortized scans. Thus all
setup, movement and rewinds are included with a fixed tape count.

For \(\tau=1-2^{-36}\), the first term is
\(O(V(n+1)^{1-3\cdot2^{-38}})\); the remainder exponent \(3/4\)
is smaller. The bounded \texttt{mixed\_stream\_transpose\_probe.cpp}
checks 8385 schedules up to 128 coordinate labels against the full
stable block rotation, including the frozen-width charge. The
asymptotic cost is supplied by this argument, not those finite checks.

For the stronger assertion put \(r=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\) and
\[
 T=2^{\lfloor q_0r/(q_0+1)\rfloor},\qquad
 \rho_*={q_0\over q_0+1}.
\]
This is an integer-logarithm, multiplication and division calculation,
not a real-power instruction. It satisfies
\(n^{\rho_*}/4<T\le n^{\rho_*}\). Therefore
\[
 nT^{\tau-1}+T
 \le4^{1/q_0}n^{1-\rho_*/q_0}+n^{\rho_*}
 =O(n^{\rho_*}).
\]
The same physical schedule proves the stronger bound. For
\(q_0=2^{36}\), it improves the earlier conservative transpose
exponent while remaining strictly above \(\tau\).
For a general fixed rational \(\tau=1-a/b\), \(0<a<b\), the
same proof uses \(T=2^{\lfloor br/(a+b)\rfloor}\) and gives
the exponent \(b/(a+b)=1/(2-\tau)\). All threshold calculations
still use only integers.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Commensurate address fields retain the interchange exponent]
\label{lem:commensurateswap}
Suppose adjacent equal-field interchange has the proved fixed-tape
bound \(O(V(q+1)^\tau\log^c(q+2))\), uniformly in opaque payloads
and complete spectators, for fixed \(0<\tau<1\) and \(c\ge0\).
Fix nonnegative integers \(p_1,\ldots,p_h\), not all zero, and a
fixed \(E\). Address fields of widths
\[
 a_i=p_iq+e_i,\qquad q\ge0,\quad 0\le e_i\le E,
\]
may be reordered by any fixed permutation with the same exponent
\(\tau\), with constants depending on the fixed interface.
No address axis is appended. In particular, for fixed positive
\(m,d\), exchanging widths \(f\) and \(\lfloor df/m\rfloor\)
costs \(O(V(f+1)^\tau\log^c(f+2))\), rather than requiring the
general unequal-field exponent \(1/(2-\tau)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split field \(i\) into \(p_i\) consecutive \(q\)-bit pieces
followed by \(e_i\) single-bit pieces. There are at most
\(C=\sum_i(p_i+E)\) pieces, independent of \(q\). The desired
stable field reorder is a permutation of those pieces and requires
at most \(C^2\) adjacent piece exchanges. Two \(q\)-bit pieces
use the supplied equal-field interchange. Every other exchange has
a single-bit side and costs \(O(V)\) by the fixed-digit split/merge
construction of Lemma~\ref{lem:streams}. When \(q=0\), omit empty
pieces; when \(q=1\), all pieces are single bits. Internal order
of each field and every opaque record is preserved.

At every exchange all other pieces form complete prefix/suffix
spectators, and headers remain attached to their opaque records.
The piece list has fixed length, with \(O(\log(q+2))\)-bit
descriptors. Preparing and updating it costs a fixed polynomial in
\(\log(q+2)\), absorbed by the complete address volume, since at
least one \(p_i>0\). Kernel head returns, scans and rewinds are
included in the supplied elementary bounds. This gives a fixed
tape count and alphabet and proves the uniform volume charge.

For the stated rounded interface write \(f=mq+r\), \(0\le r<m\).
Then
\[
 \lfloor df/m\rfloor=dq+\lfloor dr/m\rfloor,
 \qquad 0\le\lfloor dr/m\rfloor<d.
\]
Thus both widths use the same scale \(q\), with bounded offsets;
the preceding argument applies. Division by the fixed \(m\) and
the bounded remainder calculation are ordinary charged header work.
The tiny \texttt{commensurate\_transpose\_probe.cpp} checks 2470
rounded interfaces with \(m\in\{3,5,9,17\}\),
\(0\le d\le m\), \(0\le f\le64\). Symbolic addresses have at
most 128 bits; the additional opaque-record checks use arrays of
at most 256 records. These checks support the stable-order and
call-count construction; the argument supplies the bound for all
widths.
\end{proof}

This elementary specialization can price a parent-tile/child-tile
reblocking when their widths have the displayed fixed rounded ratio.
It does not replace the arbitrary source/rest transposes in
Proposition~\ref{prop:mixedtapecompiler} without a further layout
argument. In particular the scale of an ancestor supply and that
of a deeper descendant need not have a fixed bounded ratio.
No stronger phase or integer exponent is inferred from this local
routing lemma alone.

\begin{lemma}[Field exchanges with logarithmic width errors]
\label{lem:nearcommensurateswap}
Fix positive integers \(p_1,\ldots,p_j\). Suppose field widths
satisfy
\[
 a_i=p_iq+e_i,\qquad |e_i|\le p_iE,\quad q\ge2E\ge2.
\]
If equal-field interchange has cost
\(O(V(u+1)^\tau\log^c(u+2))\), any fixed reorder of these
fields costs
\[
 O\!\left(V\left((q+1)^\tau\log^c(q+2)+E+1\right)\right),
\]
uniformly in opaque payloads and complete spectators. Constants
depend on the fixed \(p_i\). Thus errors of fixed-polylogarithmic
size retain exponent \(\tau\); they need not be bounded constants.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(q'=q-E\). Split field \(i\) into \(p_i\) full
\(q'\)-bit pieces and one residual piece of width
\(p_iE+e_i\in[0,2p_iE]\), omitting empty pieces. There is a
fixed number of pieces. Exchange two full pieces by the supplied
interchange. Every other adjacent exchange has a residual side
and can be done by moving its individual bits, at cost \(O(VE)\),
using Lemma~\ref{lem:streams}. A fixed number of such exchanges
gives the desired stable field reorder. All other fields are
spectators. Integer headers, scans and head returns are included
as in Lemma~\ref{lem:commensurateswap}. Here actual widths are
at least \(q/2\), so their complete ranges pay header preparation.
No padding of a short field to an unrelated large cardinality is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Arbitrary binary coordinate wiring at the equal-field power]
\label{lem:binarycoordinatewiring}
Suppose equal-field interchange, uniformly in complete spectators and
opaque payloads, has bit cost
\(O(V(u+1)^\tau\log^c(u+2))\), where \(0<\tau<1\).
For a contiguous complete field \([2^n]\), any supplied permutation
\(\pi\) of its \(n\) binary coordinate positions can be executed in
\[
 O\bigl(V(n+1)^\tau\log^{c'}(n+2)\bigr)
\]
fixed-tape steps, for a fixed effective \(c'\). Every other address
coordinate and arbitrary attached bit payload is preserved. The
algorithm is uniform in \(\pi,n\) and payload width: the permutation
is a shape descriptor, not advice or an array lookup instruction.
No additional address axis or clean address workspace is supplied.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(n\ge128\), divide its consecutive bit positions into exactly
\(K=64\) blocks of widths \(q\) or \(q+1\),
\(q=\lfloor n/64\rfloor\). The cycle reflections
\(j\mapsto-j\) and \(j\mapsto1-j\) express \(\pi\) as two
involutions, in that chronological order. Partition each involution's
disjoint pairs by the unordered pair of blocks containing their ends,
including pairs within one block. There are at most
\(K(K+1)/2\) families per involution, a fixed number independent
of \(n\) and \(\pi\).

For one family, bring its one or two whole blocks first, keeping the
other blocks in their relative order. Lemma~\ref{lem:nearcommensurateswap},
with \(E=1\) and a fixed number of pieces, prices this actual
movement at the equal-field power. Transport the family's two partner
positions through this relocation. Write \(X\) for the selected
prefix, of width \(h\le2(q+1)\). The rest is still a contiguous
binary field. Carve its first \(h\) bits as \(U\) and the next
\(h\) as \(W\); these are existing arbitrary dirty address bits.
The remaining \(n-3h\) bits, together with the original payload,
are an opaque suffix. For \(n\ge128\),
\[
 3h\le6(q+1)\le9n/64,\qquad n-3h>4n/5.
\]
Carving these fields is a view of the current stream; their later
updates are actual charged permutations.

For a disjoint-source/target CNOT layer on \(X\), let \(F\) read
the source bits into distinct positions of \(U\), and let \(G\)
send those positions to their respective target positions in \(X\).
Then \(FG=0\). The four masks
\[
 U\gets U+FX;\quad X\gets X+GU;\quad
 U\gets U+FX;\quad X\gets X+GU
\]
restore every initial \(U\) and send \(X\) to \(X+GFX\).
This is the dirty-helper identity used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:balancednativeinterface}; it is an identity
of address bits, regardless of payload encoding. Each mask reads only
the other frozen field and uses \(W\) as its disjoint dirty auxiliary.
The three equal-width fields can be reordered to put its controls
before the updated dock with constantly many paid equal interchanges.
The construction of Lemma~\ref{lem:router} is parametric in that
interchange exponent. Apply it here with
\[
 M_0=2^{3h},\quad p=3h+1,\quad
 R_0=2^{n-3h}D,
\]
where \(D\ge1\) includes the original opaque suffix. Its metadata
term \(M_0p^a\) is \(O(M_0R_0)\), for every fixed degree
\(a\), uniformly in \(D\), because \(R_0\ge2^{4n/5}\).
Its guard classification, exact exceptional repair and head returns
remain included. Thus a completed mask costs
\(O(V(h+1)^\tau\log^{c'}(n+2))\); it returns \(W\) and the
opaque suffix. There is no uncharged polynomial-time computation
on each original one-bit payload record.

Three such CNOT layers swap every pair of a family, using twelve
complete masks. The whole family returns \(U,W\) and the opaque
suffix to their incoming values. Undo the physical block relocation
only at that boundary. These dirty fields may contain logical bits
that belong to earlier or later families; arbitrary-input restoration
is why their use is valid. The two involutions therefore implement
exactly \(\pi\), with the original physical output order.

Cycle lists, block partners and transported masks take polynomial
integer setup in \(n\); the mask decoder is one fixed polynomial-time
algorithm reading this descriptor. Its degree is independent of the
supplied permutation. The complete \(2^n\)-range pays the setup.
The fixed number of families reuses the same local tapes, and no
stack of live helper banks is created. Complete prefix spectators
repeat the body on local fibers with relative counters; suffix
spectators are opaque lanes. All their bits, copying, rewinds and
cleanup are included in \(V\). The fixed finite range \(n<128\)
uses direct coordinate swaps. This proves uniformity and the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unequal binary transposes without an exponent loss]
\label{cor:losslessbinarytranspose}

\begin{lemma}[Odd Walsh transforms give physical interchange]
\label{lem:walshinterchange}
Suppose the exact Walsh tensor \(H_0^{\otimes h}\) over
\(\mathbb F_3\) has fixed-tape cost
\(O(2^h\ell F(h))\) on \(\ell\) independent lanes, uniformly
in \(\ell\ge1\), where \(F(h)\ge1\). Then two equal
\(h\)-bit address fields in
\([B_0][2^h][B_1][2^h][B_2]\) can be interchanged in
\(O(VF(h))\) bit steps for arbitrary positive spectators and
arbitrary binary payloads. The construction uses six partial Walsh
calls, linear-volume phase and normalization passes, and a constant
factor encoding expansion. In particular, a stronger independently
proved odd-Walsh exponent would transfer back to physical interchange.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Encode each attached payload bit as one \(\mathbb F_3\) scalar
in two binary cells. Write \(x,y\in\mathbb F_2^h\) for the two
address fields, and let \(D\) multiply a scalar at \((x,y)\)
by \((-1)^{x\cdot y}\), ignoring the complete middle spectator.
Character orthogonality, over the odd coefficient field, gives
\[
 2^{-h}H_y D H_y:
       (x,y)\longmapsto(x,y+x),\qquad
 2^{-h}H_x D H_x:
       (x,y)\longmapsto(x+y,y).
\]
Here the right sides describe the destination address of each input
scalar; the additions are binary address additions, not coefficient
arithmetic. Three layers, first targeting \(y\), then \(x\),
then \(y\), send \((x,y)\) to \((y,x)\). They fix the
middle spectator and every other coordinate on all input columns.

The \(y\)-Walsh call repeats on complete prefix fibers; the
\(x\)-Walsh call regards the entire lower middle-spectator,
\(y\)-field and original suffix as independent lanes. Neither
requires an intervening transpose. For \(D\), keep aligned
\(h\)-bit \(x\) and \(y\) counters. When the \(y\) counter
increments, update its dot-product parity by reading only its changed
low prefix and the aligned \(x\) bits. The sum of changed prefix
lengths over a complete cycle is \(O(2^h)\). Initialization and
return cost \(O(h)\) per complete \(y\)-fiber and are absorbed
by its length. Repeat such cycles over the middle spectator without
an address table. Applying each sign to the current complete payload
and all counter scans costs linear actual bit volume. Also
\(2^{-h}=(-1)^h\) in \(\mathbb F_3\), so normalization is
one further fixed-symbol sign pass.

Intermediate coefficients may equal two, so both encoding cells
are retained through all six calls and all phase passes. At the
completed swap every scalar is the original zero or one at its exact
destination. Only then return to the ordinary binary payload encoding
and clear the temporary tapes. The original arbitrary payload bits
are not assumed to be field addresses or binary-linear residue codes.
The volume expansion is constant and the supplied Walsh bound is
uniform in all lanes. This proves the claimed physical bit cost.
\end{proof}

The preceding reverse transfer is distinct from the ordinary cyclic
chirp-z reduction discussed below. It supplies no automatic improvement
of a Walsh algorithm whose own cost proof uses interchange. In a joint
recursive construction, all six Walsh children and every adapter must
enter the new paid inventory; mutual algebraic reductions alone do
not certify a smaller exponent. The new binary wiring lemma does remove
the general unequal-binary-field exponent loss, while the latest phase
and whole-integer bounds already reach their imported equal-field power.
Their leading exponents are unchanged here.

The guarded \texttt{binary\_coordinate\_route\_probe.cpp} checks all
46080 six-bit coordinate-permutation/input combinations, 22400 varied
block and inverse fixtures, and 50 large-width symbolic partitions.
The helpers are bits of the original address in these tests, rather
than an independent appended register. Another 1088 exact
\(\mathbb F_3\) fixtures check all output positions of the six-call
Walsh swap, including numeric middle gaps and 1024 binary-payload
fixtures. Dirty-helper, partner-transport, involution-order,
normalization and premature-encoding-crop controls reject the stated
shortcuts. The 5626496 assertions use at most 16 address bits per
evaluated fixture or 2048 coefficient symbols. Masks and Walsh calls
are exact tiny supplier oracles; these checks do not execute the fast
tape implementations, time a growing transform or prove its recurrence.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed modular scalings as monotone stream shuffles]
\label{lem:fixedmodularshuffle}
Fix a positive integer \(a\). For every \(m\ge2\) coprime to
\(a\), the address permutation \(x\mapsto ax\bmod m\) of one
complete numeric field \([m]\), and its inverse, cost \(O_a(V)\)
steps on fixed finite-alphabet tapes. Complete prefix and suffix
spectators and arbitrary opaque bit payloads are included in \(V\).
The constant and tape count may depend on the fixed \(a\), but not
on \(m\), the spectators or payload width.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(0\le j<a\), split the source field into the consecutive
intervals
\[
 I_j=[\lceil jm/a\rceil,\lceil(j+1)m/a\rceil).
\]
On \(I_j\), the destination is \(y=ax-jm\); its values form the
increasing progression starting at
\[
 r_j=a\lceil jm/a\rceil-jm\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}
\]
with step \(a\). Since \(\gcd(a,m)=1\), the residues
\(r_j\equiv-jm\pmod a\) are a permutation of the \(a\)
residue classes. Empty intervals are allowed when \(a>m\).

Copy each interval, preserving complete record order, to one of
\(a\) reusable stream tapes. Emit destinations \(y=0,\ldots,m-1\)
in order: read the next complete record from the unique stream with
\(r_j\equiv y\pmod a\). The corresponding progression starts at
its least nonnegative residue, so every read exists, and each source
record is emitted exactly once. A finite-control residue counter and
the fixed permutation of residues select the tape; there is no random
access to a payload record. The inverse deinterleaves the incoming
destination stream by these same residues, then concatenates streams
in increasing \(j\). Thus it implements the inverse permutation,
rather than another forward multiplication.

Interval boundaries and residue order take
\(O_a(\operatorname{poly}(\log(m+1)))\) integer setup. A complete
\(m\)-record fiber pays it. Every record is copied only a fixed
number of times; its entire opaque suffix remains in its original
internal order. Amortized counters delimit records and fibers, and
each used stream is rewound and cleared. Repeat on complete prefix
fibers while the outer cursors remain at their boundaries. Parsing,
local head returns and final rewinds are included. There are a fixed
number of tapes because \(a\) was fixed before the input length.
For a negative fixed multiplier, compose with \(x\mapsto-x\bmod m\),
which fixes zero and reverses the remaining record blocks in linear
volume. A fixed rational unit \(u/v\), with \(m\) coprime to
\(uv\), is multiplication by \(u\) after the inverse shuffle for
\(v\), with signs handled as above. This also costs linear volume.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed rational matchers on complete numeric cubes]
\label{prop:rationalnumericmatcher}
Fix \(d\ge1\), \(S\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\Q)\) and
\(b\in\Q^d\). There is an effective fixed integer \(Q_*\ge1\)
such that, for all \(m\ge2\) coprime to \(Q_*\), the physical
address permutation
\[
 (\Z/m\Z)^d\longrightarrow(\Z/m\Z)^d,
 \qquad x\longmapsto Sx+b,
\]
and its inverse have fixed-tape bit cost
\[
 O_{S,b,d}\!\left(V(\lceil\log_2m\rceil+1)^\tau
             \log^c(\lceil\log_2m\rceil+2)\right)
\]
under the equal-field interchange hypothesis. Its input and output
use ordinary lexicographic order on \([m]^d\); arbitrary opaque
payloads and complete prefix and suffix spectators are preserved.
The matrix and translation are fixed; their sizes are not growing
parameters hidden in the constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Rational elimination, performed once in the finite program
construction, factors \(S\) into a fixed list of coordinate swaps,
unit coordinate scalings and shears
\(x_i\gets x_i+c x_j\), \(i\ne j\). Choose \(Q_*\) to contain
every denominator and every nonzero numerator of a diagonal scaling
in these factors, and all denominators of \(b\). All factors and
their inverses then reduce to their stated invertible maps modulo
every eligible \(m\). This is a finite effective exclusion, not an
assumption that every rational pivot is a unit for every modulus.

Put \(u=\lceil\log_2m\rceil\). To exchange two coordinate fields
in \([P][m][G][m][R]\), privately pad just those two whole numeric
ranges to \([2^u]\). The combined volume increases by less than
four. Use the equal \(u\)-bit interchange, including its arbitrary
complete middle gap, and crop only after that completed transpose.
Its genuine Cartesian rectangle is then again \([m][G][m]\).
The padding is removed before another operation; it is not a product
of pads on all \(d\) axes or on every base digit.

Scalings use Lemma~\ref{lem:fixedmodularshuffle}. For a shear, put
the source coordinate before the target by the paid numeric field
swaps, and prepare \(c x_j\bmod m\) once per complete target
fiber. Classical integer multiplication and extended Euclid, or
schoolbook arithmetic throughout, cost a fixed polynomial in
\(\log(m+1)\); the denominator is a unit by construction. Rotate
that whole \(m\)-record target fiber by the prepared offset and
restore coordinate order. Its complete length pays the preparation,
uniformly even for a one-bit external payload. All other prefix
coordinates are ordinary counters; no full spectator address is
decoded for each record. A translation has a shape-prepared offset
and uses the same complete-fiber rotation without a source control.

There are a fixed number of these stages. Each is a genuine
permutation of the complete cube, not a relabeling of a thin subset.
The scalar stream banks and swap work tapes are reused only after
their completed, cleared boundaries. Reverse the factor word, invert
each scalar shuffle, negate each shear offset and undo the
translation to obtain the inverse with the same costs. The payload
is moved as opaque bits throughout: arithmetic on address indices
does not presume any arithmetic property of its encoding.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Variable-unit weighted atom leaves]
\label{lem:weightedatomleaf}
Let \(m\ge2\) and let \(a\in(\Z/m\Z)^\times\) be read or
computed from frozen prefix metadata in a fixed polynomial number
of \(\log(m+1)\) bit steps per complete target fiber. It must not
depend on either of the two updated \([m]\) coordinates or their
opaque payload. Then
\[
 J_a(x,y)=(ay,a^{-1}x)\pmod m
\]
has a fixed-tape implementation costing
\(O(V(\lceil\log_2m\rceil+1)^\tau
\log^c(\lceil\log_2m\rceil+2))\), uniformly in those
coefficients, complete middle spectators and all opaque payloads.
It uses four ordinary numeric field interchanges, three later-target
shifts and one sign reversal, and restores its frozen prefix and
workspace. This is a single-atom leaf, not an all-width recursive
weighted interchange theorem.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 U(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L(b)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\b&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct multiplication over \(\Z/m\Z\) gives
\[
 J_a=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)U(a)L(-a^{-1})U(a),
 \qquad U(a)=J_1L(a)J_1.
\]
Thus each upper shift is actually two ordinary swaps surrounding a
lower shift. Each lower shift rotates a complete later target fiber
by its frozen-control offset. Compute \(a^{-1}\) by extended
Euclid and its offset by ordinary integer arithmetic, paying for
the supplied prefix decoder as well. The complete \(m\)-record
target fiber absorbs this fixed polynomial preparation. The four
\(J_1\) calls use the two private numeric pads and paid equal-bit
interchange described in
Proposition~\ref{prop:rationalnumericmatcher}; crop each pad only
after its completed ordinary swap. The final sign fixes zero and
reverses the other target record blocks in linear volume.
Every stage preserves the valid numeric square and all frozen
controls. The resulting weighted swap is an involution on all
address inputs, and all data bits are only moved. The argument
allows \(a\) to vary across prefix fibers; it never treats its
multiplication as a fixed-constant scalar shuffle.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Charged rational ports on complete local-ring covers]
\label{cor:qadiccoverport}
Fix a finite collection of rational matrices
\(S\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\Q)\). Choose a fixed odd prime \(q\)

 O\bigl(V(w+1)^\tau\log^c(w+2)\bigr)

fixed-tape steps. The storage order is the finite low-type index
followed by all entries of the complete high matrix, not the
lexicographic order of an unstructured list of invertible matrices.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use canonical low representatives to write uniquely
\[
 g=g_0+qH,\qquad g_0\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\F_q),\quad
 H\in M_d(\Z/q^{w-1}\Z).
\]
Every high matrix is allowed: invertibility over this local ring is
equivalent to invertibility of \(g_0\). If
\(r=(g_0S\bmod q)\) is the canonical low representative of the
destination, the exact high map is
\[
 H\longmapsto HS+b(g_0),\qquad
 b(g_0)=(g_0S-r)/q.
\]
This last expression is rational. Its denominators remain prime to
\(q\): the numerator of each entry before division is divisible
by \(q\) in its reduced \(q\)-integral presentation. Omitting it
would give an incorrect port, even when the linear high map is right.

with \(m=q^{w-1}\). The linear factorization is the same for every
low type, and all translation denominators divide denominators
already excluded. Thus there is no further prime selection depending
on the low type or on \(w\). The set of low types is finite and
independent of \(w\). Move the resulting whole low-type fibers by the finite
permutation \(g_0\mapsto r\), using a fixed number of sequential
scans. All high matrices of a given type stay in one stream, without
one tape or separately issued child program per matrix. The case

\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Physical endpoint of a framed dirty shear]
\label{lem:framedrawshear}
Let \(R=\Z/q^w\Z\), with \(q\) a fixed eligible odd prime.
For an idempotent \(P\) on \(R^n\), write
\[
 \mathsf D_P=
 \begin{pmatrix}I-P&PC_n\\C_nP&I-C_nPC_n\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathsf F=\mathsf D_I,
\]
where \(C_n\) reverses the atom coordinates. These matrices act as
permutations of complete address ranges; array entries are arbitrary
binary payloads, added bitwise over \(\F_2\).
Suppose a finite scalar word sends \((x,y,s)\) to
\((x,y+x,s)\) on all inputs, with any separately owned zero rows
excluded from \(s\). Give its main input frames \(Q,0\), main
output frames \(U,U-Q\), and side input/output frames
\(\sigma_s,U\), where all these are idempotents,
\(Q\le U\), \(\sigma_s\le U\), and every executed gate has
one common frame on the rows it couples. Then its actual framed
word has productive endpoint
\[
 x'=Kx,\qquad y'=Ky+Lx,\qquad
 s'=\mathsf D_{U-\sigma_s}s,
 \quad K=\mathsf D_{U-Q},\quad L=\mathsf D_U.
\]
One subsequent side correction
\(\mathsf D_{I-U+\sigma_s}\) gives \(s'=\mathsf F s\),
with rank \(n-\operatorname{rank}U+\operatorname{rank}\sigma_s\).
The two-main-row endpoint is an involution.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a common-frame scalar gate, conjugation by that address
permutation commutes with the unchanged scalar XOR. Telescoping
the actual frame changes therefore gives output encoding, followed
by the scalar word, followed by inverse input encoding. In
particular the actual side input has not been physically prewarped:
its logical value is \(\mathsf D_{\sigma_s}^{-1}s\).
Since the scalar word restores every arbitrary logical side value,
its physical endpoint is exactly
\(\mathsf D_U\mathsf D_{\sigma_s}^{-1}s\).

For nested commuting idempotents,
\(\mathsf D_A\mathsf D_B=\mathsf D_{A-B}\) when \(B\le A\).
Consequently the main endpoints are
\(\mathsf D_U\mathsf D_Qx=Kx\) and
\(\mathsf D_{U-Q}(y+\mathsf D_Qx)=Ky+Lx\), and the side
endpoint is as stated. The side correction is complementary to
\(U-\sigma_s\), so its product with that endpoint is \(\mathsf F\).
This retains the input-frame factor; discarding it would invalidate
the paid exterior rank. Finally \(K^2=L^2=I\) and \(KL=LK\),
so the two cross terms cancel over \(\F_2\) when the main map is
applied twice. Any owned zero rows are cleared after their completed
word; they are not arbitrary productive side inputs or extra targets.
\end{proof}

in \(E^{O(1)}\) bit steps. For these descriptors, unit-lower changes
of a fixed number of atom rows, and shears from earlier atoms into
later atoms, cost \(O(Vn)\) on \(n\) atom positions. They restore
the frozen prefix and have no separate diagonal-scaling pass.
This is uniform over descendants of a root, not over unrelated
arbitrary \(n,w\) and arbitrary prefix functions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All matrices have fixed dimension and entries of \(O(w)\) bits.
Unit inversion by extended Euclid, arithmetic and reading ancestor
fields have fixed polynomial cost. A fixed branching factor raised
to \(O(\log E)\) is polynomial in \(E\); this bounds even literal
expansion of the ancestor-query tree. Atom-index reindexing and the
\(O(w\log E)\)-bit ancestor data obey the same bound. The exponent
is fixed by the construction, independently of the values.

For each earlier-control tuple, compute the shear offset and rotate
the complete later \(q^w\)-record fiber. Since \(E\le w^{1000}\),
\(q^w\) absorbs every fixed polynomial in \(E\) after a fixed
finite threshold. Reading the offset and doing the rotation are
therefore linear in fiber bit volume. Finitely many smaller roots
use direct procedures with one enlarged constant. A unit-lower
matrix is applied in descending target-row order, so controls are
still old. There are a fixed number of controls per target atom
and \(O(n)\) complete-array passes. Reversing the triangular word
or negating a cross-shear offset gives the inverse at the same cost.

Maintain the relevant address digits by sequential counters; an
arbitrary exterior spectator index or opaque payload is never decoded
as an integer control. Actual copied bits, counter updates, preparation
scans and rewinds are included in \(V\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One whole-rank weighted child on a good class]
\label{lem:weightedgoodchild}
Let \(P\in M_m(R_w)\) be a split rank-\(r\) idempotent,
\(0<r<m\), and define on one column
\[
 D_P=\begin{pmatrix}I-P&PC_m\\C_mP&I-C_mPC_m\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad g_A=Q_A D_PQ_A^{-1},\quad Q_A=\operatorname{diag}(A,I).
\]
Suppose all leading northeast minors of sizes \(1,\ldots,r\)
of \(PC_m\) are units. On \(n=mf\) atoms the weighted residual
for \(P\otimes I_f\) is executed by later-target adapters and
exactly one contiguous \(rf\)-atom call to \(F_{A'}\).
Under Lemma~\ref{lem:rootdecoder} the adapters cost \(O(Vn)\).
Neither symmetry of \(P\) nor a coefficient field is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First take one column and write \(g=g_A\). Clear the northeast
cross block by lower row and lower column additions, without
normalizing pivots to one. The unit minors give
\[
 Lg_{XY}R=EDF^T,\qquad
 E=(e_1,\ldots,e_r),\quad F=(e_m,\ldots,e_{m-r+1}),
\]
with \(L,R\) unit lower and \(D\) unit diagonal. Put
\(T=\operatorname{diag}(L,R^{-1})\), \(\widehat g=TgT^{-1}\).
Since \(g-I\) has split rank \(r\), its unit pivot minor gives
\[
 \widehat g-I=\binom EB(A_0,DF^T),\qquad
 A_0E+DF^TB=-2I_r.
\]
Indeed, take the columns and rows through the unit minor as bases.
The remaining Schur block is zero for a split rank-\(r\) map,
also over this local ring. The second identity follows from
\((\widehat g-I)^2=-2(\widehat g-I)\), cancelling its split
left and right factors, not arbitrary nonunit matrices.

Define
\[
 K=-(B+FD^{-1})E^T+FD^{-1}A_0(I-EE^T),\qquad
 S=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\K&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(KE=-B-FD^{-1}\), \(DF^TK=A_0+E^T\), and
\[
 S\widehat gS^{-1}=
 J_D:=\begin{pmatrix}I-EE^T&EDF^T\\FD^{-1}E^T&I-FF^T\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus the chronological program is \(T,S,J_D,S^{-1},T^{-1}\).
Both inverse signs and the order of adapters matter.

Let \(L_P,R_P,D_P^{\rm piv},K_P\) be the same data for the
unweighted residual; the superscript distinguishes pivot weights
from the residual matrix. Substitution gives
\[
 L_A=AL_PA^{-1},\quad R_A=R_P,\quad K_A=K_PA^{-1},\qquad
 (D_A)_{kk}=A_k(D_P^{\rm piv})_{kk}.
\]
The diagonal weights change coefficients of unit-lower and cross
shears, instead of introducing variable diagonal address multiplication.

In column \(j\), the head atoms are at \(if+j\), whereas physical
tail atoms have within-block index \(f-1-j\), because
\(C_{mf}=C_m\otimes C_f\). The first \(r\) head blocks and last
\(r\) tail blocks are contiguous. Their pairing is exactly
\[
 (k,j)\longleftrightarrow(r-1-k,f-1-j),\qquad
 A'_{kf+j}=A_{kf+j}(D_P^{\rm piv})_{kk}.
\]
One reversed \(rf\)-atom child therefore serves all columns, with
no gather and no separately issued child per column. Every other
factor is unit lower within a bank or a head-to-tail cross shear,
so Lemma~\ref{lem:rootdecoder} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Full prime-power fallback with oriented children]
\label{lem:weightedbadchild}
On every other residue class the same residual has a program with
fewer than \(32m^2\) weighted reversed children, each of width at
most \(f\), and \(O(Vn)\) nonrecursive work. One fixed instruction
schema serves all high lifts and inherited weights in that low class.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply unnormalized Gaussian row elimination to the full invertible
\(N\)-by-\(N\) residual, \(N=2m\), using unit pivots. The remaining
Schur block is invertible, so reduction modulo \(q\) supplies a unit
in its next pivot column. At most \(N\) row swaps and \(N(N-1)\)
row additions yield a unit diagonal \(\operatorname{diag}(d_i)\).
Retain zero-coefficient additions too: a coefficient zero modulo
\(q\) need not vanish modulo \(q^w\). The low unweighted residue
fixes the unit-pivot row choices. Inherited diagonal conjugation
scales Schur rows and columns by units, with row scales permuted
at a row swap, and preserves those choices. All high lifts thus
have the same schema.

A lower addition is a later-target shear. An upper addition uses
an ordinary swap of the two coarse fields, one lower addition and
the inverse swap. Each field has forward head or backward tail
orientation. Across banks its ordinary matrix swap is one physical
reversed child; within one bank it is
\(R_{\rm later}F_AR_{\rm later}\), at most three children.
Reversal of an \(f\)-atom field is one unweighted reversed call
on its first and last \(\lfloor f/2\rfloor\) atoms, fixing the
middle atom. Tail weights are reindexed by \(j\mapsto f-1-j\);
this is metadata reindexing, not an address permutation. An upper
addition therefore uses at most six children.

Two ordinary weighted swaps satisfy
\(J_AJ_1=\operatorname{diag}(A,A^{-1})\). Pair each coordinate
\(i<N\) with the last coordinate. These \(N-1\) pairs realize
all desired diagonal entries except the final factor \(\prod_i d_i\)
on the last coordinate. The residual is a split involution with
\(\operatorname{rank}(g-I)=r\), so its determinant is \((-1)^r\).
Thus the remaining product is the fixed sign
\((-1)^{r+\#\text{row swaps}}\). Negating that last field costs
linear volume per atom. Arbitrary determinants are not admitted by
this fixed-sign argument. Apply the diagonal first, then undo the
recorded row operations in reverse order. The actual child count is
\[
 6N(N-1)+3N+6(N-1)=24m^2+6m-6<32m^2.
\]
All children have width at most \(f\), including half-field
reversals. All remaining work is later-target shifts and signs;
Lemma~\ref{lem:rootdecoder} prices their variable coefficients.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Restoration lemma for the phase compiler}
\begin{lemma}[A short prefix batch with retained suffix lanes]
\label{lem:stripedrest}
Suppose a shape-controlled linear batch kernel on the physical layout
\([B][2^r][\lambda]\), with \(w\)-bit components in each lane,
has bit cost at most a constant times
\[
 2^r\lambda w\bigl(BF(r)+p(r)\bigr).
\]
Its global routing does not inspect numerical values, its point
arithmetic acts independently on each lane, and its opaque-record
primitives are uniform in the lane width. The batch program used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:mixedtapecompiler} has this grammar.

On a vector with layout \([2^r][2^s][\ell]\), the kernel on the
high \(r\) coordinates can then be applied, for any \(0\le b\le s\),
in
\[
 O\!\left(2^{r+s}\ell w
       \left(F(r)+2^{-b}p(r)+b+1\right)\right)
\]
steps, restoring that layout. It uses no new address axis and a fixed
number of additional tape banks. The \(s-b\) unborrowed coordinates
remain suffix lanes, rather than becoming a coefficient of larger
modulus or precision.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split the low field into its high \(b\) bits and low \(s-b\) bits.
Move only the former across the high \(r\)-bit field:
\[
 [2^r][2^b][2^{s-b}][\ell]
       \longrightarrow [2^b][2^r][2^{s-b}\ell].
\]
Move its bits first-to-last on entry and last-to-first on return.
Each bit move is the fixed-digit sequential split/merge of
Lemma~\ref{lem:streams}, costing linear actual volume. The other
coordinates and lane components are opaque. Its two work streams
are cleared before the kernel call and reused on return; no live
scratch bank is appended at each recursion depth. Thus both physical
permutations, head returns and copying cost \(O(2^{r+s}\ell w(b+1))\).

Invoke the batch kernel with
\[
 B=2^b,\qquad \lambda=2^{s-b}\ell.
\]
The total component count is exactly
\(2^r B\lambda=2^{r+s}\ell\). Substituting its cost gives the
stated \(F(r)+2^{-b}p(r)\) term. Algebraically this applies the
same high-coordinate kernel for each value of both low subfields;
undoing the short permutation returns the high kernel tensored with
the identity on all \(s\) low coordinates. Correlations between
the input lane values require no promise.

The tuple implementation is explicit. Copy, add, clear and fixed
dyadic arithmetic scan the \(\lambda\) ordinary \(w\)-bit values
independently. An address permutation moves their whole bit block.
The coordinate counters and frozen controls refer only to the
\(r\) target bits. All descriptor arithmetic is once per call or
amortized on complete ranges; no polynomial calculation of a lane
index is repeated per payload bit. A header's polynomial cost in
\(\log\lambda\) is absorbed by its complete lane scan. The
alphabet and arithmetic modulus are unchanged.

If \(b=0\), the estimate retains the whole \(p(r)\) term. This is
why a free reinterpretation of all low bits as opaque lanes, followed
by a one-vector call, would not close the compiler induction. The
borrowed prefix is needed to amortize small batches and leftovers.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Native Fourier and CRT routing interfaces}
\begin{lemma}[Parametric charged Fourier transfer]\label{lem:paramtransfer}
Let \(0<\beta\le1\), and let \(E:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N\) be
monotone with \(1\le E(k)\le k+1\) and
\(E(k)\le2(k+1)^\beta\). Suppose the charged \texttt{Can} binary-kernel
interface holds at work \(2^kE(k)\), uniformly in dimension and input,
for all four dependency colours in multiplication mode \texttt{false}.
Then there are fixed order and solve programs satisfying the public
\texttt{DFTProgram} definition at every positive length, whose work is
\[
 O\bigl(n(\log n)^\beta(\log\log n)^2\bigr).
\]
The order satisfies \(0<D(n)<8n^3\); the programs receive only the
specified canonical root. The envelope \(E\) need not be a machine
instruction or a computable real-power operation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Here is the explicit substitution in the existing Fourier construction.
Keep its integer length-selection and root-preparation programs.
Write \(b(n)=(\mathrm{barge}(n)).\mathrm{vol}\),
\(\ell(n)=(\mathrm{barge}(n)).l\), \(s(n)=\mathrm{stackN}(n)\), and
\(e(n)=\mathrm{orderE}(n)\). Replace its analysis budgets by
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathrm{leg}_E(n)&=(\mathrm{score}(e(n))+1)b(n)E(\ell(n)),\\
 \mathrm{fair}_E(d,n)&=n+\mathrm{leg}_E(n)+(s(n)+2)^d
       +[\neg\mathrm{Ports}(n)](n+2)^d.
 \end{split}
\]
The only kernel call is \texttt{Bench.gleamT}. Its proof uses \(E\ge1\)
to pay materialization and uses monotonicity to increase the kernel
budget from the sector dimension to \(\ell\). Substitute the assumed
kernel program there. The sector packing, synchronized transforms and
\texttt{WorkingCompiler.effuse} compose this program without inspecting
\(E\); their budgets acquire exactly the factor \(E(\ell)\).
\texttt{Neaps.streamP} therefore has budget
\(\mathrm{leg}_E(n)+(S+2)^d\) for a fixed \(d\).

The prepared chirp and input transforms in
\texttt{ArbitraryLength.risen}, \texttt{fallen}, \texttt{pyramid} and
\texttt{singing} then give the same \texttt{Swift} construction, with
\(n+\mathrm{leg}_E(n)+(S+2)^d\) as its budget. The scalar colour is
used for the prepared chirp; prepared scaling of the input preserves
mode \texttt{false}. The \texttt{Ports} branch obtains every required
root, including \(i\), by charged powers of the supplied root.
The other branch uses \texttt{Dry.dire}, whose work is bounded by a
fixed power of \(n+2\). The two branches are combined by the same
integer test, giving \(\mathrm{fair}_E\). No program evaluates this
budget. In particular, the branch test and all length and address
calculations are independent of a real exponent.

The \texttt{Ration} extraction applied to the universal family of all
positive lengths and complex input vectors yields fixed constants
\(c_o,c_s,d_o,d_s\), a fixed order program and a fixed solve program.
An admissible total work allowance is
\[
 c_o(\mathrm{fair}_E(d_o,n)+1)+1+
 c_s(\mathrm{fair}_E(d_s,n)+1).
\]
The middle unit pays the root primitive. The proof of
\texttt{storehouse} uses only \(E(k)\le k+1\) to give one polynomial
bound for this allowance, integer peaks, root order and allocation.
\texttt{flagshipOne} then supplies every conjunct of
\texttt{DFTProgram}, including exact output and all positive lengths.

Finally \(b(n)=O(n)\), \(\ell(n)\le s(n)=O(\log n)\),
\(e(n)=O(\log\log n)\), and
\(\mathrm{score}(e(n))+1=O((\log\log n)^2)\).
Also \(2^{e(n)}=O((\log n)^2)\), so \texttt{Ports} holds eventually.
For every fixed \(d\), \((s(n)+2)^d=o(n)\). Substituting the
envelope bound in \(\mathrm{leg}_E\) proves the displayed estimate.
The direct branch affects only finitely many lengths; its exact work
and word bounds were already included above. This is a mathematical
parameterization of the cited source proofs, not a claim that a new
Lean module has been checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ordinary polynomial products in the arithmetic model]
\label{cor:ramconvolution}
Suppose the kernel hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:paramtransfer} also
hold in multiplication mode \texttt{true}. Then two polynomials of
degree less than \(n\) with arbitrary complex coefficients have their
ordinary product computed by a fixed charged RAM program in
\(O(n(\log n)^\beta(\log\log n)^2)\) work. All \(2n-1\)
coefficients are returned. The root order is less than
\(8(2n-1)^3\), with the same polynomial word and allocation requirements.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set \(L=2n-1\), and zero-pad both coefficient arrays to length \(L\).
Apply two forward transforms, multiply their values pointwise, apply
the inverse transform and divide by \(L\). No coefficient wraps, since
the product degree is at most \(2n-2=L-1\). The pointwise products
between the \texttt{left} and \texttt{right} arrays are permitted in
mode \texttt{true}; their output colour is \texttt{both}. The inverse
uses the same supplied-root preparation and prepared scalar normalization.
The three transforms and the linear padding, point-product and output
passes have the stated work and one common polynomial cap. This is
precisely the source's \texttt{ConvolutionProgram.fusing} and
\texttt{UniformBounds.flagshipBoth} construction with the substituted
envelope. The generic tensor-engine conclusion below supplies both
multiplication modes. This lemma supplies the charged transform interface required by
the native precision supplier below.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit reciprocal in the local Fourier preparation]
\label{lem:fourierscalarreciprocal}
Let \(p\ge2\), let \(u\) be a primitive complex \(p\)-th root,
and, for \(0\le j<p\), put
\[
 g_j=\prod_{r=1}^j(1-u^r),\qquad
 r_j=\prod_{r=0}^{j-1}(-u^r),\qquad a_j=g_j^{-1}.
\]
The source's sequence \(\mathrm{recoil}(\mathrm{floorH}(u),j)\)
is exactly \(r_j/g_j\) on this entire range. In particular the
\texttt{gap}, \texttt{cog} and reciprocal-sequence preparation in
\texttt{LocalCompiler.publish} can use these explicit products and
quotients, rather than an open-ended scalar reciprocal recurrence.
They obey
\[
 (4/p)^j\le |g_j|\le2^j,\qquad |r_j|=1,\qquad
 |a_j|=|r_j/g_j|=|(g_jr_j)^{-1}|\le(p/4)^j.
\]
The arrays can be prepared in \(O(p)\) exact scalar operations.
This statement prices scalar counts and bounds these particular
denominators; it does not price bit multiplication or certify the
conditioning of the full Fourier word.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All factors \(1-u^r\), \(1\le r<p\), are nonzero. Work in
\(\mathbb C[T]/(T^p)\), with
\(A(T)=\sum_{j<p}a_jT^j\) and
\(B(T)=\sum_{j<p}(r_j/g_j)T^j\).
The relations \(g_j=g_{j-1}(1-u^j)\) and
\(r_j=-u^{j-1}r_{j-1}\) give
\[
 (1-T)A(T)=A(uT),\qquad B(T)=(1-T)B(uT).
\]
Consequently \(A(T)B(T)=A(uT)B(uT)\). The coefficient of
\(T^j\), \(1\le j<p\), is fixed by multiplication by
\(u^j\ne1\), and is therefore zero; the constant coefficient is
one. Thus \(AB=1\) modulo \(T^p\). Uniqueness of the truncated
reciprocal with constant term one proves the claimed equality with
the actual \texttt{recoil} definition. This is the elementary
finite form of the usual reciprocal \(q\)-binomial identity.

For a nonidentity \(p\)-th root \(v\),
\(|1-v|\ge2\sin(\pi/p)\ge4/p\), by the chord bound for sine
on \([0,\pi/2]\); also \(|1-v|\le2\).
Taking products gives the estimates. Running products for \(u^j\),
\(g_j\) and \(r_j\), followed by their nonzero quotients, give
all arrays with \(O(p)\) scalar operations. Their worst reciprocal
log-magnitudes are \(O(p\log(p+1))\). The proof does not assume
that a general prepared scalar has this bound.
\end{proof}

The bounded \texttt{fourier\_scalar\_reciprocal\_probe.cpp} checks
the closed form against the source's actual recurrence and an
independent coefficient convolution. Its 1221 exact assertions cover
51 primitive-root fixtures and 275 nonconstant coefficients over five
prime fields, with root orders at most 12. Controls detect an omitted
\(r_j\), the wrong recurrence sign and confusion with
\((g_jr_j)^{-1}\). These algebraic checks do not test the complex
magnitude estimates or an asymptotic preparation cost.

\begin{lemma}[Separated denominators in the actual shear word]
\label{lem:flashcondition}
For a complex shear coefficient \(f\), the public
\texttt{DisjointIntervals.flash} word uses
\[
 t=1+|f|^2,\qquad s=f-t
\]
as its two \texttt{spark} coefficients. Its reciprocal denominators
obey \(|4t/5|\ge4/5\) and \(|4s/5|\ge3/5\), including
\(f=0\). Every individual diagonal coefficient in its 26 strokes
has modulus at most \(4(1+|f|)^2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Writing \(f=a+ib\) gives
\[
 -\Re(s)=(a-1/2)^2+b^2+3/4\ge3/4.
\]
The first coefficient has \(t\ge1\), proving both denominator
bounds. In the actual word, \texttt{hinge} has diagonal coefficients
\(1,i,1-i,1+i\); \texttt{brick} adds the constants \(-3,1,2\).
A \texttt{spark} with coefficient \(h\) adds only
\((4h/5)^{-1},1,-h/10,-1/6\). The reciprocal terms are bounded
by \(5/3\), and \(|t|,|s|\le(1+|f|)^2\).
These observations prove the stated coefficient bound directly from
the 26-stroke construction. In particular no inverse of \(f\) is
required in \texttt{flash}. Its exact endpoint is the shear
\((d,v)\mapsto(d+fv,v)\), including arbitrary dirty \(d,v\),
as in the source's \texttt{twins\_flash} identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Polynomial guard precision and a charged layout invoice]
\label{prop:outerprecision}
There is an effective fixed polynomial \(G\) with the following
property. Let two nonnegative radix-\(2^b\) digit vectors have length
\(n\ge1\), with \(b\ge1\), and put
\(L=\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil\),
\(U=\lceil\log_2(L+2)\rceil\), and \(W=b+G(L)\).
The actual arbitrary-length Fourier factorization used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:paramtransfer} admits a finite-precision implementation
returning their ordinary integer convolution exactly. It uses
\(O(W)\)-bit real and imaginary components and computes its roots
without an oracle. Its bit work is bounded by
\[
 O\!\left(n\bigl[W(L+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}}+\mathsf M(W)\bigr]U^2\right)
       +\operatorname{poly}(W,L)+T_{\rm move},
\]
where \(\mathsf M(W)=O(W\log^2(W+2))\) is an established
Sch\"onhage--Strassen word-product bound, and \(T_{\rm move}\)
charges the outer gathers, sector layouts and CRT permutations.
These transfers have an explicit classical fixed-tape implementation
with \(T_{\rm move}=O(nWL U^2)\), after enlarging \(G\) to pay
their metadata. The ordinary integer product is obtained by an
additional \(O(n(b+L))\) carry pass. A strict saving for
\(T_{\rm move}\) remains a separate requirement for a sub-\(n\log n\)
integer bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We describe the precision and arithmetic implementation rather than
assuming that an exact-complex RAM operation is a bit operation.
The finitely many direct-branch lengths use their explicit direct
formulas; all subsequent bounds allow that finite exception.
For the working branch write \(m=\mathrm{barge}(n).\mathrm{vol}\),
\(\ell=\mathrm{barge}(n).l\), \(e=\mathrm{orderE}(n)\),
\(R=2^e\), and \(S=\mathrm{score}(e)\).
Replacing \(n\) by \(2n-1\) for ordinary convolution changes
only fixed constants. The actual length-selection bounds give
\[
 m=O(n),\quad \ell=O(L),\quad R=O((L+1)^2),\quad
 e=O(U),\quad S=O(U^2).
\]

First bound the actual local word, including its intermediate strokes.
Put \(H_R=(R+1)^{R+1}\). Lemma~\ref{lem:fourierscalarreciprocal}
bounds \texttt{gap}, its reciprocal, \texttt{roof},
\(\texttt{cog}^{-1}\) and \texttt{recoil} by \(H_R\).
These bounds are needed only at indices below the local root order
\(p\). In \texttt{LocalCompiler.publish}, both reciprocal
arrays have fabric width exactly \(p\), and
\texttt{ToeplitzPreparation.skirtEntry}/\texttt{Spots.bore}
require each actual source index to be below that width.
Their zero-fill branches return zero without querying an out-of-range
entry. Thus the ambient binary Toeplitz size does not introduce an
inversion at \(\mathrm{gap}(u,p)=0\) or require a bound on the
formal reciprocal sequence beyond index \(p-1\).
The scalar arguments of \texttt{flash} in \texttt{eddy}/\texttt{bore}
are Fourier sums of at most \(2R\) selected entries of one of these
arrays; their roots have modulus one. The small \texttt{sand} branch
also uses products of two such entries. Thus every such argument has
modulus at most \((2R+1)H_R^2\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:flashcondition}, the unit roots in \texttt{pulse},
and the integer normalization in \texttt{backPulse} show that every
stroke coefficient has complex component-sum norm at most
\[
 D_R=256(R+1)^2H_R^4.
\]
The remaining constructors \texttt{stack}, \texttt{parallel},
\texttt{atBase}, \texttt{harp} and \texttt{reef} select, translate
or concatenate these strokes; they do not multiply hidden coefficients
across word boundaries. This covers the complete local
\texttt{ballad}, not only its final Fourier matrix.

All local tables have polynomial size in \(R,\ell\): a word has
\(S=O(\log^2 R)\) strokes and at most \(R\) entries per stroke.
Their explicit recursive table generators have fixed branching and
logarithmic depth; their Fourier sums contain at most \(2R\) terms.
Alternatively the actual local reciprocal recurrence can be retained:
its completed coefficients are bounded by \(H_R\), and each partial
sum has at most \(R\) terms of modulus at most \(H_R^2\).
The reciprocal denominators in these preparations are the nonzero
\texttt{gap}/\texttt{cog} products, unit roots, positive integer
normalizers, the constant term \(\mathrm{floorH}(u,0)=1\), and
the separated \texttt{spark} denominators. Their magnitudes are at
least \(H_R^{-1}\) or \(3/5\). All intermediate scalar magnitudes
and reciprocal magnitudes are therefore \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(L)}\),
and there are only \(\operatorname{poly}(L)\) local preparation
operations. This conclusion uses these particular generators, not
a condition estimate for arbitrary scalar RAM programs.

For completeness the required unit roots are computable in polynomial
bit time in their precision and integer order. Use
\(\pi/4=4\arctan(1/5)-\arctan(1/239)\): the double-angle formula
gives \(\tan(4\arctan(1/5))=120/119\), and subtracting
\(\arctan(1/239)\) gives tangent one with angle in \((0,\pi/2)\).
The alternating series give \(q\) accurate bits with \(O(q)\) terms.
Divide the desired angle \(2\pi a/d\), with \(0\le a<d\), by
32, evaluate its exponential by the Taylor series on an argument of
modulus below \(1/4\), and square five times. Taylor truncation and
rounded arithmetic require only polynomial work in
\(q+\log(d+2)\); a further \(O(\log(q+2))\) guard pays the sum
of term errors. All relevant orders have \(O(L)\) bits. Each local
root is evaluated at the actual exponent specified by \texttt{spray};
it is not replaced by a different primitive root. Conjugation changes
the sign of one stored component. The exact Gaussian unit is \((0,1)\).
Because local denominators have the lower bounds just proved, rounded
addition, product and quotient error recurrences give any requested
\(q\)-bit absolute scalar accuracy using
\(q+\operatorname{poly}(L)\) internal bits. More explicitly, let
\(T=\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bound the local preparation DAG size,
and choose \(M=2^{\operatorname{poly}(L)}\ge2\) bounding every
exact component-sum norm and inverse-denominator norm. While the error
is at most \(1/(2M)\), addition, multiplication and division obey
\(E_{j+1}\le32M^4E_j+\varepsilon\), including the rounding error.
For division use \(|b'|\ge1/(2M)\) and expand
\(a'/b'-a/b\); this gives the same bound.
Choosing
\(\varepsilon\le2^{-q-3}M^{-1}(32M^4)^{-T-1}\)
maintains that hypothesis by induction and delivers the requested
accuracy. Thus the extra precision is explicitly
\(O(T\log M+\log M)\), a polynomial in \(L\).
Schoolbook arithmetic already gives
\(\operatorname{poly}(q,L)\) local setup time.

A synchronized stroke has a scaled gather followed by disjoint
copies of \(C^{\otimes a}\), with \(a\le\ell\).
Use \(\|x\|_{\infty,1}=\max_j(|\Re x_j|+|\Im x_j|)\).
A gather has norm at most one even when indices repeat; a tensor
diagonal has norm at most \(D_R^\ell\); the phase part has norm
at most \(2^\ell\). Every exact stroke therefore has norm at most
\((2D_R)^\ell\). Set \(\Lambda=4(2D_R+4)^{\ell+1}\).
If its global coefficients are supplied to absolute accuracy
\(\delta=2^{-q}\), multiplication is rounded on this grid and
Corollary~\ref{cor:dyadicphase} rounds each completed phase call,
its local error is at most
\(\delta\Lambda(\|y\|_{\infty,1}+1)\), after a fixed extra guard.
Writing \(Z_t\) for an exact prefix bound and \(E_t\) for its error,
we may use
\[
 Z_{t+1}\le\Lambda Z_t,\qquad
 E_{t+1}\le\Lambda E_t+\delta\Lambda(Z_t+1),\qquad
 E_S\le\delta S\Lambda^{S+1}(Z_0+1).
\]
Both \(\log_2\Lambda\) and \(S\log_2\Lambda\) are bounded by fixed
polynomials in \(L\). This bounds every intermediate dirty word,
rather than inferring stability from its final Fourier norm.
The astronomical finite-word implementation of the phase call adds
no numerical prefix loss here: it runs exactly modulo an odd modulus
and rounds only the recovered target kernel.

Global coefficient lists are charged too. For each stroke build its
tensor diagonal in the current native output order. Process axes from
most significant to least significant in that actual mixed-radix order,
making the next digit the inner loop. Starting with one prefix
coefficient, expand each prefix by scanning the next local table,
printing products on an alternating output tape and rewinding only
that table and the parked prefix word. Every local radix is at least
two, so all prefix-list lengths sum to less than twice the final
length. The whole list therefore uses \(O(m)\) word products and
linear scans, including rewinds. Compute at an additional
\(O(\ell\log(D_R+2)+\log(\ell+2))\) guard, then round the list
to its requested accuracy. It is zipped with the newly gathered
data in that same order. There is no \(\ell\)-fold word-product
loop per final record or assumed transport of an old scalar list.

The outer chirps can be generated sequentially by their first and
second multiplicative differences, using \(O(n)\) word products.
Their root errors accumulate by at most a polynomial in \(n\),
which costs \(O(L)\) extra guard bits. The three working transforms,
chirp products and normalization for one DFT therefore have error
\(2^{-q+\operatorname{poly}(L)}(1+\|x\|_{\infty,1})\).
Two forward DFTs, a pointwise product and an inverse DFT give
ordinary digit-convolution error at most
\(2^{-q+2b+\operatorname{poly}(L)}\).
Choose \(q=2b+G(L)\), taking \(G(L)\ge4L+10\) and enlarging it to
make this less than \(1/4\) and cover setup and metadata guards.
Intermediate data magnitudes are
\(2^{2b+\operatorname{poly}(L)}\), so the complete signed fixed-point
records have \(O(b+G(L))\) bits. Rounding the real outputs returns
the exact integer coefficients. The outer constants \(1/3,1/5\)
are approximated outside the phase subroutine; they are not inverted
modulo its odd working modulus.

Each working stroke now costs \(O(m\mathsf M(W))\) for coefficient
lists and data scalar products, and
\(O(mW(L+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}})\) for its phase calls.
There are \(O(U^2)\) synchronized strokes and a fixed number of
transforms. Local setup is \(\operatorname{poly}(W,L)\), proving
the arithmetic ledger. The remaining outer transfers have total
record volume \(O(n)\) per stroke by the actual sector partition
and packing identities. Their indices depend only on shape.
For a classical fixed-tape implementation, print requests with source
and output indices, sort by source, merge with the input while copying
repeated requests, then sort by output index. Keys have \(O(L)\)
bits; ordinary tape mergesort takes \(O(nWL)\) bit steps per transfer
when \(W\) pays metadata. Pack/unpack and CRT permutations use the
same construction. A fixed number of such bulk transfers per stroke
gives the stated \(T_{\rm move}\) bound with a fixed tape count.
The output and carry passes were priced above. All constants and
polynomial degrees are independent of \(n,b\).
\end{proof}

The proposition completes a precision and coefficient-arithmetic
interface for this specific Fourier construction. For block digits
\(b\) chosen as a sufficiently large fixed power of \(L\), its
arithmetic ledger is \(nb\,(L+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}}\) times a
fixed power of \(\log(L+2)\), and local setup is polynomial in
\(L\). The classical layout invoice still has exponent one in
\(L\). Replacing it requires a proved native-layout supplier; a
RAM gather or a conditional community routing interface does not do so.
For a full integer input length \(B_{\rm tot}\), one can choose
\(b=\lceil\log_2(B_{\rm tot}+2)\rceil^d\) with a fixed integer
\(d>\deg G\), then \(n=\lceil B_{\rm tot}/b\rceil\).
Thus the precision choice does not require an input-dependent
polynomial degree or a circular choice of block size.

\begin{lemma}[Working lengths for large native radices]
\label{lem:largeradixlength}
For every sufficiently large \(n\), put
\(L=\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil\) and \(K=L+8\).
One can deterministically construct distinct odd primes
\(K^3\le p_i\le K^4\) and a power of two \(q<2K^4\) such that
\[
 V=\prod_i p_i,\qquad 2n\le M=qV<4n.
\]
Setup costs a fixed polynomial in \(L\). If
\(e=\lceil\log_2(2K^4)\rceil\), the same Fourier construction
has \(R=2^e<4K^4\), \(S=O((\log L)^2)\) and supplied-root order
\(D=(2n)M2^e<8n^3\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).
Even-padding the odd axes has only
\[
 q\prod_i(p_i+1)=M\bigl(1+O(L^{-2})\bigr)
\]
volume. With \(L_o=\lceil\log_2(2\prod_i(p_i+1))\rceil\),
each even native radix satisfies \(p_i+1\ge L_o^3\).
The coarse CRT permutation separating the single dyadic axis from
the odd part, and its inverse, cost \(O(MW\log(q+2))\) on
\(W\)-bit opaque records, plus polynomial setup in \(L\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The effective elementary Chebyshev lower bound used in the source's
\texttt{PrimeSelection.harvest} gives
\(\pi(x)\ge c x/\log x\) eventually for an effective absolute
\(c>0\). Subtracting the trivial upper bound \(\pi(K^3)\le K^3\)
shows that the interval \([K^3,K^4]\) contains at least \(K^3\)
primes for all sufficiently large \(K\). Their product exceeds
\(2n\). Enumerate them in increasing order, multiplying into \(V\)
until the next prime would first make the product exceed \(2n\).
All primes are odd, so \(V<2n\); the rejected prime is at most
\(K^4\), giving \(V>2n/K^4\). Take the least power of two with
\(qV\ge2n\). Then \(q<2K^4\) and \(qV<4n\).
Trial division through \(K^4\), multiplication of \(O(L)\)-bit
integers and the dyadic fill all take polynomial bit time in \(L\).
The finitely many small lengths can use a fixed direct branch.

There are at most \(L+1\) selected primes. Hence
\[
 1\le\prod_i(1+1/p_i)
 \le\exp((L+1)/K^3)=1+O(L^{-2}).
\]
In particular \(q\prod_i(p_i+1)<8n\) eventually, so
\(L_o\le L+4<K\). This proves the native lower bound without
silently equating two rounded address widths. All axes, including
\(q\), are below \(2^e\), and \(2^e\le n\) eventually.
The root-order estimate follows as before. The proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:outerprecision} remains valid with
\(R=O(L^4)\): its guard polynomial changes, while
\(\log R=O(\log L)\) and the number of synchronized strokes
have the same asymptotic bound.

For the coarse CRT use exactly
\[
 F(u,r)=qu+Vr\pmod{qV},\qquad 0\le u<V,\quad0\le r<q.
\]
In an input \([V][q]\) rectangle, first transpose to \([q][V]\).
Since \(q\) is dyadic of logarithmic width \(O(\log L)\), pad
\(V\) to its next power of two, move these \(\log_2q\) binary
digits across the other field using Lemma~\ref{lem:streams}, and
crop the private pad after the completed transpose. The volume grows
by less than two, giving \(O(MW\log(q+2))\) time.
Permute the \(q\) row blocks so row \(r\) reads old row
\(Vr\bmod q\), then rotate that row by
\(\lfloor Vr/q\rfloor\bmod V\). This reads exactly
\(x_{F(u,r)}\). Sorting whole row blocks costs
\(O(MW\log(q+2))\); the rotations are linear-volume streams.
Offsets and the inverse modulo \(q\) are polynomial setup.
Reverse these operations for the inverse map.
Finally
\(F(u,r)F(v,s)\equiv q^2uv+V^2rs\pmod{qV}\), so the
precise factor roots are \(\zeta_M^{q^2}\) and
\(\zeta_M^{V^2}\). Thus the separated dyadic Fourier axis and
odd Fourier tensor have the required exact root convention.
Padding and unpadding the odd axes remain charged transfers.
\end{proof}

The same guarded root-recovery probe checks this coarse CRT map and
its inverse on 64 shapes, with 7680 address positions and 1855040
Fourier phase pairs, all at volume at most 512. Using a residue in
place of the quotient row shift fails at 6061 positions; using
unsquared cofactor roots fails at 1669568 phase pairs. These are
finite identity checks, separate from the tape-cost proof.

This supplies the size hypotheses in the cited
\texttt{CONTROLLED\_TENSOR\_CRT\_ROOT\_20261030.txt} route,
where each even native radix must be at least the cube of its total
address width. The original working axes, bounded by \(O(L^2)\),
do not meet that hypothesis. The single small dyadic axis can be
handled separately. A full reuse still requires the actual layout
and matching contracts, with per-child costs depending on active
columns; the size lemma alone does not establish them.

\begin{lemma}[Opaque records do not inherit the routing field]
\label{lem:opaquerouting}
Suppose a fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape linear routing construction over a
fixed finite field has completed target equal to a record permutation,
uniformly for arbitrary binary payload width. It also permutes signed
integer, Gaussian fixed-point and residue records of that width, with
the same bit bound up to a constant factor. This statement transfers
the permutation endpoint, not another field's arithmetic kernel.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Encode each payload bit as field zero or one, using the fixed binary
encoding of a field element, and treat these symbols as vector lanes.
In characteristic two, grouping bits into extension-field coordinates
is another fixed encoding. A completed permutation matrix moves each
lane unchanged; hence it moves the entire encoding exactly, including
signs, carries already stored in the record, and any last-lane zero pad.
Decode and crop in a linear scan. No integer or Gaussian arithmetic
is simulated by field addition. Internal field-valued mixtures are
retained until the complete permutation has returned. In particular,
one must not project an extension-field coordinate at an intermediate
stage. Encode once for the complete call, rather than enlarging the
representation at every recursive child. The supplier's uniform
payload bound and these two linear scans prove the cost claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The actual square Fourier strokes have bijective lookups]
\label{lem:invertiblestrokes}
Fix a positive local Fourier length and the exact primitive roots in
\texttt{FourierWords.creates\_ballad}. Every lookup in that local
word is a permutation, and every one of its diagonal coefficients
is nonzero. The same holds after adjoining one fixed dummy coordinate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The source's \texttt{Stroke.Sound} makes every stroke a closed square
operator on the specified local width. Write it as
\(A_s=C_{\mu_s}D_sG_s\), where \(G_sx(j)=x(\iota_s(j))\),
\(D_s\) is diagonal, and \(C_{\mu_s}\) applies \(C\) on the
two-cycles of its partner involution and identity on fixed points.
Since \(\det C=i\), the last factor is invertible. The product
of these square operators is the exact Fourier matrix by
\texttt{creates\_ballad}; its Vandermonde determinant is nonzero.
Thus every factor, and then \(D_s,G_s\), is invertible. A lookup
of a finite set into itself is invertible exactly when its index map
is bijective: a missing source gives a zero column. A zero diagonal
entry likewise makes \(D_s\) singular. Extending each factor by
identity on a dummy coordinate preserves all these statements.
This conclusion uses the actual square invertible endpoint; the
source's general stroke type still permits noninjective gathers.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One native phase kernel suffices for every matching]
\label{lem:nativeselector}
On even native axes \(n_i\), let
\[
 K_{\boldsymbol n}=\bigotimes_i(I_{n_i/2}\otimes C)
\]
in their original interleaved label/endpoint order. For arbitrary
public partner involutions on those same axes, their matching tensor
is implemented by two tensors of local record permutations, five
calls to \(K_{\boldsymbol n}\), and five diagonal scans with
coefficients in \(\{1,-1,i,-i\}\). There is no endpoint gather
and no enlargement of any native axis. The identity holds over
\(A_P\) for every odd \(P\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Complete each involution to a partition into pairs: retain its genuine
two-cycles and pair its fixed points arbitrarily. The number of fixed
points is even. Enumerate the resulting pairs, giving a local
permutation to adjacent canonical pairs; mark genuine pairs active
and added pairs inactive. On a marked pair the desired operation
is respectively \(C\) or \(I\).

Put \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) and
\(S=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)\). Direct multiplication gives
\[
 C Z C(-iZ)=I,\qquad C S C S C(-iS)=I.
\]
Thus one \(C\) followed by two copies of the first identity gives
a five-\(C\) word for \(C\); the two displayed identities
concatenated give a five-\(C\) word for \(I\). In chronological
order, the diagonals \emph{after} the five calls are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 \text{active}&I&Z&-iZ&Z&-iZ\\
 \text{inactive}&Z&-iZ&S&S&-iS.
 \end{array}
\]
Tensor these words across all native axes and all canonical pairs.
Each of the five kernel slots is exactly \(K_{\boldsymbol n}\);
each diagonal is a product of the displayed local factors, depending
only on the current public pair label and endpoint. Sequential prefix
arrays generate the product list in \(O(\prod_i n_i)\) work, and
signs/component exchanges apply it in linear bit volume. Undo the
initial local permutations. All scalar identities use only dyadic
Gaussian coefficients, so they specialize to every odd modulus.
An added dummy coordinate may be mixed inside an inactive identity
word; retain it until the completed word has returned it exactly.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Native execution of the whole tensor of Fourier strokes]
\label{lem:nativestroketensor}
Let the local words on axes of sizes \(r_i\) have strokes
\(A_{i,t}=C_{i,t}D_{i,t}G_{i,t}\), where \(G_{i,t}\) is a
permutation lookup, \(D_{i,t}\) is diagonal, and \(C_{i,t}\) is
the genuine partner matching. Adjoin a fixed dummy coordinate when
needed to make each axis size \(n_i\) even, and pad shorter words
with identity strokes. On the same native product of size
\(M=\prod_i n_i\), the whole tensor of local words is implemented
by one tensor of local permutations, one product diagonal, and one
tensor matching per synchronized slot. No sector packing, endpoint
gather, or stride permutation is required between these slots.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Extend every local factor by identity on its dummy coordinate.
For a lookup \((G_{i,t}x)(j)=x(\iota_{i,t}(j))\), the physical
destination of source \(j\) is \(\iota_{i,t}^{-1}(j)\).
Thus the routing call returns exactly
\[
 z_{\boldsymbol j}=x_{(\iota_{i,t}(j_i))_i}
\]
in the ordinary current native output order. Build the diagonal
\(d_t(\boldsymbol j)=\prod_i d_{i,t}(j_i)\) in that order,
rather than transporting an old coefficient list with the input.
The next matching tensor is supplied directly by
Lemma~\ref{lem:nativeselector}, including identity on its actual
fixed points. Consequently a completed slot is exactly
\(\bigotimes_i A_{i,t}\).

For any number of slots, the mixed-product identity gives
\[
 \left(\bigotimes_i A_{i,S-1}\right)\cdots
 \left(\bigotimes_i A_{i,0}\right)
   =\bigotimes_i(A_{i,S-1}\cdots A_{i,0}).
\]
Each extended local factor preserves its dummy coordinate, hence
each completed slot preserves every original dummy pattern.
Keep the dummy data through the internal five-call matching word;
its temporary pair canonicalizations need not preserve that pattern.
Crop only at a completed boundary.

The pure local source definitions are precisely
\texttt{Stroke.applyM}, \texttt{applyC} and \texttt{send} in
\texttt{LayerWords.lean}. Its \texttt{Sound} fields close all
indices below \(r_i\) and fix the outside. Theorems
\texttt{sound\_ballad} and \texttt{creates\_ballad} identify the
local endpoint as the required Fourier map; the lookup certificate
is Lemma~\ref{lem:invertiblestrokes}. These pure algebraic
definitions do not require the RAM sector implementation of the
kernel. The direct native construction here implements their tensor
without calling that RAM program. This is why the classical sector
sort charge in Proposition~\ref{prop:outerprecision} is not an
unmatched step in the later native integer algorithm.

For the physical application retain the hypotheses
\(L^3\le n_i\le R=\operatorname{poly}(L)\) and
\(W\ge P_1(R,L)\) of the native supplier. All local lookup,
partner and inverse tables are the polynomial-size integer-generated
tables already charged in Lemma~\ref{lem:nativeparameter}.
A prefix expansion constructs each product diagonal with
\(O(M)\) word products, then a sequential zip applies it.
The routing, matching, scalar and recovery charges are therefore
exactly those already stated per slot. The local word has
\(S=O((\log L)^2)\) slots. Its synchronization adds only that
secondary-log factor, not a sort of the full native array.
\end{proof}

For routing, an even shorter common word selects \(I\) or the
adjacent exchange \(X\): two calls to \(C\), with post-call
diagonals \((Z,-iZ)\) for \(I\) and \((I,I)\) for \(X\).
This follows from the first displayed identity and \(C^2=X\).
The choice may depend on a frozen control and the pair label.
Target-only kernel calls preserve that control at every completed
outer boundary. Consequently the controlled-routing construction
can be investigated over the fixed ring \(A_3\), using
these two-call switches and binary payload encodings; its algebra
does not require a characteristic-two kernel. The following lemmas
supply the full shuffle, ragged-network and moving-control-gauge invoice.

\begin{lemma}[Native endpoints can borrow a separate binary data field]
\label{lem:nativedock}
Let \(M=\prod_i n_i\), \(L=\lceil\log_2(2M)\rceil\), with
even \(L^3\le n_i\le R\) and \(\log R\) bounded by a fixed
power of \(\log(L+2)\). Suppose the first \(h\) axes form a bank
\(X\), the next \(h\) form a dirty native bank \(W\), and
\(1\le h\le L/(40\log_2 R)\). The remaining whole native suffix
can be padded once by a factor less than two, then split into
\[
 [D_h]\,[B_h]\,[Z_c]\,[O_\ell],
 \qquad c=\max(\lceil\log_2|X|\rceil,
                   \lceil\log_2|W|\rceil),\quad \ell\ge0.8L
\]
for sufficiently large \(L\). These are disjoint raw binary fields.
Here \(D_h\) is a data field, \(B_h\) a dirty binary auxiliary,
\(Z_c\) a movement dock and \(O_\ell\) an opaque index suffix.
With total stored bit volume \(V\), there is a record permutation
\(P\) swapping the \(h\) endpoint parities of \(X\) with
\(D_h\), preserving every native quotient label and restoring
\(W,B_h,Z_c,O_\ell\), at cost
\[
 O\bigl(V(h\log_2 R+1)^\tau(\log(L+2))^{c_0}\bigr)
\]
for a fixed \(c_0\). Its inverse has the same bound. The suffix
pad must be retained through any intervening operation on \(D_h\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The binary width of the remaining suffix is at least
\(L-2-2h\log_2 R\). Removing \(2h+c\) bits leaves at least
\(L-3-3h\log_2R-2h\ge0.875L-3\), proving the stated margin.
Treat its low \(\ell\) bits as opaque coefficient lanes. Their
record width is at least \(2^{0.8L}\) times the external width,
so every fixed polynomial in the \(O(L)\)-bit public address
is paid in linear actual bit volume. Splitting this complete binary
suffix requires no permutation of its already numeric rank.

We first price the native operation
\(x_i\gets x_i\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}g_i\) on endpoint
parities, for a frozen mask \(g\) excluding \(X,W,Z_c\).
Use the eight integer updates of Lemma~\ref{lem:router}, now with
the true native place weights in \(X\) and \(W\). Each update
has offset \(\sum_i a_i\delta_i\) on one whole numeric bank;
the offset reads only the other bank and frozen controls. Exchange
that bank's binary-padded numeric field with an equal-width slice
of \(Z_c\), rotate its \emph{true valid prefix}, and return the
dock. Invalid target tails are fixed and offsets are zero when the
other bank is invalid. These two private pads have combined factor
less than four and are cropped at the completed mask boundary.
Lemma~\ref{lem:swap} gives the charge
\(O(V(h\log_2R+1)^\tau)\) for the fixed number of exchanges.

On every digit whose pair quotient is between 10 and
\(n_i/2-11\), all intermediate unit displacements stay in range.
The weighted numeric updates then coincide with the exact independent
integer tiles and implement the required parity mask, restoring
\(W\). Define the good set by these quotient margins in both
banks. Its complement has fraction \(\delta=O(h/L^3)\), and
the ideal parity mask preserves it. The packed word is a bijection
agreeing with the ideal map on the good set, so it also preserves
the complement. Extract only those exceptional output records,
compute their packed preimages and ideal destinations, sort by
\(O(L)\)-bit keys and reinsert. The charge is
\(O(V+\delta VL+M_a\operatorname{poly}(L))=O(V)\), where
\(M_a\) counts active address records before the opaque index
suffix is included. Its exponential opaque record pays the metadata.
This reconstructs the needed weighted mask, including its correction;
it does not assume a mixed-radix endpoint gather.

Write \(x\in\F_2^h\) for the endpoint vector in \(X\) and
\(d\in\F_2^h\) for the separate field \(D_h\). Perform
\[
 x\gets x+d,\qquad d\gets d+x,\qquad x\gets x+d.
\]
The first and last operations use the native mask just proved,
with \(W\) as dirty tile auxiliary and \(Z_c\) as movement dock.
Their control is \(D_h\), which is disjoint from both. The middle
operation uses Lemma~\ref{lem:router} on binary target \(D_h\)
and dirty binary auxiliary \(B_h\); its mask is the current native
endpoint vector, computed from public \(X\) addresses. The native
bank is frozen throughout this middle call. All its metadata is
again paid by \(O_\ell\). Thus no call reads its own target or
dirty tile auxiliary as a frozen control, and the data field is
never used as the native movement dock. Every complete call restores
its auxiliaries. The three equations are exactly \((x,d)\mapsto
(d,x)\), proving the operator and cost statements for arbitrary
dirty values and labels. This permutation need not preserve the
original unpadded suffix subset at an intermediate boundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact native Gaussian endpoint tensors]
\label{prop:nativephase}
On the native shapes of Lemma~\ref{lem:nativedock}, with arbitrary
external lanes and every odd modulus \(P\), the interleaved kernel
\(K_{\boldsymbol n}\) has a uniform fixed-tape implementation in
\[
 O\bigl(V(L+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}}
                   (\log(L+2))^{c_1}\bigr)
\]
bit steps for a fixed \(c_1\), including ordinary native output
order and inverse. No lower bound on the external payload width is
needed. This is an internal mathematical construction from the
already stated interchange, frozen-router and phase theorems.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Partition the endpoint axes into consecutive blocks of at most
\(h=\lfloor L/(40\lceil\log_2R\rceil)\rfloor\). There are
\(O(\log R)\) blocks, since every native radix is even and
the number of axes is at most \(L\). For a selected block,
exchange the entire aggregate prefix preceding it with that block,
putting the block first. An aggregate \([A][B]\) exchange pads
only the two whole numeric ranges by a combined factor less than
four, applies Lemma~\ref{lem:unequalswap}, and crops the completed
transposed rectangle. This costs \(O(VL^\rho)\), rather than
padding each individual native axis. The first equally many
remaining native axes give \(W\); the rest give the suffix in
Lemma~\ref{lem:nativedock}. The dimension inequality ensures enough
axes. A shorter final block uses its own shorter banks and dock.

Apply its permutation \(P\), run the exact contiguous binary
phase tensor \(C^{\otimes h}\) on \(D_h\) using
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixedphase}, and apply \(P\) again. The other
binary suffix fields are opaque Gaussian lanes for this phase call;
\(X,W\) are complete prefix spectators. On the full padded product,
the exact conjugation identity is
\[
 P^{-1}(I_X\otimes C_{D_h}^{\otimes h})P
       =C_{X,\mathrm{endpoints}}^{\otimes h}\otimes I_{D_h},
\]
with identity on every quotient label and all other coordinates.
Consequently it is identity on the \emph{entire original suffix
index}. Although the borrowed data field may cross its padding
boundary inside the call, all artificial suffix entries return to
zero after the completed conjugation. Crop only then, and undo the
aggregate exchange. All private pads have been removed before the
next block starts.

The phase charge is \(O(V(h+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}})\),
uniformly in the opaque lanes and modulus width. The two masks,
one binary mask and aggregate exchanges have exponents
\(\tau,\rho<\gamma_{\rm mix}\), with only fixed powers of
\(\log(L+2)\). Summing the \(O(\log R)\) completed block
calls proves the bound. The phase theorem supplies its inverse
with the same exact endpoint, so the identical conjugation prices
the inverse too. All sufficiently small shapes use a fixed direct
branch, and all tape families are reused sequentially.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Balanced blocks for polynomial native radices]
\label{lem:balancednativeblocks}
Let \(M=\prod_i n_i\), \(L=\lceil\log_2(2M)\rceil\),
and fix an integer \(d\ge3\). Suppose the native radices are even
and \(L^3\le n_i\le R\le L^d\). For all sufficiently large
\(L\), one can partition the consecutive axes into
\(K=128(d+1)\) nonempty blocks such that any permutation of the
whole blocks costs
\[
 O\bigl(V(L+1)^\tau(\log(L+2))^c\bigr)
\]
under the equal-field primitive, with a fixed effective \(c\).
Any one or two blocks, when brought first, form a prefix of \(h\)
axes satisfying the dock bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:nativedock};
there are at least \(h\) further native axes for its dirty bank.
All other coordinates and opaque payloads are preserved by a
completed block exchange.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(T=L-1=\lceil\log_2M\rceil\),
\(q=\lfloor T/K\rfloor\), and
\(E_0=\lceil\log_2R\rceil+K+2\). Work in the eventual range
\(q>4E_0\). Place internal cut \(j\) just after the first
native prefix whose product is at least \(2^{jq}\),
\(1\le j<K\). A single radix increases the logarithm by at
most \(\log_2R\), so cuts are distinct, all blocks are nonempty,
and the logarithm of each block product differs from \(q\) by
at most \(E_0\). The last block obeys the same bound because
\(0\le T-Kq<K\); there is no arbitrarily short final block.
Products and threshold comparisons are ordinary polynomial-time
integer setup in \(L\), absorbed by the complete native volume.

An aggregate containing \(p\) whole blocks, in any current order,
has padded binary width \(a\) with
\(\lvert a-pq\rvert\le pE_0+1\le2pE_0\).
A single block has the same bound for \(p=1\).
Pad only these two whole numeric ranges to their own next powers
of two, with combined volume factor less than four, and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:nearcommensurateswap} with \(E=2E_0\).
The exact completed transpose sends the true \([A][B]\)
rectangle to \([B][A]\); crop its private padding only then.
As \(K\) is fixed and \(E_0=O(\log L)\), this costs the
stated bound. A fixed number of aggregate exchanges implements
any permutation of the \(K\) blocks. Each is completed and
cropped before the next, so private padding does not accumulate.

One or two selected blocks have total logarithmic size less than
\(3q\). Since each native axis contributes at least
\(3\log_2L\), their axis count \(h\) satisfies
\[
 h<q/\log_2L,\qquad
 h\lceil\log_2R\rceil\le(d+1)q\le L/128<L/40.
\]
The full native axis count is at least
\((L-2)/\log_2R\), so more than \(h\) axes remain after that
prefix. Take the next \(h\) as the dirty bank; this is a view
boundary, without another gather. The remaining whole suffix has
the margin required for the separate binary fields and opaque
index of Lemma~\ref{lem:nativedock}. Small shapes failing the
displayed guard form a fixed finite range and use direct routing.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Native kernels and endpoint permutations at the equal-field power]
\label{prop:balancednativeinterface}
On the polynomial-radix shapes of
Lemma~\ref{lem:balancednativeblocks}, suppose the phase tensor
has an opaque-lane, odd-modulus bound with leading exponent
\(\gamma\ge\tau>0\) and fixed secondary logarithms.
Then native Gaussian endpoint tensors, arbitrary specified
permutations of endpoint coordinates, tensor perfect shuffles,
and their inverses have bit cost
\[
 O\bigl(V(L+1)^\gamma(\log(L+2))^{c'}\bigr)
\]
for an effective fixed \(c'\), with ordinary native output order.
The endpoint permutations preserve every quotient label.
This statement uses actual permutations, including their physical
block exchanges; it is not a free change of index convention.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the native phase tensor, process the balanced blocks in turn.
Bring the entire chosen block first using the preceding lemma.
The dock lemma supplies its separate binary data field and the
same exact conjugation \(P^{-1}C_{D_h}^{\otimes h}P\) used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:nativephase}. Its masks cost at most the
\(\tau\) power; the phase call costs at most the \(\gamma\)
power, uniformly in opaque lanes and coefficient width. Restore
the dock and the whole suffix before cropping its private pad,
then undo the actual block exchanges. There are \(K\) blocks,
not a growing number depending on \(L\).

We give the full endpoint-permutation construction. Any permutation
is a product of two involutions: on a cycle indexed by \(j\),
use the reflections \(j\mapsto-j\) and \(j\mapsto1-j\).
Group each involution's disjoint pairs by the unordered pair of
balanced blocks containing their endpoints, allowing both in one
block. There are at most \(K(K+1)/2\) families. Bring the one
or two whole blocks of a family first, without splitting them to
make their endpoint counts equal. Transport all partner indices
to their new prefix positions by the explicit block relocation.
The resulting prefix \(X\), its equally long dirty native bank,
and its once-padded remaining suffix meet the dock lemma.

Write \(x\in\F_2^h\) for its endpoint vector and take a
separate dirty binary field \(u\) from that suffix. For disjoint
source and target endpoint sets choose maps \(F,G\) with
\(FG=0\). The four complete frozen masks
\[
 u\gets u+Fx;\quad x\gets x+Gu;\quad
 u\gets u+Fx;\quad x\gets x+Gu
\]
return every initial \(u\) and apply \(x\gets x+GFx\).
Indeed after the first two masks the state is
\(u_0+Fx_0,\ x_0+Gu_0+GFx_0\); the third returns \(u_0\)
because \(FG=0\), and the fourth removes \(Gu_0\).
All operations here describe address-bit XOR, not arithmetic on
the odd-ring payload.

Each binary update of \(u\) reads only frozen native \(X\)
and uses the separate dirty binary auxiliary. Each native update
of \(x\) reads only frozen \(u\) and uses the separate dirty
native bank. The movement dock and opaque index are disjoint from
these controls. Thus the charged weighted-mask and binary-mask
subconstructions of Lemma~\ref{lem:nativedock} apply, for arbitrary
dirty inputs. A numerical bank is padded to its own binary width
and uses only that width's slice of the dock; a shorter bank is
not padded to the full cardinality of a longer one.

For a matching, three such disjoint-source/target CNOT layers swap
all its pairs, so twelve complete masks suffice for a family.
The maps \(F,G\) are just the transported partner selections.
Their generation and public-address evaluation have polynomial
cost in \(L\). The restored suffix has an opaque index of at
least \(0.8L\) bits: prepare each mask once per active control
fiber, then scan its whole opaque record. This pays all such
metadata and the already charged sparse guard correction.
No function reads its own target or dirty auxiliary as a frozen
control. Every mask restores its auxiliaries. The complete family
also restores \(u\), all quotient labels and the entire raw
suffix index. Crop its private suffix pad then and undo the
whole-block relocation. The two involutions give the required
endpoint permutation in a fixed number of these families.

The tensor perfect shuffle is the unchanged flat perfect shuffle,
which is linear-volume, followed by the endpoint rotation in
Lemma~\ref{lem:nativeshuffle}. The endpoint rotation is now priced
by the construction just given. Reverse the completed words for
their inverses. Private movement pads are always removed at their
proved restored boundaries. The original even-native dummy
coordinates, including those outside an original odd prime axis,
are different: they remain present through the completed arithmetic
matching, as before. No intermediate endpoint permutation is
assumed to preserve that smaller odd-grid subset.
\end{proof}

The same docking conjugation lifts any specified linear operator on
a contiguous binary endpoint field that returns its spectators.
Another useful frozen-mask identity is
\[
 q\gets q+Fx;\quad x\gets x+Gq;\quad
 q\gets q+Fx;\quad x\gets x+Gq,
 \qquad FG=0.
\]
It returns \(q\) and applies \(x\gets x+GFx\), even for dirty
\(q\). Disjoint-source/target CNOT batches satisfy this condition.
A coordinate permutation is a product of two involutions; each is
three parallel CNOT batches. Thus 24 frozen masks suffice to permute
arbitrary endpoint coordinates within a current native prefix.
Their docking width is that prefix, rather than the original root
width. This identity provides a second route to prefix-scaled native
adapters, without importing a characteristic-two arithmetic kernel.

These lemmas sharpen the remaining supplier question. The cited
characteristic-two controlled CRT permutations can act on our Gaussian
records by Lemma~\ref{lem:opaquerouting}, subject to their own full
machine proofs. They need not be reimplemented as Gaussian arithmetic.
Lemma~\ref{lem:invertiblestrokes} removes apparent duplication from
the actual square local Fourier words. Their matching stages reduce
to the single \emph{native} kernel of Lemma~\ref{lem:nativeselector}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:nativephase} supplies that kernel by a
separate data-field swap, rather than treating its interleaved
endpoints as already contiguous. We next reconstruct the remaining
controlled routing interface. The shuffle, ragged-network and
balanced CRT ideas are adapted from the cited
\texttt{TENSOR\_PERFECT\_SHUFFLE\_ROOT\_20261030.txt},
\texttt{GENERIC\_TENSOR\_PERMUTATION\_ROOT\_20261030.txt} and
\texttt{CONTROLLED\_TENSOR\_CRT\_ROOT\_20261030.txt}.
Here the arithmetic switch supplier is the exact odd-ring native
phase kernel just proved, rather than the characteristic-two
canonical kernel used in those notes.

\begin{lemma}[Sparse repair of selective native translations]
\label{lem:nativetranslate}
On the native shapes above, any public coordinatewise translation
by \(a_i\in\{-1,0,1\}\), and its inverse, costs \(O(V)\)
bit steps on opaque records, including output order.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(w_i\) be the native place weights and let \(P\) be the
flat cyclic rotation by \(\sum_i a_iw_i\) modulo \(M\).
It is a tail/prefix split of complete records. Let \(T\) be the
desired coordinatewise translation. They agree on every source
where each selected digit avoids the endpoint in its translation
direction. The bad source set \(B\) has fraction
\(\delta\le\sum_{a_i\ne0}1/n_i\le L^{-2}\).

Build its mask by successive native product expansions; the sum
of intermediate lengths is less than \(2M\). Rotate the mask
with the data under \(P\). These are exactly the output holes
\(P(B)\), not generally \(B\). Since two permutations agreeing
off \(B\) have equal complementary images,
\(P(B)=T(B)\). At a marked output \(y\), compute
\(x=P^{-1}(y)\) and attach destination \(T(x)\).
Native decoding and reconstruction cost \(O(L^2)\): successive
schoolbook divisions have total cost bounded by \(L\) times
the sum of the digit widths, which is \(O(L)\).
Sort those records by their \(O(L)\)-bit destinations and refill
the marked holes. With external record width \(D\ge1\), its
charge is
\[
 O\bigl(V+\delta ML(D+L)+\delta ML^2\bigr)=O(V).
\]
All mask generation, full scans, key copies, resets and rewinds
are included. Negative translations use the opposite source
endpoints. No invariance of the original bad-source set is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tensor perfect shuffles on the native axes]
\label{lem:nativeshuffle}
Writing \(n_i=2k_i\), the simultaneous permutations
\[
 s_i(k_i b_i+r_i)=2r_i+b_i,
 \qquad b_i\in\{0,1\},\quad0\le r_i<k_i,
\]
and their inverses cost \(O(VL^\rho(\log(L+2))^{c_2})\),
where \(\rho=1-3\cdot2^{-38}<\gamma_{\rm mix}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
One flat perfect shuffle \(d\) is linear-volume: split the
\([2][M/2]\) rectangle and interleave its two halves. Its
destination is \(2x\bmod(M-1)\) for \(x<M-1\), with the
last point fixed. When doubling a native digit,
\[
 2(k_i b_i+r_i)+c=n_i b_i+(2r_i+c),\qquad c\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The outgoing carry is \(b_i\), independently of the incoming
carry. Wrapping the leading carry therefore gives output digits
\(y_i=2r_i+b_{i+1}\), with cyclic indexing in most-significant
first order. After \(d\), rotate the \emph{low endpoint parities}
by one place, taking the preceding digit's parity at each position.
This changes \(y_i\) to \(2r_i+b_i\), proving
\(\bigotimes_i s_i=\text{endpoint rotation}\circ d\).
The order of this composition is essential.

The endpoint rotation is two interval reflections: reverse all
endpoint positions, then reverse all except the first. For each
reflection, partition its opposite intervals into pairs of blocks
of at most \(h=\lfloor L/(40\lceil\log_2R\rceil)\rfloor\) axes.
There are \(O(\log R)\) paired blocks. Bring one pair to
native banks \(X,Y\), with a disjoint equally long dirty bank
\(W\), by a fixed number of whole-aggregate rectangle exchanges.
Taking the first available axes for \(W\) requires at most three
pieces, so the number of exchanges is fixed. There are enough
axes since \(3h\log R<L/8\). Pad only the whole remaining
suffix, reserving a movement dock of width at most \(h\log R+1\)
and leaving an opaque index of at least \(0.8L\) bits.

Use the native weighted-mask subconstruction in
Lemma~\ref{lem:nativedock} for the three updates
\(X_i\mathrel{\mathrm{xor}=}Y_{h-1-i}\),
\(Y_{h-1-i}\mathrel{\mathrm{xor}=}X_i\),
\(X_i\mathrel{\mathrm{xor}=}Y_{h-1-i}\), on endpoint parities.
Each source bank is disjoint and frozen during its complete mask;
\(W\) is the separately restored tile auxiliary. Reverse indexing
is a public polynomial-address computation, not a physical gather.
The masks swap the required endpoints and preserve all quotient
labels. Their cost is \(O(V(h\log R+1)^\tau\operatorname{polylog}L)\).
The once-padded suffix is unchanged after the complete swap and
can then be cropped. Undo the aggregate exchanges. Each costs
\(O(VL^\rho)\), and all private pads are removed before the
next paired block. Summing the \(O(\log R)\) blocks proves the
bound. Reverse the actual operations for the inverse shuffle.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ragged routing with one global shuffle per level]
\label{lem:raggednative}
Every permutation \(\pi\) of an even \(n\)-wire set has a
word of \(O(\log n)\) full \(s_n^{\pm1}\) slots and adjacent
switch sublayers. At each level an input and an output switch
layer split into even-start and odd-start adjacent pairs. The
slot pattern depends only on \(n\), and the settings can be
generated by a deterministic polynomial-time sequential-list
algorithm from \(\pi\). No odd subnetwork is padded.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Maintain contiguous parent intervals, with the residual permutation
preserving each interval on both input and output. Pair consecutive
positions from each parent's beginning, leaving its last position
unmatched if its length is odd. Make the same output pairing and
pull it back through the residual permutation. In the bipartite
graph whose vertices are these input/output pairs or singletons,
each packet is one edge. A paired vertex has degree two. An odd
parent has one singleton on each side, giving one odd-edge path;
its alternating edge colors agree at both ends. Force that color
to the absolute parity of the parent's last wire. Even cycles
are alternately colored. Coincident unmatched packets give the
same forced-color case with a one-edge path.

The input switch \(E_{\rm in}\) puts each packet on the parity
of its color. Choose the involution \(E_{\rm out}\) so that its
action on that packet's desired destination also has this parity.
The stable global parity partition \(s_n^{-1}\) then makes the
residual permutation \(Q\) block diagonal on
\[
 [\text{all parents' even fragments}]
 [\text{all parents' odd fragments}],
\]
in parent order within each part. This is the actual stream gauge;
siblings need not be adjacent. The exact factorization is
\[
 \pi=E_{\rm out}\,s_n\,Q\,s_n^{-1}\,E_{\rm in}.
\]
Every child interval has at most half its parent's length rounded
up. Repeat on the same original \(n\) wires and this new public
block list; after \(\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) levels only
singletons remain. Completed singleton fragments use identity
switches; their subsequent shuffle frames cancel on unwind.
Each switch layer is two global adjacency-parity sublayers, since
all its selected edges are disjoint. Thus one depth has a fixed
six-slot pattern and there is one residual full-wire recursion,
not a separate full-volume execution for every child interval.
The fragment sizes and positions depend only on the preceding
block list, so topology is independent of \(\pi\). Colorings,
switch tables and residual tables use \(O(n)\) packets per
depth. Replacing every lookup by a full finite-list scan still
gives a fixed polynomial setup bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generated controlled native record permutations]
\label{prop:nativecontrolled}
Partition the native axes into static targets \(T\) and controls
\(C\). For each control tuple \(c\), suppose a fixed polynomial
integer algorithm generates a local permutation \(\pi_i(c)\)
on every target axis. For one effective fixed polynomial
\(P_1(R,L)\), records of width \(W\ge P_1(R,L)\) admit
\[
 (c,(x_i)_{i\in T})\longmapsto
       (c,(\pi_i(c)(x_i))_{i\in T})
\]
and its inverse in
\(O(V(L+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}}(\log(L+2))^{c_3})\) bit steps.
This includes the record codec, all public setting generation,
ordinary native order and a fixed tape count.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Encode each payload bit as zero or one in the fixed ring \(A_3\).
Its two residues use four bits, a fixed expansion. Keep this codec
for the complete word. At a completed record permutation every
lane again contains its original bit, so one final scan decodes it.
The native phase proposition applies over this fixed ring with
\(W\) independent lanes; it does not simulate integer addition
by field addition.

First implement \(K_T=C_T\otimes I_C\) by the five-call
selective word of Lemma~\ref{lem:nativeselector}, with only static
axis-membership diagonals. To select \(I\) or exchange on a
target canonical pair, use two completed \(K_T\) calls. Their
post-call diagonals are respectively \((Z,-iZ)\) or \((I,I)\)
on that pair. For fixed controls the factors tensor across target
axes. The choice can depend on the control tuple and the current
pair label. No control-dependent outer scalar is evaluated inside
an incomplete \(K_T\) call. Although controls can mix inside
its five native calls, the completed \(K_T\) is exactly identity
on them. Odd-start switch sublayers are conjugates by the selective
translations of Lemma~\ref{lem:nativetranslate}; missing wrap
edges get identity settings. These translations are target-only
and independent of control values.

Use Lemma~\ref{lem:raggednative} on each target permutation,
padding its depth to \(\lceil\log_2R\rceil\) with passive
singleton levels. Run the same full tensor shuffle on \emph{all}
native axes, including controls, at its shuffle slots. If \(e\)
is the signed count of completed forward minus inverse shuffles,
the physical control coordinate is
\(q_e(c)=\bigotimes_{i\in C}s_{n_i}^{e}(c_i)\).
At a scalar boundary, recover logical controls by
\(c=q_e^{-1}(c_{\rm physical})\) and generate the target
switch setting for \(\pi_i(c)\). The current target edge label
is its actual physical wire label, not an old payload key.

Induct on completed slots. On the original logical \(c\) fiber,
the target action is its prescribed network prefix, and the
control support is \(q_e(c)\). A completed target kernel or
translation preserves that support; a full shuffle updates it
and the target wiring together. A scalar uses exactly the
logical \(c\) for that fiber. This proves the invariant for
arbitrary dirty field-valued arrays by linearity. Descent and
unwind return \(e\) to zero, so the final operator is precisely
the claimed controlled permutation, with no residual gauge.

For each record, decode its public address, invert the gauge,
generate the relevant local permutation tables and their ragged
network settings, multiply the chosen fourth-root factors, and
erase/reuse the temporary tables. All are polynomial in \(R,L\).
There is no table containing all control tuples. A loose sequential
implementation uses at most \(Q^5\) bits of local route records
and \(Q^6\) full-list lookup/update operations, where
\(Q=R+L+2\). Schoolbook arithmetic and control reconstruction
fit \(O(Q^{12})\), plus the stated permutation generator's
fixed polynomial. Choose \(P_1\) to dominate these costs, table
returns and address copying. Then a scalar stage costs \(O(V)\):
park one record once, compute on separate metadata tapes and scan
its field lanes. All field multiplications are fixed finite maps.
This pays computation on \emph{every} record.

There are \(O(\log R)\) network slots, each costing a fixed
number of native phase calls, priced shuffles, translations or
linear scans. Their leading exponent is
\(\gamma_{\rm mix}>\rho,\tau\). Decode once at the completed
word. Inverse target permutations have polynomial generators
(scan a forward table for each image), so the same construction
prices the inverse. Tape families are reused sequentially; the
metadata algorithm does not add tapes at each depth or control.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Coupled ordinary-index to prime-CRT routing]
\label{prop:nativeCRT}
For distinct odd primes \(p_i\) with native axes \(n_i=p_i+1\)
as above, let \(m=\prod_i p_i\), \(M=\prod_i(p_i+1)\).
On records of width at least a fixed polynomial in \(R,L\),
the ordinary cyclic \([m]\) order converts to the prime-residue
tuple order and back in
\(O(MW(L+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}}(\log(L+2))^{c_4})\) bit steps,
including padding, inverse and metadata. The completed permutation
on the padded grid preserves every per-axis dummy pattern.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Number primes least-significant first for this argument, so
\(x=a_1+p_1a_2+p_1p_2a_3+\cdots\). Physical stream order is
\([p_t]\cdots[p_1]\). Pad once to \([p_t+1]\cdots[p_1+1]\)
by a nested lexicographic scan, inserting zeros exactly at dummy
coordinates. Valid records are still read in their original order.

For a contiguous block \(I=A\cup B\), with \(A\) lower
significance, write \(x_I=x_A+P_Ax_B\) and
\(P_A=\prod_{j\in A}p_j\). After the two child CRT maps, the
\(A\) residues are already correct. For \(i\in B\), update
\[
 z_i\longmapsto(P_Az_i+x_A)\bmod p_i.
\]
It is a permutation since \(P_A\) is prime to \(p_i\).
Its controls are only the current \(A\) residues. Reconstruct
the unique \(x_A\in[0,P_A)\) by CRT integer arithmetic on
these public address digits, using prepared products and Euclidean
inverses. These are polynomial-bit operations on \(O(L)\)-bit
integers. Fix the target's dummy coordinate. If any controlling
coordinate is dummy, define the entire merge on \(B\) to be
identity. This is an explicit bijection on every control fiber.

Use an ordered balanced merge tree. At each depth, the union of
right-child axes is one static target set, and all left-child
axes and passive axes are controls. Every node's target function
reads only its own left controls. Thus \emph{all} merges at a
depth form one controlled tensor in
Proposition~\ref{prop:nativecontrolled}. There are
\(O(\log(t+1))\) such depths; no separate full-array pass is
charged for every tree node. Induction proves the exact residues
of the original ordinary integer on all valid tuples.

Each completed merge fixes dummy coordinates individually; controls
are restored by the completed network. Therefore every dummy
pattern class is preserved, even though an internal switch or
phase call may mix such classes. Keep the entire native padding
through each complete controlled permutation. Reverse merge levels
and invert each affine permutation for the inverse CRT map, with
the same public generators and cost. After the complete map,
crop only the known dummy coordinates by a native lex scan.

All per-record generators are polynomial: decoding, CRT sums,
affine tables, gauge inversion, and sequential ragged-network
construction. The explicit \(Q^{12}\) allowance above is
sufficient for these affine families, so \(P_1=Q^{64}\)
works for all sufficiently large \(Q\), with a fixed direct
branch for the rest. Setup products, inverses and native padding
patterns are polynomial in \(L,R\). This proves the complete
bit invoice, independently of any coefficient-field CRT oracle.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The actual Fourier roots after CRT routing]
\label{lem:CRTphase}
Let \(m=\prod_i p_i\), with distinct primes, and let \(\omega\)
be a primitive \(m\)-th root. Put
\[
 E_i=(m/p_i)\bigl((m/p_i)^{-1}\bmod p_i\bigr)\pmod m.
\]
Under the ordinary residue tuple permutation on both input and
output, the \(m\)-point Fourier matrix is the tensor of the
\(p_i\)-point Fourier matrices with roots \(\omega^{E_i}\).
All exponent preparation costs a fixed polynomial in \(\log m\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The CRT idempotents satisfy \(E_iE_j\equiv0\) for \(i\ne j\),
\(E_i^2\equiv E_i\), and \(\sum_iE_i\equiv1\pmod m\).
Thus, if \(r_i=x\bmod p_i\), \(s_i=y\bmod p_i\),
\[
 xy\equiv\sum_iE_i r_i s_i\pmod m,\qquad
 \omega^{xy}=\prod_i(\omega^{E_i})^{r_i s_i}.
\]
Each factor root has order \(p_i\). This is exactly the local
\texttt{FourierIdentities.winds} convention \(u^{ij}\), with
no omitted output reordering. Euclidean inverses and products use
\(O(\log m)\)-bit integer arithmetic and polynomial setup.
The local word is the generic \(\mathrm{ballad}(p,e,u,r)\),
instantiated at these particular roots \(u_i=\omega^{E_i}\).
An old prepared \texttt{spray} table using another CRT convention
is not silently substituted. For the separate coarse dyadic split,
first use precisely the
squared-cofactor roots of Lemma~\ref{lem:largeradixlength}, then
apply this identity to its odd part. All complex roots themselves
are computed by Proposition~\ref{prop:outerprecision}; the CRT
index compiler provides no root oracle.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mixed tape and phase compiler}
\begin{lemma}[Fed columns contract and gather does not duplicate values]
\label{lem:fedcolumns}
For an engine with paid ranks at most \(J\) and target ranks at least
\(L>J\), a parent unit with \(f\) columns feeding a paid rank-\(r\)
job to a child target of rank \(t\) has
\[
 t f'=r f,\qquad f'\le\frac JL f.
\]
Along any chain of fed units, the sum of column counts is at most
\(f_0/(1-J/L)\); its positive-width part has \(O(\log(f_0+1))\)
edges. For one paid letter, gathering the fed jobs and the selected
fresh supply vectors is a coordinate selection and permutation of
their concatenated input. Its Euclidean operator norm is at most one.
These assertions do not bound fresh supply generation or tape access.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The public \texttt{Eng.slotR\_spec} in
\texttt{Mixed/Prog/SweepMath.lean} gives the exact child slot and
\(\mathrm{slotSize}(\mathrm{slotR}(p))=r\,\mathrm{cUc}(p)\).
By definition, the left side is the target rank times that child
unit's column count. Since \(r\le J<t\) and \(t\ge L\), this
gives the contraction. Sum its geometric bounds; a positive integer
column count cannot remain positive after more than
\(1+\log(f_0)/\log(L/J)\) such steps when \(f_0\ge1\).

The same theorem gives \(\mathrm{ppR}(\mathrm{slotR}(p))=p\).
Its converse, \texttt{slotR\_ppR} in \texttt{Mixed/Prog/CostC.lean},
holds on fed slots, so no two fed slots select one parent unit.
\texttt{cjob\_fed} reads that parent's selected role via the bijective
\texttt{blockJob} address placement. In the source branch,
\texttt{cjob} reads supply index
\(o+\operatorname{count}(\mathrm{srcP},t)\), which is distinct for
distinct source slots. Fed entries and source entries are distinct
components of the concatenated input, and the initially zero owned
rows add no values. Selection and permutation have norm at most one.
The identities are effective index maps; this norm argument does not
make their physical tape implementation free.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Monotone fed/source streams]\label{lem:streammerge}
Normalize the fixed target and paid direction families to coordinate
prefixes, extending each supplied family itself to a basis.
Store a class in one fixed tape bank per role, with units in increasing
code order and each unit's \(2^n\) entries in binary address order.
For a paid call from \((n,h+1,\mu)\) to \((n,h,\mu r)\), its fed
jobs and selected fresh jobs can be merged into child slot order by
sequential scans. The fed live returns scatter back in parent unit order.
If \(s\) is the working residue width and \(F,S\) are the numbers of
fed and fresh jobs, these operations cost \(O_W((F+S)2^n(s+1))\)
bit steps, including metadata and child live-bank initialization.
Normalization is charged separately by Lemma~\ref{lem:affinetape}.
The statement does not sum these local costs over the recursion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(W_0\) be the number of live roles, and write an increasing parent
code as \(u=a+W_0v\), \(0\le a<W_0\). The actual
\texttt{slotR} formula in \texttt{Desc.lean} is
\[
 \operatorname{slotR}(u)=
 W_0\operatorname{PC}_{\rm child}(v)+a.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:fedcolumns} ensures that every such tail \(v\) is in
the child region. Within one tail the formula increases with \(a\);
between distinct valid tails their increasing enumeration ranks
\(\operatorname{PC}_{\rm child}(v)\) strictly increase. Thus
\(\operatorname{slotR}\) is strictly increasing on parent units.
Its inverse on fed slots is \texttt{ppR}. On nonfed slots
\texttt{siR} selects the next fresh vector, since it is the starting
offset plus the number of previous source slots. One forward scan of
child slots therefore reads the next fed vector or the next fresh vector,
with neither sorting nor repeated scans of earlier input vectors.
Regenerating this pattern splits fed returns in their original order.

For an explicit address check, a parent paid job has \(d=rf\) active
low bits; its child target has \(d=tf'\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:fedcolumns}. With coordinate-prefix bases, the remaining
\(n-d\) bits are the concatenation of the inactive grain and instance
bits. Both placements encode a job address as
\(x+2^d y\), with the same low active value \(x\) and high value
\(y\). Changing the grain/instance boundary from \(mf\) to \(mf'\)
changes a view, not the sequential bit order. No growing digit shuffle
is hidden in the equality \(tf'=rf\). For nonprefix paid bases the
fixed affine normalizer must actually run before this view is used.

Split the child job stream by its live-role index, a fixed finite range,
to initialize one bank per live role; fill owned banks with zeros.
The number of banks is fixed by the engine. Point letters read aligned
unit/address streams on these banks. Region codes, column counts and
fed/source flags can be generated by bounded loops in polynomial
\(n+1\) work per job, since \(h=O_W(\log(n+2))\).
Their cost is absorbed by that job's complete \(2^n\) entries.
Use a sequential stock cursor instead of recomputing an absolute large
offset: \texttt{childC} reserves its own fresh slots before descendants,
and \texttt{kidsUp} advances by their count plus subtree consumption.
Hence depth-first processing reads the source list in its defining
\texttt{srcSizes} order. This prices fresh input access; ordering all
descendant source answers on return is a separate composition cost.

Working-modulus conversion of a fetched fresh record costs linear
width by Lemma~\ref{lem:pendinglift}; it introduces no lookup.
The bounded probe checks 650 mixed configurations, 673 nonconsecutive
fed gaps, 589 wrong-order controls, and complete small normalized
address placements. Its combined 1861206 assertions also cover
Lemma~\ref{lem:unequalswap} and 54 whole-tree moment identities;
it stores no transform arrays.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pending-word modulus width in the mixed handler]
\label{lem:pendinglift}
Fix an engine word satisfying Lemma~\ref{lem:oddclear}, label dimension
\(m\), minimum target rank \(L\ge2\), and maximum paid rank
\(J<m\). Consider the valid mixed-handler schedule with class invariant
\(m\mu L^h\le n\), class edges decreasing \(h\), leaf paid calls
of dimension \(r\mu\), and top rest calls made after their root class
has returned. If the outer requested modulus has \(s\) bits and the
outer transform has \(2^k\) entries, all requested and working residue
moduli need at most
\[
 s+O_W\bigl(\log^2(k+2)\bigr)
\]
bits. Fresh supply vectors need only be lifted when read. This is an
exact residue-value width bound for this schedule, not a tape running-time
bound or an evaluation of the outer complex chirp roots.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A whole-word invocation increases its requested modulus by at most
the fixed factor \(Q^2\), and returns to its caller's requested
modulus by Lemma~\ref{lem:oddclear}. At ambient dimension \(n\),
validity and \(\mu\ge1\) bound a pending class chain by
\(1+\log_L(n+1)\) words. At a leaf, the next top dimension obeys
\[
 n'=r\mu\le J\mu\le(J/m)n.
\]
Set \(\xi=J/m<1\). Between such descents, top rest calls do not
increase dimension or retain their completed root's extra modulus.
Hence the active word stack has groups at dimensions at most
\(k,\xi k,\xi^2k,\ldots\). There are \(O_W(\log(k+2))\)
nontrivial groups, and their total pending depth is bounded by
\[
 \sum_{i:\xi^i k\ge1}O_W(1+\log(\xi^i k+1))
   =O_W(\log^2(k+2)).
\]
Multiplying \(Q^2\) once per pending word proves the width bound.
Neither the number of batches nor fresh-column contraction is used.

The source contracts are explicit: \texttt{Valid.lean} defines the
region invariant; \texttt{Desc.lean}'s \texttt{kidsUp} decreases height
and \texttt{exitC} sets the leaf dimension to \(r\mu\).
In \texttt{MixedCost/TopDesc.lean}, \texttt{topRoot} takes its fresh
supply from the unchanged top input \(v\), and \texttt{topRest}
uses the root's low-bit answers. \texttt{TopBody.copy\_elem} obtains
the root answer before making the rest calls. These contracts have
been read and hash-pinned, without a new Lean build.

For ownership, every ancestor requested modulus divides a descendant's
working modulus. If an input role is specified modulo a divisor \(P_0\)
of the common requested modulus, choose any lift to that modulus.
Its independent productive endpoint is dyadic, so reduction modulo
\(P_0\) is independent of this choice. Supply values can consequently
remain stored at their owner modulus and be lifted on fetch; the same
argument applies to fed values and returns. This proves numerical
correctness of the residue schedule. It assigns no cost to the fetch,
scatter, unequal-field transpose, or complete handler execution.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Weighted routing budget for a mixed tree]
\label{lem:weightedledger}
Fix a separated finite word with target ranks \(t_l\in[L,m]\),
paid ranks \(r_j\in[1,J]\), and \(J<L\). For \(0<z<1\), put
\[
 T_z=\sum_l t_l^z,\quad P_z=\sum_j r_j^z,\quad q=P_z/T_z<1.
\]
For a finite mixed tree, let \(\mathcal S,\mathcal E\) be its
multisets of source and exit sizes, and \(f_u\ge1\) the column count
of each unit. Then
\[
 (T_z-P_z)\sum_u f_u^z
   =\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}s^z-\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}e^z,
 \qquad
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}e^z\le q\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}s^z.
\]
For the valid top tree at dimension \(n\ge m\), every source size
satisfies \(n/m<s\le n\). If
\(\epsilon=z m^{z-1}-q>0\), then
\[
 |\mathcal S|n^z-
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}(n-s)^z-
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}e^z
 \ \ge\ \epsilon\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}s^z.
\]
Consequently any fixed local toll
\(O(f^a\log^c(f+2))\), \(0\le a<z\), summed over units,
is bounded by a constant times this last budget. A top transpose toll
\(O(|\mathcal S|n^a)\) and a source-copy toll
\(O(|\mathcal S|\log(n+2))\) also fit. Actual bit costs additionally
multiply these budgets by record volume and word width.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(I\) be the total \(z\)-moment of internal fed jobs.
Every target input is a source or an internal job, and every paid
output is an internal job or an exit. Therefore
\[
 T_z\sum_u f_u^z=S+I,\qquad
 P_z\sum_u f_u^z=I+E,
\]
where \(S=\sum s^z\), \(E=\sum e^z\). Subtraction proves the
identity. Since \(I+E=q(S+I)\), we have
\(E=qS-(1-q)I\le qS\). In particular
\(\sum_u f_u^z\le S/(T_z-P_z)\).

For a nonroot source at a rank-\(r\) call, write its child unit's
column count as \(f'=\mu r c\) and its target rank as \(t\).
It is a source precisely because its putative parent code is outside
the region: \(m\mu t c>n\). Thus its size
\(s=tf'=r\mu t c>rn/m\ge n/m\), whereas child validity gives
\(s\le mf'\le n\). At the root, maximality of height \(G\)
gives \(mL^{G+1}>n\); every root source has size at least
\(L^{G+1}>n/m\). This also proves the source interval directly
from the region rules used by \texttt{Flow.src\_size}.

Concavity, or integration of \(z x^{z-1}\), gives
\[
 n^z-(n-s)^z\ge z n^{z-1}s
 =z s^z(s/n)^{1-z}\ge z m^{z-1}s^z.
\]
Sum this inequality and subtract \(E\le qS\) for the stated
budget. Since \(a<z\), the fixed toll is at most a constant times
\(f^z\) for every integer \(f\ge1\). The unit identity pays its
sum. Finally \(S\ge |\mathcal S|(n/m)^z\) pays the top and
copy tolls. All constants depend on the fixed word and the positive
slack; no logarithmic factor is silently multiplied into an exponent.
\end{proof}

For the present strictified word these budget premises have an elementary
check well above the finite rate exponent. Set
\[
 \rho=1-3\cdot2^{-38},\qquad z=1-5\cdot2^{-39}.
\]
Here \(\rho<z<1-2^{-37}\), and the strict moment certificate at
\(t=1-0.00312\) implies
\(q\le(J/L)^{z-t}\). Since \(z-t>3/1000\),
\(\log(L/J)=1024\log(17/16)>1024/17\), and
\(e^{-x}\le(1+x)^{-1}\), we obtain \(q<7/8\).
Also \(m=145^{1024}<2^{8192}\), so
\[
 z m^{z-1}\ge1-(1-z)(1+\log m)>15/16.
\]
Thus \(\epsilon>1/16\). The affine toll exponent \(\tau\)
and the unequal-transpose exponent \(\rho\) both fit below \(z\).
With an outer working width \(s+O_W(\log^2(k+2))\), the algebraic
budget is compatible with a final phase exponent \(1-2^{-37}\).
This earlier parameter choice is included as context for the same phase word.
The selected stronger phase application below uses the later explicit
moment certificate and mixed-BIT interchange supplier.

\subsection{Physical stock storage and the mixed-tree bit budget}

\begin{lemma}[Assigned stock intervals and source-answer stacks]
\label{lem:stockstack}
For a fixed finite word, a mixed class can process exactly its assigned
source interval and return source answers in that interval's original
order using a fixed number of tapes. For a tree of height \(H\),
the extra stock movement is
\(O_W(2^n B_{\rm stock}(H+1))\) record moves, where
\(B_{\rm stock}\) is its number of source vectors. Parking active
role banks costs \(O_W(2^n)\) moves per unit. This permits arbitrary
record widths and does not scan an ancestor's unread stock on each call.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Keep active banks separate from stack tapes. A stack head stays at its
top between operations. Push a block in reversed physical bit order,
with a reverse-readable length header at its top; popping reads its
payload forward. Each push or pop, including a preparation scan, header,
rewind and erasure, costs linear block bit volume. Empty blocks need
only their headers. A fixed number of such stacks serves every depth;
parent frames remain below the current stack tops. Empty-block and
frame headers are charged to the nonempty class's per-unit term,
rather than to an empty stock interval.

The source list of a class is the concatenation, in paid-letter order,
of its children's own fresh slots followed by their descendant stock.
Partition precisely this assigned interval into that fixed number of
chunks, and park them in reverse order on the input stack. Pop the next
chunk when its paid letter is reached. Its initial fresh vectors merge
with the fed stream by Lemma~\ref{lem:streammerge}; only its remaining
contiguous descendant interval is passed to the child. Compact that
interval to a current input bank if necessary. A class with no descendant
stock passes an empty interval. It never hands the child the entire
ancestor supply array. Parent role banks and loop state are pushed on
their own stacks while the child uses the active banks, then popped
before inserting fed returns. The word has a fixed number of letters,
so this costs constant work per parked record per class level.

On return, separate the child's own fresh live answers from its fed
answers. Concatenate those fresh answers, in slot order, with its
descendant source answers. Push this completed chunk on an answer
stack. At the end of the word, pop its fixed number of chunks onto a
second stack, then pop them forward into the source-answer stream.
Two block-order reversals preserve the contents of each block and restore
paid-letter order. All parent answer chunks stay below frame markers.
Each source record belongs to one such interval at each ancestor level,
so summing the linear interval costs gives the asserted \(H+1\) factor.
The role-bank term is charged separately per unit, rather than to the
possibly much larger stock interval.

For a top batch, group complete tree copies and the leftover block in
one input scan and push them on the input stack. Process copies from
last to first and push their completed outputs on a separate output
stack. Popping restores the initial batch order. Thus a growing number
of top copies does not multiply scans of the remaining batch. The same
method parks a root's answered source vectors before their independent
rest calls. Only a current block is active when a recursive call starts.
All metadata is stored at the visited stack tops, not at a randomly
addressed ancestor location.
The top loop terminates at its frame marker, not by copying a large
batch counter into every child frame. The original batch header is
parked once for the whole top call; grouping updates its counter
amortized during the initial scan. This matters when \(\log B\)
is much larger than the current vector dimension.

The bounded \texttt{mixed\_stock\_stack\_probe.cpp} checks this
abstract LIFO schedule on 255 stock cases and 288 top-copy cases,
including unchanged ancestor stack prefixes and 1032 wrong-order
controls. It does not model the data transforms or replace the
linear-bit-time push/pop construction above.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Reducing a source answer to its owner modulus]
\label{lem:ownerreduce}
If \(0\le x<P_0D\), the canonical residue \(x\bmod P_0\)
can be computed in \(O(S(1+\log(D+1)))\) bit steps, where
\(S\) bounds the bit widths of \(x,P_0D\). No coprimality is required.
Consequently owner reduction in Lemma~\ref{lem:pendinglift} has cost
\(O_W(S\log^2(k+2))\) per Gaussian record, with a harmless constant
for bounded \(k\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The quotient is less than \(D\). Shift \(P_0\) left by the largest
possible quotient position, then compare and subtract down through
those \(O(1+\log(D+1))\) positions. Every comparison, subtraction
and scan costs \(O(S)\) on ordinary tapes. The final remainder is
canonical. Apply this to both residue components. The descendant
working modulus divided by the source's owner modulus has
\(O_W(\log^2(k+2))\) bits by Lemma~\ref{lem:pendinglift}.
Fresh answers may be reduced once on leaving their productive live
slots; their independent endpoints justify that reduction even if fed
roles had a larger requested modulus. Descendant source answers already
reduced to the same top owner's modulus need only be copied afterwards.
An explicit quotient bound can be generated from the fixed denominator
product \(Q\) and the depth bound, so no unrestricted division is needed
to discover \(D\). A sufficiently large power of \(Q\) has only
\(O_W(\log^2(k+2))\) bits and supplies the shift count directly.
The bounded probe checks 32032 small-quotient reductions, including
shared factors. Its combined stock/reduction checks total 302988.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Shrinking exits leave an eventual routing budget]
\label{lem:shrinkingexits}
In the separated rule above, suppose \(P_t\le T_t\) for some
\(0<t<z<1\). At root height \(G\), put
\[
 b_G=\bigl(J(J/L)^G\bigr)^{z-t}.
\]
Then \(P_z<T_z\), and the top tree satisfies
\[
 \sum_e e^z\le b_G\sum_s s^z,\qquad
 |\mathcal S|n^z-\sum_s(n-s)^z-\sum_e e^z
 \ge\bigl(zm^{z-1}-b_G\bigr)\sum_s s^z.
\]
In particular the budget has a fixed positive slack for all sufficiently
large \(n\). This conclusion does not require
\(P_z/T_z<zm^{z-1}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Separation gives
\(P_z/T_z\le(J/L)^{z-t}(P_t/T_t)<1\).
Every exit is a product of \(G+1\) paid ranks, so it is at most
\(J^{G+1}\). Every source is greater than \(n/m\ge L^G\).
The weighted cancellation at exponent \(t\) gives
\(\sum_e e^t\le\sum_s s^t\). Hence
\[
 \sum_e e^z
 \le J^{(G+1)(z-t)}\sum_e e^t
 \le J^{(G+1)(z-t)}L^{-G(z-t)}\sum_s s^z.
\]
This is the first estimate. Use the source/rest inequality of
Lemma~\ref{lem:weightedledger} for the second. Since \(J/L<1\)
and the maximal root height tends to infinity with \(n\), eventually
\(b_G<zm^{z-1}/2\). The finite smaller range is handled directly.
This reconstructs the shrinking-exit argument of the cited source's
\texttt{Mixed/Bound.lean} at the level needed by the physical schedule;
it does not assume its RAM compiler supplies bit costs.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Mixed-tree compilation with charged bit primitives]
\label{prop:mixedtapecompiler}
Let a fixed rational finite word with Gaussian monomial adapters have
proved all-column independent productive kernels and initially zero
owned rows, as in Lemmas~\ref{lem:ramframe} and~\ref{lem:oddclear}.
Suppose its separated target ranks lie in \([L,m]\), its paid ranks
in \([1,J]\), and \(J<L\), \(m\ge3\). All its fixed address maps
are binary affine maps with fixed fourth-root quadratic phases.
Let \(\rho=1-3\cdot2^{-38}\), and choose \(\rho<z<1\).
Assume either
\[
 q=P_z/T_z<z m^{z-1}.
\]
or \(P_t\le T_t\) for some \(0<t<z\).
Then, for every fixed \(z<\zeta<1\), there is a deterministic
fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape algorithm for \(C^{\otimes k}\) over
every odd \(A_P\), of bit cost
\[
 O_W\bigl(2^k\ell s(k+1)^\zeta\bigr)
\]
on \(\ell\) independent lanes, uniformly in \(k,\ell\) and odd
\(P\). Setup, coefficient arithmetic, source and owned storage,
padding, input/output order, and all recursion branches are included.
This is a compiler from a proved finite word; it assumes no external
unit-cost RAM-to-tape compiler.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Extend each supplied productive direction family to a fixed ordered
basis \(G\); conjugate the whole word by the outside pair
\(P_G\) at entrance and \(P_G^{-1}\) at exit. For each paid family,
enter its call with \(P_G^{-1}\) and exit with \(P_G\).
Each conjugation retains its actual vectors. These are actual
Lemma~\ref{lem:affinetape} calls. Their finite counts affect constants,
not the invoice of paid ranks. Classes use the region, slot and source
definitions already read above, with the physical implementation of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:streammerge} and~\ref{lem:stockstack}. Point matrices,
zero initialization and erasure use sequential role banks. Diagonal
phases use Lemma~\ref{lem:streamphase}. The complete ranges are retained
as opaque selected or spectator fields throughout; there is no growing
field conversion or random lookup.

First count work for records of a common padded width, leaving one
global arithmetic factor to the end. At top dimension \(n\), choose
maximal root height \(G\) with \(mL^G\le n\). The number of region
codes is at most \(W_0^G\); the number of class calls is at most
\(1+P_0+\cdots+P_0^G\), where \(W_0,P_0\) are fixed role and
paid-letter counts. Thus all source, exit and metadata counts are
bounded by a fixed polynomial in \(n+1\). Binary enumeration, integer
arithmetic and sequential tables generate them in polynomial work.
This setup is absorbed by complete \(2^n\)-entry vectors.

Write \(B_n=|\mathcal S|\). Affine adapters, role-bank parking and
phase letters cost at most
\[
 K2^n\sum_u f_u^\rho\log^c(f_u+2)
\]
for a fixed \(c\). Source-answer storage costs
\(O_W(2^n B_n\log(n+2))\), since \(G=O_W(\log(n+2))\).
Rest transposes cost \(O_W(2^n B_n n^\rho)\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:unequalswap}. Lemma~\ref{lem:weightedledger} pays
all these local costs by \(K'2^n\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}s^z\).

For clarity include the rounding branches explicitly. Let \(T(n,B)\)
be the top work on \(B\) vectors of \(2^n\) records, measured before
the global width factor. We prove, for a fixed polynomial \(p\) and
constant \(A\),
\[
 T(n,B)\le A2^n\bigl(Bn^z+p(n)\bigr)\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Below a fixed threshold use direct butterflies. If \(B<B_n\), use
them on every vector; otherwise process complete \(B_n\)-vector copies
and use them on the fewer than \(B_n\) leftover vectors. Their work
is \(O_W(2^n B_n(n+1))\), covered by \(2^np(n)\).
All grouping, stack and batch counters have linear data-scan cost plus
polynomial setup. No independent input vector is rounded to a larger
transform length.

Within one complete copy, a leaf exit of size \(e\) calls
\(T(e,2^{n-e})\); a source of size \(s\) has a rest call
\(T(n-s,2^s)\), with the two physical low/high transposes included
in the local budget. A zero-dimensional rest is omitted as an identity
on the already held buffer; its eventual copying is paid by the local
linear scan. The inductive main terms therefore sum to
\[
 A2^n\left(\sum_e e^z+\sum_s(n-s)^z\right).
\]
They leave at least \(A\epsilon2^n\sum_s s^z\) for a fixed
\(\epsilon>0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\). Under the direct
ratio premise take \(\epsilon=z m^{z-1}-q\); under the lower-exponent
moment premise use Lemma~\ref{lem:shrinkingexits} and take
\(\epsilon=zm^{z-1}/2\).
The child rounding terms have total at most
\[
 A p_1(n)2^{\theta n},\qquad
 \theta=\max(J/m,1-1/m)<1,
\]
for another fixed polynomial \(p_1\): exits have \(e\le(J/m)n\),
rest dimensions have \(n-s<(1-1/m)n\), and both multiplicities
are polynomial. Exponential domination makes this at most half the
available budget for all sufficiently large \(n\), independently of
\(A\). Choose \(A\) to pay the local work and the remaining finite
range. This proves the induction, including arbitrary small batches.

To compute one \(k\)-bit tensor, take \(a=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\),
\(b=k-a\). Run \(T(a,2^b)\) on the low bits, physically exchange
the two fields, run \(T(b,2^a)\), and exchange them back.
Their kernels commute on disjoint bits and give \(C^{\otimes k}\).
The polynomial remainder terms are \(O_W(2^k)\), and the two
transposes cost \(O_W(2^k(k+1)^\rho)\). Handle \(k\le1\)
directly. Hence this operation count is \(O_W(2^k(k+1)^z)\).

Finally use Lemma~\ref{lem:oddclear} at every whole-word invocation.
Lemma~\ref{lem:pendinglift} bounds working width by
\(S=s+O_W(\log^2(k+2))\). Store all active and parked records at
this common padded width. Fixed point arithmetic and modular halvings
are linear in \(S\); Lemma~\ref{lem:ownerreduce} adds only a fixed
polynomial in \(\log(k+2)\). Modulus headers are copied at the visited
stack tops. Long arithmetic for shape headers is absorbed by its
complete vector or lane scan; no general \(S\)-bit multiplication or
unrestricted division is charged as linear. Thus for some fixed \(d\)
the actual bit work is at most
\[
 O_W\left(2^k\ell\bigl(s+\log^2(k+2)\bigr)
 (k+1)^z\log^d(k+2)\right).
\]
Since \(s\ge1\) and \(\zeta>z\), the fixed logarithmic factors
fit \((k+1)^{\zeta-z}\). The owned zeros, local primitive padding,
stock buffers and visited stack frames are removed on return. An external
linear copy fixes the output bank and initial lexicographic order.
This proves the claimed uniform bit bound.
\end{proof}

The preceding strictified-word budget is an earlier parameter choice. The selected phase-only transfer below uses the later moment certificate and the mixed-BIT interchange supplier.
For the explicit dyadic product of Lemma~\ref{lem:wordproduct}, take
\(Q=1\) throughout: all words and source owners share the original
modulus \(P\), the working width is exactly \(s\), and no owner
reduction is necessary. The stack and rounding proof still applies.

\begin{proposition}[Mixed compilation with short batch transposes]
\label{prop:stripedcompiler}
Retain the all-column and physical-word hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:mixedtapecompiler}, for a fixed dyadic word,
and its separated ranks \(1\le J<L\le m\). Suppose equal-field
interchange has a proved opaque-record bound with exponent
\(0<\tau<1\), including a fixed power of secondary logarithms.
If \(P_t\le T_t\) for some \(0<t<z<1\) with \(\tau\le z\),
then the phase tensor has a deterministic fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape
bit algorithm of cost
\[
 O_W\!\left(2^k\ell s(k+1)^z(\log_2(k+2))^C\right)
\]
for an effective fixed \(C\), over every odd \(s\)-bit modulus,
uniformly in \(k,\ell,s\). No general unequal-field exchange
primitive is required. In particular the endpoint \(z=\tau>t\)
is permitted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We construct the batch program and its lane extension together by
induction on target dimension. This does not assume a fast one-vector
algorithm in order to prove its batch bound. Every operation is
shape controlled or a lane-wise numerical operation of the grammar
in Lemma~\ref{lem:stripedrest}. We keep the original dyadic word,
direction families, source intervals, owned clears and modulus.

Here is a complete classification of the physical routing. Fixed
affine normalizers use Lemma~\ref{lem:affinetape} at exponent
\(\tau\); its selected fields have equal column width, and all
other coordinates and tuple lanes are complete spectators. Point
letters, zero rows, quadratic phases and role-bank parking have
linear volume cost or that same adapter bound. Fed placements and
fresh-source access use the monotone streams of
Lemma~\ref{lem:streammerge}: after the actual prefix normalizers,
both sides use the same low active address. The parent/child
grain boundary is a view, with \(tf'=rf\), not a permutation.
The assigned input intervals and returned source answers use the
fixed stack schedule of Lemma~\ref{lem:stockstack}. These stages
add a fixed power of \(\log(n+2)\), not an unequal-field transpose.
Leaf exits already have prefix batches in the normalized layout.
Only the source-rest calls and final single-vector seam previously
exchanged large low/high fields; we replace both below.

Choose a polynomial \(p(n)\le C_0(n+2)^d\) covering the same
small-batch leftovers and setup as in the earlier batch induction.
Its degree is fixed by the word's polynomial source/class counts
and header routines. It can be chosen independently of the constant
number of bit moves per logarithmic scale: the new bit moves are
linear scans, and their batch loops are charged to the data,
rather than to a table of all batch addresses. Their constants may
enlarge \(C_0\). Fix an integer \(K\ge d+4\) and put
\[
 b(n)=K\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil.
\]
Only ordinary integer logarithms and multiplication by fixed
integers are used. If \(mb(n)>n\), use direct butterflies.
This is a fixed finite range, since \(b(n)=O_W(\log(n+2))\).
All small batches and leftover vectors remain direct as before.

Each complete copy consumes the next assigned input interval and
uses relative source/unit codes. It does not copy an absolute
\(B\)-dependent batch index into every child descriptor. The outer
copy counter stays in its parent's stack frame with low bits at
the visited top; updates over the complete batch loop are amortized
linear. This preserves uniformity for large \(B\), including the
borrowed prefix batches. Preparing their integer headers is once
per call. The constant \(K\) changes the number of short scans and
header lengths, but not the degree \(d\) of the unamortized
source-count/leftover polynomial. Thus choosing \(K\) after \(d\)
is not a circular choice.

For the other calls, every source has size \(s'>n/m\), so
\(b(n)\le s'\). After the root's low \(s'\)-bit answer is back
in its original source buffer, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:stripedrest} to the high \(r=n-s'\) bits,
borrowing \(b(n)\) existing low bits. The recursive descriptor is
\[
 \bigl(r,\ B=2^{b(n)},\ \lambda=2^{s'-b(n)}\ell\bigr).
\]
Its target dimension is smaller than \(n\); tuple lanes grow but
do not enter the termination measure. A zero-dimensional rest is
identity and is omitted. All actual source vectors and their
arbitrary lane values are preserved. The \(2^{b(n)}\) inputs are
existing coordinate slices, not additional zero-initialized or
correlated helpers receiving productive credit.

Let \(H(n)=(\log_2(n+2))^C\). The inductive batch estimate is
\[
 T(n,B;\ell,s)\le
 A2^n\ell s\bigl(Bn^zH(n)+p(n)\bigr),\qquad n\ge1.
\]
An exit \(e\) still calls \(T(e,2^{n-e};\ell,s)\).
For a source \(s'\), the new rest call contributes at most
\[
 A2^n\ell s\left((n-s')^zH(n-s')
                           +2^{-b(n)}p(n-s')\right),
\]
apart from its already priced short permutation. In particular
the changed lane count has not multiplied the main volume by
\(2^{s'}\) a second time. Moreover
\[
 2^{-b(n)}p(n-s')\le C_0(n+2)^{-4}.
\]
Thus all rest rounding terms sum to at most
\(A2^n\ell s C_0|\mathcal S|(n+2)^{-4}\).
They are negligible compared with
\(A2^n\ell s\sum_{s'\in\mathcal S}(s')^zH(n)\), since
\((s')^z>(n/m)^z\). The unchanged exit rounding terms are
polynomial times \(2^{\theta n}\), \(\theta<1\), as before.

Lemma~\ref{lem:shrinkingexits} supplies, eventually, a fixed
\(\epsilon>0\) with
\[
 |\mathcal S|n^z-\sum_{s'\in\mathcal S}(n-s')^z
                          -\sum_e e^z
       \ge\epsilon\sum_{s'\in\mathcal S}(s')^z.
\]
The unit moment identity pays the adapter tolls
\(\sum_u f_u^\tau\log^c(f_u+2)\), even at \(\tau=z\),
after enlarging \(C\). Source-answer stacks and the two short
transposes per source cost
\(O_W(2^n\ell s|\mathcal S|\log^c(n+2))\), which the same
budget pays. Monotonicity bounds every child \(H\) by \(H(n)\).
Choose \(A\) to cover the local work after the two rounding
contributions have consumed, say, half the positive budget. Direct
finite branches and leftovers are covered by \(p\). This proves
the batch estimate, without a new logarithmic factor per depth.

For a single \(k\)-bit tensor take \(a=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\),
\(d=k-a\). Apply \(T(a,2^d;\ell,s)\) to the naturally
contiguous low-bit vectors. For the high \(d\)-bit tensor, borrow
only \(b(k)\) low bits and use
\(T(d,2^{b(k)};2^{a-b(k)}\ell,s)\), then undo the short
permutation. If \(a<b(k)\), use the fixed finite direct branch.
The first polynomial remainder is exponentially diluted by
\(2^d\); the second is diluted by \(2^{b(k)}\).
The two kernels commute on disjoint coordinate fields, and their
product is exactly \(C^{\otimes k}\). Their short permutations
cost only \(O(2^k\ell s\log(k+2))\), absorbed since \(z>0\).

Tuple arithmetic scans ordinary \(s\)-bit components independently;
it does not invoke arithmetic at a larger packed modulus. Frozen
controls never read the retired suffix coordinates. Stack headers
record the actual block volumes and return all heads and source
answers in their original order. Dyadic closure retains precisely
the original odd modulus and width. The fixed bank count, zero
initialization, erasure, setup, padding and final external output
copy are the same charged operations as in the earlier compiler.
This completes the fixed-tape construction and all its branches.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform modular powers of two with short arithmetic]
\label{lem:shortshift}
Let \(P\ge3\) be odd, of \(s\) bits, and \(t\ge0\).
After \(O(s\log^4(t+2))\) bit steps of preparation, multiplication
of a canonical residue by either \(2^t\) or \(2^{-t}\) modulo
\(P\) costs \(O(s\log^4(t+2))\) bit steps on fixed tapes.
The constants are independent of \(s,t,P\). No special modulus,
prime assumption or growing alphabet is needed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Balanced multiplication of \(u\)-bit integers costs
\(O(u\log^2(u+2))\) using classical Sch\"onhage--Strassen
arithmetic. Divide-and-conquer division, including sequential operand
copies and rewinds, costs \(O(u\log^3(u+2))\) on \(O(u)\)-bit
operands. The same loose bound covers inversion modulo \(2^u\) by
precision-doubling Newton steps. These standard integer algorithms
are described in \cite[Sections 1.3.5, 1.4.3 and 2.5]{MCA}; the
multiplication bound used is the classical one \cite{SS}.

If \(s\le t+1\), prepare \(2^t\bmod P\) and
\(((P+1)/2)^t\bmod P\) by binary exponentiation. Multiplications
and reductions have \(O(s\log^3(s+2))\) cost, and there are
\(O(\log(t+2))\) of them. Apply the prepared scalar by one such
modular product per residue. Since \(s\le t+1\), the stated bound
follows. Handle \(t=0\) by copying.

Now suppose \(s\ge t+2\), and put \(h=t+2\), \(D=2^{s-h}\).
Write \(P=pD+p_0\), \(x=aD+x_0\), with \(0\le p_0,x_0<D\).
Here \(p\ge2^{h-1}\), \(a\le p\). Compute the short quotient
\(q=\lfloor2^t a/p\rfloor\). For the true quotient
\(q_* =\lfloor2^t x/P\rfloor\),
\[
 \left|\frac{2^ta}{p}-\frac{2^tx}{P}\right|
 =\frac{2^t|ap_0-px_0|}{pP}
 <\frac{2^t(a+p)}{p^2}
 \le2^{t-h+2}=1.
\]
Thus \(|q-q_*|\le1\). Form \(R=2^tx-qP\), then make at
most one addition or subtraction of \(P\); since \(-P\le R<2P\),
the result is canonical. The division producing \(q\) has only
\(O(t+1)\)-bit operands, regardless of \(s\).

Multiplication of an \(s\)-bit word by an \(h\)-bit word costs
\(O(s\log^2(h+2))\) here: split the long word into consecutive
\(h\)-bit blocks, multiply each by the short word, and carry between
adjacent blocks in one sequential pass. At most \(\lceil s/h\rceil\)
balanced products and linear-volume copying are needed. This prices
\(qP\) without applying an \(s\)-bit FFT independently at every
record. Extracting the leading blocks and the final correction cost
\(O(s)\). The total forward cost has the claimed bound.

For the inverse direction, prepare
\(a=P^{-1}\bmod2^t\). Starting from one bit, the iteration
\(a\gets a(2-Pa)\) doubles the valid low bits, with all operands
truncated to that precision. Put
\(r=-ax\bmod2^t\), \(0\le r<2^t\). Then
\[
 y=\frac{x+rP}{2^t}
\]
is an integer in \([0,P)\), and \(2^t y\equiv x\pmod P\).
The products computing \(r\) are short, and \(rP\) has the same
blocked long-by-short implementation. No inverse modulo \(P\) is
needed. All temporary words have \(O(s+t)\) bits. Operands are
streamed to dedicated arithmetic banks while the main array head stays
at its current record; no parent array is rescanned per scalar.

The bounded \texttt{odd\_modular\_shift\_probe.cpp} checks 171105
cases over odd prime and composite moduli, including both size branches,
7517 canonical corrections and 432 failures when the leading quotient
loses its two guard bits. Its 601843 assertions check exact identities;
the asymptotic bit cost is supplied by this argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Instantiated mixed phase and Walsh bound]
\label{thm:mixedphase}
Put \(\gamma_{\rm mix}=1-2^{-37}\). A deterministic machine with
a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed number of ordinary tapes applies
\(C^{\otimes k}\) over every odd \(A_P\), on \(N=2^k\) records
of \(\ell\) lanes, in
\[
 O\bigl(N\ell s(k+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}}\bigr)
\]
bit steps, uniformly in \(k,\ell,P\). The same lexicographic input
and output order is retained, and preparation, all data movement,
owned storage and cleanup are included. The Walsh transform and its
inverse over \(\Z/P\Z\) have the same bound, uniformly over all
odd \(P\). For fixed \(P\) this is
\(O(N\ell(k+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}})\); modulo \(2^w+1\) it is
\(O(N\ell(w+1)(k+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}})\).
Exact Boolean integer Walsh spectra and \(0/1\) XOR counts cost
\(O(N(k+1)^{1+\gamma_{\rm mix}})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take the actual raw word of Proposition~\ref{prop:recordunit}, with
all supplied directions and absolute phases retained. Form its
1024-fold explicit product by Lemma~\ref{lem:wordproduct}, complete
target families to \(L=17^{1024}\), then split paid families at
\(J=16^{1024}\). Completion retains every existing supplied vector
and appends independent new ones; splitting partitions the actual
family. The label dimension is \(m=145^{1024}\). All target ranks
are in \([L,m]\), all paid ranks are in \([1,J]\), and the exact
charged moment certificate above proves \(P_t<T_t\) at
\(t=1-39/12500\). Its sufficient normalized upper bound is
\(16777216015641/16794395869184<1\). The explicit product has
precisely the inventories used by this bound, without requiring the
vendor's product implementation.

Every owned row starts and ends zero. By dyadic coefficient closure
the all-column identities specialize to every odd \(A_P\), with
the original modulus and \(s\)-bit components throughout. Normalize
the resulting supplied direction families exactly as specified above.
Choose \(z=1-5\cdot2^{-39}\). Then
\[
 t<\rho=1-6\cdot2^{-39}<z<\gamma_{\rm mix}<1.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:mixedtapecompiler} applies. Its explicit stock,
rounding, seam and arithmetic costs give the uniform phase bound.
There is no import of the community's conditional bit compiler.

The identity \(H_0=(1-i)\operatorname{diag}(1,i)\,
C\,\operatorname{diag}(1,i)\), already used in the direct proof,
gives the Walsh wrappers. For fixed \(P\) their remaining scalars
cost linear volume. For arbitrary odd \(P\), write
\((1-i)^k=2^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}(-i)^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
(1-i)^{k\bmod2}\). Lemma~\ref{lem:shortshift} prices its power
of two, and also the inverse's scalar \(2^{-k}\), in
\(O(N\ell s\log^4(k+2))\) steps. This is lower order than the
phase bound because \(\gamma_{\rm mix}>0\). The remaining signs,
component exchanges and fourth-root phase rows cost linear volume.
Modulo \(2^w+1\) the earlier signed-shift implementation remains
valid, with the two exponent residues kept separately. The phase
inverse itself is \(X^{\otimes k}C^{\otimes k}\); reversing the
opaque address blocks implements \(X^{\otimes k}\) in linear volume.
The Boolean output argument
and point products of Corollary~\ref{cor:integer} apply with
\(\gamma_{\rm mix}\) in place of \(\gamma\); their cost remains
lower order than the displayed bound. All wrappers include canonical
input/output conversion and the external linear output copy.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Native selector parameter lemmas}
\begin{lemma}[One odd modulus for the entire native matching word]
\label{lem:nativewordrecovery}
Consider a tensor matching layer on \(t\) native axes, each applying
\(C\) on its genuine pairs and identity on fixed coordinates.
Suppose its input is \(2^{-q}(a_j+ib_j)\), with signed integers
\(|a_j|,|b_j|<2^A\). Set
\[
 P=2^{A+2t+4}+1.
\]
Execute the complete native permutation/five-kernel/diagonal word over
\(A_P\), on the input numerators, without numerical decoding or rounding
inside it. The exact Gaussian output numerators with common denominator
\(2^{q+t}\) are recovered by multiplying the output residues by
\(2^t\) and taking balanced representatives. The modulus needs no
coprimality with any native radix or transform length. Recovery costs
linear bit volume times a fixed power of \(\log(t+2)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After multiplication by \(2^t\), every row of the tensor matching is
a Gaussian-integer row. A two-point \(C\) has numerator entries
\(1+i,1-i\), and an identity row may use numerator two. The sum
of absolute real and imaginary components in such a row is at most
\(4^t\). Each component of the target numerator is therefore less
than \(2^{A+2t}\), hence strictly below \(P/2\).

The native matching identity, its record permutations and fourth-root
diagonals are exact over every odd \(A_P\). All their divisions are
by two; every numerical outer coefficient has already been supplied
as a dyadic input numerator. The whole word consequently returns the
residue of the actual matching result, regardless of the sizes or
ordinary rational denominators of its internal states. Multiplication
by \(2^t\) gives the target integer residue. The component bound
makes its balanced representative unique. This argument uses the
completed matching endpoint, not fifteen separately rounded axis calls
in its five native kernels. All native padding stays present until
that endpoint has restored the dummy-coordinate classes.

The odd residue width is \(A+O(t+1)\). Lemma~\ref{lem:shortshift}
prices multiplication by \(2^t\) uniformly in that width, followed
by linear balanced decoding and, if requested, one final rounding to
the original fixed-point grid. Opaque-record routing over the fixed
ring \(A_3\) moves these residue encodings as bit lanes; it does
not replace their arithmetic by arithmetic in \(A_3\).
The common fractional exponent is one call descriptor, not a fresh
per-record integer computation. This proves the recovery invoice.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The native composition is uniform in the primitive exponents]
\label{lem:nativeparameter}
Suppose equal-field interchange, unequal-field exchange and odd-ring
phase tensors have the proved bit bounds above with fixed exponents
\(0<\tau_0<\rho_0<\gamma_0<1\), respectively, uniformly in

constructions have cost
\[
 O(V(L+1)^{\gamma_0}(\log(L+2))^c)

of Proposition~\ref{prop:outerprecision}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We check the actual uses rather than retuning an assumed compiler.
The weighted masks in Lemma~\ref{lem:nativedock} use a fixed number
of equal-field interchanges, true-prefix rotations and sparse correction.
Their opaque suffix is excluded from the frozen controls in every
actual call: controls are \(D_h\), or another explicitly selected
native source bank. Metadata is paid by that suffix. Their cost power
is \(\tau_0\), while the direct correction stays linear.
The aggregate rectangle exchanges use \(\rho_0\). Docking conjugates
and \(O(\log R)\) phase blocks in Proposition~\ref{prop:nativephase}
therefore cost \(V(L+1)^{\gamma_0}\operatorname{polylog}L\).

fixed powers of \(\log L\), since \(R=O(L^4)\).
No storage schedule or dummy boundary changes with the exponents.

For numerical data, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:nativewordrecovery} to each
completed arithmetic matching slot, including its outside local
permutations. Its residue width is \(O(W+L)\), still linear in
the chosen wide word. There is no numerical rounding inside its
native kernels or routing calls. The exact target is the same
\(C_\mu\) as in the source stroke, so the outer prefix/error
analysis uses precisely the same stroke norm and coefficient bounds.
It acquires no factor from the number of operations inside an exact
modular call. Recovery and final rounding are charged as in that lemma.
This closes both the physical exponent substitution and the precision

\end{proof}
\subsection{Finite BIT supplier and strengthened phase budget}
\begin{proposition}[Finite substitution before one common cover]
\label{prop:delayedbitcover}
Suppose a fixed finite chronological \(\F_2\)-data program has
rational address-projector requests in an ambient head space of
dimension \(N_0\ge2\). Internal jobs may be replaced by finite
native words computing their specified projector endpoints on
arbitrary dirty inputs, with owned zero rows explicitly allocated
and cleared. Embed these words and all their fixed rational chart
routes in the same ambient space before canonical child lowering.

Suppose the resulting word, including all source/complement repairs,
has full interchange endpoint on \(B_0\) independent productive
arrays and has \(F_0\) paid rank-one projector leaves. If
\[
 F_0/B_0<N_0^{1-C_0},\qquad 0<C_1<C_0<1,
\]
then, for any fixed rational \(0<\theta<1\) satisfying
\[
 a=(1-\theta)C_1+\theta a_L<\theta<1-a,
\]
the charged fixed-tape construction gives an opaque equal-field
interchange with leading saving \(a\) and fixed secondary logarithms.
This is conditional on the stated finite program and leaf bound;
it does not import a mixed compiler's termination or invoice claim.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For rational idempotents \(P,Q\) of equal rank, take rational bases
of their images and kernels, with image columns first. Their full
basis matrices give \(S=B_PB_Q^{-1}\) and \(SQS^{-1}=P\).
On one eventual \(\operatorname{GL}_{N_0}(R_w)\) cover, route a
complete target-role fiber by \(x_g\mapsto y_{gS}=x_g\), run
the native word whose target is \(D_{hQh^{-1}}\), then undo that
route. The physical endpoint is exactly \(D_{gPg^{-1}}\).
Different target roles may have different fixed matchers; their
routed inputs at a common row are still independent arbitrary data.
The routes are genuine whole-cover bijections, priced by
Corollary~\ref{cor:qadiccoverport}, not dense address maps.

Apply the common external \(Q_A=\operatorname{diag}(A,I)\)
only after all finite substitutions. It commutes with pointwise
data operations and fresh-cover row routes since \(A\) excludes
all those fresh fields. Thus every internal word receives the same
external weight. Only the final leaves are lowered by
Lemma~\ref{lem:weightedgoodchild}. Their possibly different pivot
weights are then permitted ancestors for their separate recursive
calls. Placing previously normalized, differently weighted internal
jobs into one scalar word would not satisfy this argument.
Owned zero storage stays zero under address permutations and is
excluded from \(B_0\). Source/complement completion uses
\(D_PD_{I-P}=D_I\); all its paid work is already required in
\(F_0\), not added after certifying the moment.

Choose the address prime after this finite program is fixed, avoiding
all its denominators and rank witnesses. Put \(d=C_0-C_1\) and
require \(q>256N_0^5/d\). The density and actual fallback proofs
give, at \(\sigma=1-C_1\),
\[
 \mu_\sigma\le(F_0/B_0)N_0^{-\sigma}
                  (1+64N_0^5/q)
 <N_0^{-d}(1+d/4)<{1+d/4\over1+d/2}<1.
\]
The penultimate step uses \(N_0^d\ge1+d/2\), since
\(\log2>1/2\). Each good leaf has width
\(\lfloor n/N_0\rfloor\), and fallback children have no larger
width. All finite scalar, route, ownership and normalization counts
replace constants in the recurrence; they do not multiply child time.

Keep \(w=\lceil E^\theta\rceil\) at the root. The decoder proof
uses \(E\le w^{1/\theta}\) instead of \(E\le w^{1000}\) and
is otherwise unchanged. One cover slot has cardinality
\(B_0|\operatorname{GL}_{N_0}(\F_q)|q^{N_0^2(w-1)}\).
Depth is \(O_{N_0}(\log E)\). The following generic reserve argument
uses the same cardinality proof as Lemma~\ref{lem:threecoverstock},
with \(N_0\) and \(B_0\) replacing the fixed template parameters.
It borrows \(O_{N_0,B_0,q}(w\log E)\) digits and pads once.
It passes volume divided by \(B_0|\operatorname{GL}_{N_0}(\F_q)|\),
retaining all high matrices. The stopped recurrence gives
\(O(n+n^{1-C_1}w^{1-a_L}\operatorname{polylog}w)\).
Let \(R\) reverse one \(n\)-atom field. An in-place call of the core
map \(F_I(H,D)=(C_nD,C_nH)\) on its first and last
\(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) blocks realizes \(R\), fixing the middle atom
when \(n\) is odd. Therefore
\[
 R_{\rm left}F_I R_{\rm left}(H,D)=(D,H),
\]
so the ordinary exchange uses three core calls. Each is completed
before the next begins and restores the common root stock and pad.
The root exponents are \(1-\theta\), \(1-a\), and \(\theta\),
with a reserve logarithm. The two strict inequalities on \(\theta\)

\begin{lemma}[Integer lockstep flow and physical field ownership]
\label{lem:finitebitflow}
Let a finite native word have target-role ranks in \([L,m]\), paid
rank list \(r_1,\ldots,r_p\) in \([1,J]\), and \(1\le J<L\).
For every integer top rank \(N\ge m\), it has a finite acyclic
integer flow of native unit instances. Every original input occurs
once as a source job of rank \(N/m<s\le N\). All unmatched paid
requests are terminal exits of rank at most
\(J^{G+1}<N\), where \(G\) is maximal with \(mL^G\le N\).
If \(P_t\le T_t\), \(t>0\), then its source and exit lists obey
\[
 \sum_{\rm exits}e^t\le\sum_{\rm sources}s^t.
\]
Fed owned fields receive no new independent-source credit.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use actual productive role identities as a finite alphabet, with
letter \(a\) having target rank \(k_a\). A class is a chronological
paid-letter sequence \(\sigma\) of length \(d\le G\), with
\(\rho_\sigma=\prod_{j\in\sigma}r_j\). Its units are target-role
words \(w\) of length \(G-d\), with
\[
 c(w)=\prod_{a\in w}k_a,\quad f=\rho_\sigma c(w),\quad mf\le N.
\]
These are finite integer sets. Each unit is embedded in an
\(mf\)-dimensional rational subspace of the common \(N\)-space,
with complete complementary spectators.

For a parent unit whose word is \(aw'\), its paid round \(j\)
is matched to target role \(a\) of child unit \((\sigma j,w')\).
The ranks are literally equal:
\[
 r_j\rho_\sigma k_a c(w')
       =k_a\rho_{\sigma j}c(w').
\]
The child is admissible since its column count is smaller by
\(r_j/k_a<1\). First-letter deletion is a bijection between
parent jobs and these fed child slots; retaining the chronological
letter identity distinguishes repeated equal ranks. Unfed target slots
are distinct source arrays. At depth zero their ranks are at least
\(L^{G+1}>N/m\). At positive depth an unfed slot fails its parent's
admissibility test, so its rank exceeds \(r_jN/m\ge N/m\).
Every target rank is at most \(mf\le N\). Only depth-\(G\)
units have unmatched paid requests, of rank at most \(J^{G+1}\).
Since \(N\ge mL^G\ge L^{G+1}>J^{G+1}\), each is proper.

Process all units of one class in their actual word order. At paid
letter \(j\), suspend them all, complete the entire child class
\(\sigma j\), restore its matched fields, then advance the parents.
Depth strictly increases in a child, and sibling rounds are completed
sequentially, so this defines a finite acyclic chronological word.
Input slots within a class are physically disjoint: fed slots come
from distinct parent units at one fixed paid letter, owned fields
have distinct unit tags, and source slots have distinct original tags.
Different sibling rounds may use the same parent field, but their
lifetimes do not overlap. Current values may be correlated; the native
word's correctness is for all inputs, not statistical independence.

Allocate one owned bank per unit instance, retaining it until that
unit clears it. One bank per depth would generally alias simultaneous
live parents. A paid request on a copied parent centre is fed as that
very field, not replaced by an additional source. Its child has distinct
owned storage. Parent point operations resume only after the child's
whole-job endpoint. Whole-array adapters are the fixed rational
right-route matchers, and every one is restored; role-bank permutations
are actual finite bank moves. No fractional piece of an array is sent
to a different unit without such an adapter.

Finally sum each unit's \(t\)-potential, multiplying the word moment
by its integer column count to the power \(t\). Every internal paid
job cancels exactly against its matched target slot. The uncancelled
terms are sources minus exits, and each unit contributes
\((T_t-P_t)f^t\ge0\). This proves the inequality with all integer
multiplicities and owned-target requests included.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Effective finite uniform BIT precompilation]
\label{thm:finitebitcompiler}
Suppose the separated native word of Lemma~\ref{lem:finitebitflow}
has all-column independent projector endpoints, finite fixed rational
right routes, and explicit owned zero storage, and \(P_t\le T_t\)
for \(0<t<1\). For every rational \(t<z<1\) there is an effectively
specified finite word with ambient dimension \(N\ge2\), \(B_N\)
independent full-interchange inputs and only rank-one paid requests,
whose count \(F_N\) satisfies
\[
 F_N<B_NN^z.
\]
All operations and ownership counts remain finite and charged. No
asymptotic mixed BIT compiler is assumed as a premise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Construct words \(U_n\) by strong induction on the integer rank.
For small \(n<m\), take one array and its \(n\) disjoint rank-one
requests; \(U_0\) is identity. Write \(B_n,F_n\) for productive
and paid counts. At \(n\ge m\), form the preceding integer flow.
Let its source ranks be \(s_i\), \(1\le i\le S\), and ordered
exit ranks \(e_j\). They and all \(n-s_i\) are smaller than \(n\).
Choose a positive integer \(a\) divisible by every needed
\(B_{e_j}\) and \(B_{n-s_i}\). A product of those finitely many
integers suffices; an LCM is optional. Provide \(aS\) independent
original inputs, indexed by source slot and circuit replica.
Replication here is of the circuit, never copying input values.

First apply each source's complementary rank-\(n-s_i\) projector
to its \(a\) replicas, grouped into integral batches for
\(U_{n-s_i}\). Match its actual rational image to the smaller
word's canonical full endpoint by the equal-rank right routes.
Run the \(a\) copies of the lockstep flow. At each terminal exit,
the \(a\) replicas form integral batches for \(U_{e_j}\). Restore
the matching routes and field ordering before any parent resumes.
All inductive subwords are still native rational-projector words;
none has been lowered to a weighted child or given a separate cover.
Their fixed embeddings and right-route matrices extend by identity
to the common \(n\)-space.

Each source receives its complementary projector and its assigned
source projector, so \(D_PD_{I-P}=D_I\) gives a full interchange
on that original input. Fresh source answers inside a child are parked
after their independent endpoint and are not subsequently cleared with
the child's owned banks. Fed fields are the original suspended parent
fields. All original inputs occur exactly once in \(aS\); every owned
array is excluded. This supplies full arbitrary-input endpoints and
explicit cleanup. Inductively substitute the smaller words completely;
only rank-one requests remain. No procedure assumes that differently
normalized internal weights can be mixed.

Put \(\ell(n)=F_n/B_n\), \(B_n=aS\). The literal construction has
the exact count identity
\[
 \ell(n)={1\over S}\left(\sum_i\ell(n-s_i)+\sum_j\ell(e_j)\right).
\]
Clearing batches multiplies productive and paid counts equally. It
creates no rounding or unused input credit. Scalar, COPY, CLEAR,
bank-move and rational-route counts have their own finite inventories;
they are not omitted from the eventual supplier cost.

Choose \(t<z_0<z\). Write \(w_s\) for source multiplicities divided
by \(S\), and \(x_e\) for exit multiplicities divided by \(S\).
Then \(\sum_s w_s=1\) and potential cancellation gives
\(\sum_e x_e e^t\le\sum_s w_s s^t\). For
\(\vartheta=\log J/\log L<1\), the largest exit satisfies
\(E(n)\le J(n/m)^\vartheta\). Hence
\[
 \sum_e x_e e^{z_0}
 \le (mE(n)/n)^{z_0-t}\sum_s w_s s^{z_0}.
\]
The coefficient tends to zero. For every \(s/n\ge1/m\), concavity
also gives
\[
 n^{z_0}-(n-s)^{z_0}
 \ge z_0n^{z_0-1}s
 \ge z_0m^{z_0-1}s^{z_0}.
\]
Eventually the exit coefficient is less than half this fixed positive
coefficient. Strong induction in the exact count recurrence now proves
\(\ell(n)\le A n^{z_0}\), with finite \(A\) covering all smaller
dimensions. Consequently some fixed \(N\) has \(\ell(N)<N^z\).

The construction is effective without an explicit numeric estimate
of \(A\): enumerate ranks, generate these finite integer recipes and
counts, and test the strict inequality. For \(z=p/q\), its test is
the integer comparison \(F_N^q<B_N^qN^p\). The preceding bound proves
termination. Only after choosing that finite word are its full matrix,
owned, role and route inventories and its single address prime fixed.
Their constants may be enormous and are not uniform in the candidate
dimension \(N\). This is the finite-precompilation order of limits,
not a free dynamic simulation at growing input length.
Tensoring the finished rational projectors and charts by \(I_f\)
preserves the construction for every outer column multiplicity;
point instructions are copied once, not tensor-powered. The terminating
generator specifies a fixed machine with the finite bank inventory of
its winning recipe; that inventory does not grow with the later input.
The fixed recipe can be hardwired, and any fixed-table startup cost
on those already fixed banks is included in the global time constant.
This uses no unpriced universal interpreter or dynamic tape allocation.
All input-dependent preparation remains charged.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dyadic complex phase interface for the precision supplier}
\begin{corollary}[Exact dyadic complex kernels and charged rounding]
\label{cor:dyadicphase}
Let \(k\ge0\), \(N=2^k\), \(B\ge1\), and \(q\ge0\).
Suppose each of \(\ell\ge1\) lanes has complex inputs
\(x_j=2^{-q}(a_j+i b_j)\), where the signed integers satisfy
\(|a_j|,|b_j|<2^B\). There is a fixed finite-alphabet, fixed-tape
machine returning the exact values of \(C^{\otimes k}x\) as
Gaussian integer numerators with common denominator \(2^{q+k}\), in
\[
 O\bigl(N\ell(B+k+1)(k+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}}
       +\log(q+2)\bigr)
\]
bit steps. The same holds for the inverse phase tensor. Alternatively,
the machine returns dyadic values on the original \(2^{-q}\) grid,
with error at most \(2^{-q-1}\) in each real and imaginary component,
within the same bound. The exponent \(q+k\) is stored as a binary
descriptor; a string of \(q+k\) zero bits is not materialized.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put \(h=B+2k+4\) and \(P=2^h+1\). Its residue width is
\(s=h+1=O(B+k+1)\). Construct its binary description and convert
the signed input numerators to canonical residues in \(O(N\ell s)\)
steps, retaining their ordinary address order. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixedphase} in \(A_P\). No complex scalar is
approximated during that invocation.

The matrix \(2C\) has Gaussian integer entries \(1+i,1-i\).
Consequently
\[
 z=2^k C^{\otimes k}(a+i b)\in\Z[i]^{N}
\]
in each lane. Unitarity of \(C^{\otimes k}\) gives
\[
 |\Re z_j|,|\Im z_j|
 \le |z_j|<2^{B+(3k+1)/2}<\frac P2.
\]
Multiply each returned residue by \(2^k\) modulo \(P\), using
Lemma~\ref{lem:shortshift}. This costs
\(O(N\ell s\log^4(k+2))\), including scalar preparation.
Since \(2^k\) clears the target denominator in
\(\Z[i,1/2]\), reduction of \(z\) is exactly the resulting
residue. For each component, subtract \(P\) if its canonical
residue exceeds \((P-1)/2\). The displayed bound proves that this
linear-volume operation recovers the unique actual integer component,
not merely a congruent answer. The returned exact value is
\(z_j/2^{q+k}\).

For the grid-rounded alternative, divide each signed component of
\(z_j\) by \(2^k\), rounding to nearest with a fixed tie rule.
Scanning its binary magnitude once gives the quotient, rounding bit
and carry in \(O(s)\) steps. The resulting component error is at
most \(2^{-q-1}\). Only the final return is rounded; there is no
accumulation of unpriced internal rounding. Adding \(k\) to the
common exponent descriptor costs \(O(\log(q+2)+\log(k+2))\).
The inverse is \(X^{\otimes k}C^{\otimes k}\); reversing the
complete address blocks costs linear volume and has the same norm and
denominator bounds. Finally
\(\log^4(k+2)=O((k+1)^{\gamma_{\rm mix}})\), so all these costs
fit the asserted running time. Setup, signed conversion, residue
components, exact reconstruction and the external output copy are
included on a fixed number of tapes.
\end{proof}
\subsection{A fixed-template retained-stock calculation}

\begin{lemma}[Fresh-cover density and retained high-matrix stock]
\label{lem:threecoverstock}
For the formal producer with \(m=67\), fix a prime \(q>2^{80}\)
preserving its finite rational ranks and port denominators. Each
positive-rank residual is bad on fewer than \(10^{-16}\) of fresh
low cover classes. One root reserve of \(O(w\log(E+2))\) digits
per selected field supplies every descendant cover without new padding.
A child batch has volume \(V/L\), where
\[
 L=90140|\operatorname{GL}_{67}(\F_q)|,\quad
 A_w=q^{4489(w-1)},\quad W_w=LA_w,
\]
and retains all \(A_w\) high matrices of its parent cover.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed base projector \(P\) of rank \(r\), a leading northeast
minor of \((gPg^{-1})C_m\) agrees, up to a fixed column-order sign,
with a leading principal minor of \(gPg^{-1}\).
Clearing \(\det(g)^j\) from its size-\(j\) minor
gives a polynomial of degree at most \(mj\). It is nonzero:
conjugate \(P\) to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0)\). The fixed prime
exclusions preserve this witness. The sum of degrees for
\(j=1,\ldots,r\) is less than \(m^3\). The polynomial zero bound
and \(|\operatorname{GL}_m(\F_q)|>q^{m^2}/2\) give bad fraction
at most \(2m^3/q<10^{-16}\). Unit minors lift as unit conditions
over \(R_w\). Inherited weights exclude this fresh cover and do
not change the test. Moments average each edge separately, so no
union over the edge list is needed.

Use the root depth budget
\[
 D_0=48(\lceil\log_2(E+1)\rceil+1).
\]
All proper child ratios are at most \(66/67\), and
\(2\cdot66^{47}<67^{47}\), so this bounds every path. Choose
the least \(r_0\) with \(q^{2r_0}\ge W_w^{D_0}\), and increase
it by fewer than \(w\) digits to \(r\) with \(w\mid E-r\).
Since \(\log_q W_w=O(w)\),
\(r=O(w\log(E+2))=o(E)\). A fixed finite range with \(r\ge E\)
uses direct digit moves. Exchange the high \(r\)-digit fields and
move the right high field before the remaining low head field, as
in the arbitrary-width wrapper of Theorem~\ref{thm:e8swap}. The
short moves cost \(O(Vr)\). The joined high fields supply
\(q^{2r}\) existing row values. Pad this single row range to
\[
 R'=W_w^{D_0}\left\lceil q^{2r}/W_w^{D_0}\right\rceil<2q^{2r}.
\]
Group it as an outer factor and \(D_0\) successive \(W_w\) slots.
In a fresh slot the order is low cover type, productive role, all
high-matrix entries, and remaining unused slots. This groups
consecutive numeric records without entrywise radix conversion.
Counters and one root copy/zero-fill scan realize it.

At depth \(d\), unused stock has lost \(W_w^d\), but all ancestor
high matrices remain complete earlier spectators. A paid edge fixes
only a productive role and low cover type. It passes \(1/L\) of
the current volume, retains the entire high cube, and obtains the
next fresh slot solely from unused stock. After \(d\) such choices
the retained high factors contribute \(A_w^d\), and unused stock
has size \(R'/W_w^d\); their product is \(R'/L^d\). Dividing
child volume by \(W_w\) too would count the high-matrix saving twice.

Partition current streams by the fixed finite low types and roles,
keeping their high-matrix runs together. Execute ports on complete
high cubes by Corollary~\ref{cor:qadiccoverport}. Use stack-top
push/pop as in Lemma~\ref{lem:stockstack} to park current inactive
banks while a child runs. Assigned unused slots and all current
spectators travel with that child; parked ancestor intervals are not
scanned. Each fixed local instruction has only a fixed number of bank
operations. Their copying, headers and rewinds cost linear active
volume. The 69 owned centre rows have the same stock axes, are
allocated on reusable private banks, and are cleared at their proved
boundary. Their residuals are already in the invoice; they add no
productive rows. Banks are reused across depths, with parent banks
below stack tops, so the tape count is fixed.

Ancestor matrices and instruction choices are read from sequential
prefix counters and current stack descriptors. Their depth is
\(O(\log E)\), including at small-width descendants, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:rootdecoder} applies. An exterior batch or payload
index is not in the coefficient expression. Completed calls restore
the entire row slot, although intermediate calls may populate the
once-added invalid rows. Crop only after the completed root endpoint.
No child redoes the wrapper or introduces another pad.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rank pivots and later-field work}
Every fixed rational matrix \(A\) has a factorization
\(A=E_1\Pi E_2\), with invertible lower-triangular \(E_1,E_2\)
and a partial permutation \(\Pi\) with \(\operatorname{rank}A\) ones.
Choose the topmost nonzero row and its rightmost pivot, clear to its
left by lower-triangular column operations and below by lower-triangular
row operations, remove the pivot indices preserving order, and reverse.
Choose one fixed odd prime \(q\) avoiding all denominators and all
nonzero diagonal-unit numerators in the finite frame/factor table.
Finite elimination and trial division construct it once, independently
of every input length.

Write two address chunks as \(m_0\) fields of \(b\) radix-\(q\)
digits. The address permutation
\(\Phi_A(H,D)=(H+AD,D)\pmod {q^b}\) is valid for singular \(A\) too.
Lower-triangular changes are updated in descending coordinate order,
with earlier controls still old, by later-field rotations.
Fixed rational unit scalings cost linear volume: multiplication by a
fixed integer splits the integer interval into a fixed number of
increasing pieces, merged by the unique preimage residue; reversing this
implements its inverse. Negation reverses nonzero blocks and then each
block's bit order. Offset setup is polynomial in \(b\), absorbed by the
complete \(q^b\) fiber. Spectator counters advance amortized.

Apply \(E_1^{-1}\) to \(H\), \(E_2\) to \(D\), the \(\Pi\)
shears, then undo. Each pivot is implemented by
\[
 D_j\gets D_j+H_i;\quad
 \operatorname{SWAP}(H_i,D_j);\quad
 D_j\gets H_i-D_j.
\]
Its outside operations target a later field. Exactly one smaller swap
is used per pivot.

\subsection{Power and arbitrary widths}
Define a separate power-width procedure \(\operatorname{SwapPow}_j\)
on layouts
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &[B_0][R_{\rm row}][B_{\rm pre}][q^e][B_{\rm gap}][q^e][B_{\rm suf}],\\
 &e=m_0^j,\qquad W_0^j\mid R_{\rm row}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
It exchanges the two selected fields, fixes the designated row and all
spectators, and returns lexicographic order. The pretarget spectator
\(B_{\rm pre}\) is allowed because a pivot need not be the first slot
of its parent chunk. The base \(j=0\) is a fixed-radix digit exchange.
For an internal call, use the already supplied row pool; do not create
new padding or invoke the public arbitrary-width lift.
Split whole rows into equal roles. Common-frame permutations commute with
bitwise XOR, and telescoping gives \(H\gets H+D\) on every row after
bank restoration, by the same logical-input frame invariant used for
the phase construction. Each rank pivot calls
\(\operatorname{SwapPow}_{j-1}\) at volume \(V/W_0\), width
\(e/m_0\), and the inherited designated row pool divided by \(W_0\).
All unselected parent fields are retained as collapsed pretarget, gap
or suffix spectators. Induction supplies exactly \(s_0\) such calls.
Convert that single shear into a whole swap by
\(D\gets D-H;\ H\gets H+D;\ D\gets H-D\).
Only the middle step uses the motif. The normalized recurrence is
\(F(e)\le(s_0/W_0)F(e/m_0)+C\), yielding \(O(e^\tau)\).

For arbitrary \(e\), put \(j=\lceil\log_{m_0}e\rceil\) and choose
the least \(\rho\) with \(q^{2\rho}\ge W_0^j\).
Eventually \(\rho=O(\log e)<e\); the fixed remaining range uses direct
digit exchanges. Exchange the two existing high \(\rho\)-digit fields,
move them together before the low fields, and regard their
\(q^{2\rho}\) values as rows. Pad that range once to a multiple of
\(W_0^j\), by less than two. Partition the existing low width
\(e-\rho\) into consecutive base-\(m_0\) power pieces.
The sum of their costs is \(O(Ve^\tau)\).
Completed calls restore their row coordinates, so crop only after full
role restoration and restore the high-digit layout in \(O(V\log e)\).
Move right-hand short fields left first-to-last, left-hand fields right
last-to-first. No target width is rounded upward.

To swap binary ranges \(2^u\), pad them numerically to the least
\(q^e\ge2^u\), at a volume factor below \(q^2\).
Numerical order is radix lexicographic order; no per-entry conversion
occurs. Apply the whole \(q\)-power swap and crop invalid numerical
coordinates only afterward. The final swap preserves the valid square;
intermediate piece swaps need not. Since \(e=O(u)\), the bound is
\(O(V(u+1)^\tau)\).

\subsection{Fixed tapes and descriptors}
At every power-width child the complete original chunk ranges remain
selected or spectator fields. Only independent role rows shrink.
Park current inactive siblings, copy only the active child, and pop
siblings on return using stack-top heads. The finite \(s_0,W_0\)
affect additive constants, not a multiplier on child time. Reuse the
same fixed banks at each depth and erase current visited intervals only.

therefore fits \(O(V_j)\). Local descriptors list a fixed number of
selected fields and collapsed spectators, not all ancestor subdivisions.
All local temporary padding is removed before return.
This proves Lemma~\ref{lem:swap}.

\begin{lemma}[A common rational basis for partial-swap pivots]
\label{lem:e8genericpivot}
For any fixed finite family of rational idempotents in dimension \(m\),
one fixed rational basis makes the lower--permutation--lower factor of
every rank-\(r\) partial swap \(S(P)\) have, with \(k=\min(r,m-r)\),
exactly the exchanges
\[
 H_i\leftrightarrow D_{m-1-i}\ (0\le i<k),\qquad
 H_i\leftrightarrow D_i\ (k\le i<r),
\]
and no other exchanges. Its cost is \(r\) narrow interchanges when
\(2r\le m\); otherwise it is \(m-r\) narrow interchanges and
one same-order interchange of width \((2r-m)f\).
The additional triangular work is \(O(V)\), and no external basis
change is applied to the input or output.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(R_P(i,j)\) for the rank of the first \(i\) rows and last
\(j\) columns of \(S(P)\), in physical \(H,D\) order. These ranks
are unchanged by invertible lower triangular factors and determine
their permutation factor. For \(r=\operatorname{rank}P\), they
are at most
\[
 U_r(i,j)=\begin{cases}
 \min\{i,j,r,m-r+(i+j-m)_+\},&i,j\le m,\\
 \min\{i,r+(i+j-2m)_+\},&i\le m<j,\\
 \min\{j,r+(i+j-2m)_+\},&j\le m<i,\\
 i+j-2m+U_r(2m-j,2m-i),&i,j>m.
 \end{cases}
\]
The first bound uses both \(P\) and \(I-(I-P)\). For a mixed
corner, add the corresponding \(P\) columns to the added \(I-P\)
columns; only overlapping identity columns remain beyond rank \(r\).
The last bound is the complementary-submatrix rank identity, since
\(S(P)^{-1}=S(P)\).

All bounds are simultaneously attained by an explicit projector.
Put \(\tfrac12\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
on each pair \(\{i,m-1-i\}\), \(0\le i<k\), and put diagonal
ones at the remaining positions \(k\le i<r\). Its rank is \(r\).
Each pair's rank-one factorization from the preceding appendix has
one pivot exchange \(H_i,D_{m-1-i}\); the disjoint diagonal ones
give the stated middle exchanges. Their lower factors have disjoint
supports and combine into lower factors. Counting this permutation
in a first-\(i\)/last-\(j\) corner gives precisely \(U_r(i,j)\).

Every rank-\(r\) rational idempotent is conjugate to this witness.
For each projector and each positive corner bound, select a minor
attaining it at such a conjugate. Its numerator as a function of
\(A\) in \(S(APA^{-1})\) is a nonzero polynomial. Their finite
product, together with \(\det A\), is nonzero over \(\mathbb Q\).
It has a nonzero integer evaluation: if its degree is \(D\), the
grid \(\{0,\ldots,D\}^{m^2}\) suffices by the elementary univariate
root bound and induction. Thus a common integer \(A\) exists and is
found by finite enumeration independent of the input length.

Conjugate all frame labels and the form in the finite description.
All common-frame identities and residual ranks persist, and
\(\operatorname{diag}(A,A)\) commutes with the full swap. The
endpoint is therefore the original physical interchange, without a
runtime \(A\) or \(A^{-1}\) pass. Compile each rational factor
\(L S(P) R=\Pi\) as \(L^{-1}\Pi R^{-1}\). Choose the fixed odd
address prime after these tables, avoiding all denominators and
nonzero pivot/diagonal numerators. Then the same factors hold modulo
every \(q^f\), with unit diagonals. Lower triangular operations and
their inverses are exactly the already priced later-target rotations
and fixed-unit scalings. Only the aligned middle pairs are merged;
the reversed outer pairs stay individually charged.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Independent E8 interchange]
\label{thm:e8swap}
Two equal \(u\)-bit address fields, with arbitrary complete prefix,
gap and suffix fields and arbitrary opaque records, can be exchanged
in \(O(V(u+1)^{\tau_E})\) fixed-tape bit steps, where
\(\tau_E=1-3/2000000\) and \(V\) is their total bit volume.
The construction uses no phase kernel, numerical multiplication
theorem, NTT supplier or number-theoretic hypothesis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:e8genericpivot} to every residual and work
projector of the finite word just constructed. It changes only their
compilation, not the word or its rank sum. The complete child-width
histogram is
\[
\begin{array}{c|r}
 b&c_b\\\hline
 1&31428518400\\
 370&3456000\\
 372&108864000\\
 384&108993600\\
 496&109123200.
\end{array}
\]
Its first moment is \(\sum_bbc_b=Wm-2N+L\). Each call has the
full current role volume \(V/W\), not that volume divided by its
width. With \(f=\lfloor e/512\rfloor\), the prepared-row cost
divided by current volume satisfies
\[
 F(e)\le\frac1W\sum_bc_bF(bf)+C.
\]
Fewer than 512 leftover radix digits use direct exchanges. Fixed
lower factors, copies, bitwise scalar gates, work streams, row
split/merge, descriptors and local stack movements contribute the
constant parent term. All unselected fields remain complete spectators.

Here is a small exact contraction certificate. Put \(a=3/2000000\),
and for the five widths use the logarithm upper bounds
\[
 \log(512/b)<L_b,\qquad
 (L_1,L_{370},L_{372},L_{384},L_{496})
       ={1\over1000}(6239,326,320,288,32).
\]
Positive 80-term exponential sums already exceed \(512/b\),
proving these bounds. Since \(e^y\le1/(1-y)\) for \(0\le y<1\),
\[
 {1\over W}\sum_bc_b(b/512)^{1-a}
 \le \sum_b\frac{bc_b}{512W(1-aL_b)}
 =\frac{53354609712617889603744709651135881857421875}
        {53354613096691794374204281653199297832914087}
 <1-10^{-8}.
\]
Induction on the actual integer width closes the recurrence at
\(F(e)=O((e+1)^{\tau_E})\), including every finite base width.

The row supply and arbitrary-width wrapper require care. The largest
child ratio is \(496/512=31/32\), and \((31/32)^{32}<1/2\).
Reserve once at the root a budget
\(D_0=32(\lceil\log_2(e+1)\rceil+1)\). Choose the least
\(r\) with \(q^{2r}\ge W^{D_0}\); then \(r=O(\log(e+2))\).
For the fixed small range where \(r\ge e\), use direct digit moves.
Otherwise write the physical fields as
\((H^+,H^-,G,D^+,D^-)\), with the two high parts of width \(r\).
First exchange \(H^+,D^+\), then move \(H^+\) left across
\(H^-,G\). These charged short-field moves give
\((D^+,H^+,H^-,G,D^-)\). Regard the joined high parts' \(q^{2r}\)
values as a row reservoir; the low selected chunks have width \(e-r\).
Pad this row range once to
\(W^{D_0}\lceil q^{2r}/W^{D_0}\rceil<2q^{2r}\).
Every internal call inherits budget \(b-1\) and row count \(R/W\)
from its parent; it neither recomputes a fresh conservative budget
nor adds padding. The root budget bounds every actual descendant depth.

The local LIFO schedule parks inactive current streams, executes one
fully charged child, and returns without scanning parked ancestors.
Both original low-chunk ranges remain present among selected fields
and spectators. As in the earlier interchange proof this gives
\(\log V_0=O(\log(2V_j))\) at depth \(j\), so polynomial
descriptor work is paid by the active child volume. Fixed numbers
of selected controls determine every later-field offset; a large
spectator address is never decoded afresh for each record.
Completed calls restore the entire row index. The core has then changed
the fields to \((D^+,H^+,D^-,G,H^-)\). Only then crop the row pad
and move \(H^+\) right across \(D^-,G\), obtaining precisely
\((D^+,D^-,G,H^+,H^-)\). All high-field movements together cost
\(O(Vr)=O(V\log(e+2))\).
Temporary invalid rows may be nonzero internally; completion restores
them. This smaller toll fits the asserted power.

Finally pad each original binary range numerically to the least
\(q^e\ge2^u\), with factor below \(q^2\) for both fields together.
This is outside the whole radix procedure and is not repeated in a
child. The final full swap preserves the original valid square, so
crop only on its completed boundary. Integer power comparisons select
all widths and budgets. Since \(e=O(u+1)\), the binary bound follows,
uniformly for every record width and spectator range.
\end{proof}
