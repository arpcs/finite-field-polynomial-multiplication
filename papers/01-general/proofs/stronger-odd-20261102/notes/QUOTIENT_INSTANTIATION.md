
# Selector identities in the odd cyclotomic quotient

The following argument proves the selector identities directly in the
odd cyclotomic quotient. Its formalization status and the remaining
carrier-specific proof obligations are stated below.

## Result

For an odd prime \(p\), \(\nu\ge1\), and an odd squarefree
\(\alpha>1\) with \(p\nmid\alpha\), the actual host

\[
 R=\mathbb Z/p^\nu\mathbb Z,\qquad
 A=R[I]/(I^2+1),\qquad
 B=A[U]/(\Phi_\alpha(U)),\qquad D=\varphi(\alpha)
\]

does satisfy the written three-C premises. Its exact finite-etale local
factors have residue degrees dividing \(D\); in each factor the maximal
ideal is generated by \(p\). Therefore, for

\[
 E=(p^D-1)p^\nu,\quad f=\mu^E,\quad v=(1-f)\mu,
\]

one has \(f^2=f\) and \(v^\nu=0\) for every \(\mu\in B\). There is no
mathematical obstruction in the actual quotient and no \(T^2=2\) coordinate
is needed. The remaining gap is formal implementation: the checked bundle
does not prove these selector and canonical-quotient facts for this carrier.

For the fixed-field core case \(\nu=1\), the selector closes by one
Frobenius identity directly in \(B\): \(f=\mu^{p^D-1}\) is already
idempotent and \((1-f)\mu=0\). No local decomposition, principal-unit
lifting, or Newton iteration is needed in that case. The stated general
exponent \(E=(p^D-1)p^\nu\) gives the same \(f\) when \(\nu=1\), because an
idempotent is fixed by every positive power.

For general \(\nu\), there is also a shorter selector proof using Frobenius
in \(B/pB\) and an elementary \(p\)-adic idempotent lift. It proves the
same exact exponent without formalizing the local-factor decomposition, and
is the narrowest bridge to the already checked generic three-C composition.

## Exact local factors and residue degrees

Both successive moduli are monic, so the iterated AdjoinRoot model of
\(B\) is free of rank \(2D\) over \(R\). The coefficient ring \(R\) is a
finite local Artin ring with maximal ideal \(pR\) and \(p^\nu=0\).

First reduce modulo \(p\). The algebra

\[
 \bar A=\mathbb F_p[I]/(I^2+1)
\]

is etale because \(p\ne2\), and is either \(\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_p\)
or the unramified quadratic field \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\). Thus each residue
field factor of \(\bar A\) has degree \(s\in\{1,2\}\) over \(\mathbb F_p\).
Put \(k=\operatorname{ord}_\alpha(p)\). Since \(p\nmid\alpha\), every
irreducible factor of \(\Phi_\alpha\) over \(\mathbb F_{p^s}\) has degree

\[
 r_s=\operatorname{ord}_\alpha(p^s)=k/\gcd(k,s).
\]

This is the Frobenius orbit length of a primitive \(\alpha\)-th root. In
particular, the residue fields of \(\bar B\) have degrees

\[
 d_s=s r_s=\operatorname{lcm}(k,s)
\]

over \(\mathbb F_p\). Lagrange gives \(k\mid D\). For \(s=1\), \(d_s=k\mid
D\). For \(s=2\), \(d_s=k\) if \(k\) is even, while \(d_s=2k\mid D\)
if \(k\) is odd, because \(D\) is even. The last fact follows from
\(D=\varphi(\alpha)\) and odd \(\alpha>1\).

More explicitly, choose the two residue components of \(\bar A\) in the
split case, or its single quadratic residue field in the nonsplit case.
Over each such field, factor \(\Phi_\alpha\) into its \(D/r_s\) distinct
monic irreducibles \(q_j\), all of degree \(r_s\). Their pairwise
coprimality follows from separability: \(\Phi_\alpha\) divides
\(X^\alpha-1\), whose derivative \(\alpha X^{\alpha-1}\) is coprime to it
in characteristic \(p\). Hensel lifting across the nilpotent ideal \(pR\)
lifts this coprime factorization uniquely. The resulting factors of \(B\)
are the corresponding monic quotients by the lifted \(q_j\). Each is a
finite unramified local algebra over the corresponding local factor of
\(A\), with maximal ideal exactly \(p\) times that factor and residue field
\(\mathbb F_{p^{d_s}}\). In the split case there are two families (one over
each \(R\) component); in the nonsplit case there is one family over the
unramified quadratic \(R\)-algebra. These factors have total rank \(2D\),
as required by the monic basis.

Consequently, on a local factor \(B_j\), a unit \(\mu_j\) has residue in
\(\mathbb F_{p^{d_j}}^\times\), killed by \(p^D-1\) since \(d_j\mid D\).
Thus \(\mu_j^{p^D-1}=1+py\), and repeated binomial lifting gives
\((1+py)^{p^{\nu-1}}=1\) modulo \(p^\nu B_j=0\). The exponent in this
note uses \(p^\nu\), one extra \(p\), so \(\mu_j^E=1\). If \(\mu_j\) is a
nonunit, it lies in \(pB_j\), hence \(\mu_j^\nu=0\) and \(\mu_j^E=0\);
here \(E\ge p^\nu\ge\nu\).
Therefore \(f\) is componentwise \(1\) on unit factors and \(0\) on
nonunit factors. This proves \(f^2=f\); \(v\) is \(0\) on the former and
\(\mu_j\in pB_j\) on the latter, so \(v^\nu=0\).

The local-factor paragraph is a structural proof, not a computed
factorization or a per-\(\mu\) algorithm.

## Frobenius selector proof (preferred formal route)

The selector conclusion has a direct proof in the quotient, without using
the local factorization above. Let \(Q=p^D\) and reduce to

\[
 \bar B=B/pB=\mathbb F_p[I,U]/(I^2+1,\Phi_\alpha(U)).
\]

The \(D\)-fold Frobenius \(x\mapsto x^Q\) fixes every generator:

* it fixes each scalar in \(\mathbb F_p\);
* \(D\) is even, so \(Q\equiv1\pmod4\), and \(I^2=-1\) gives \(I^Q=I\);
* \(k=\operatorname{ord}_\alpha(p)\mid D\), hence \(Q\equiv1\pmod\alpha\),
  and \(U^\alpha=1\) gives \(U^Q=U\).

Frobenius is a ring homomorphism in characteristic \(p\), and \(\bar B\) is
generated as an \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebra by \(I,U\). Hence

\[
 \bar x^Q=\bar x\qquad(\bar x\in\bar B).
 \tag{F}
\]

For arbitrary \(\mu\in B\), put \(y=\mu^{Q-1}\). From (F),

\[
 y^2-y=\mu^{Q-2}(\mu^Q-\mu)\in pB.
 \tag{S1}
\]

Use the checked power-polynomial Taylor identity from
OddPowerHensel.powerTaylor. If
\(z^2-z=p^{t+1}b\), write \(d=z^2-z\). Then \(z^2=z+d\), and
\[
 (z^p)^2-z^p=(z^2)^p-z^p
 =p z^{p-1}d+d^2R
 =p^{t+2}\bigl(z^{p-1}b+p^t b^2R\bigr),
\]
where \(R=\texttt{powerRemainder}(z,d,p)\). The derivative identity
\(\texttt{powerDerivative}(z,p)=p z^{p-1}\) holds for \(p>0\).
Thus each p-power raises the defect precision by one, using only a
commutative-ring Taylor identity; no divisibility fact about binomial
coefficients is needed. Iterating \(\nu\) times from (S1) gives

\[
 f:=y^{p^\nu}=\mu^{(Q-1)p^\nu}=\mu^E,\qquad f^2-f=0,
\]

since \(p^\nu B=0\). Reducing (S1) modulo \(p\) says \(\bar y\) is
idempotent, so \(\bar f=\bar y\). Then

\[
 f\mu\equiv y\mu=\mu^Q\equiv\mu\pmod{pB};
\]

thus \(v=(1-f)\mu\in pB\), and \(v^\nu=0\). This proves the exact
selector premises in the nested quotient directly. It uses neither a field
factorization nor an unproved unit/nonunit dichotomy in \(B\).

### The fixed-field specialization \(\nu=1\)

Now \(B=\bar B\) and (F) holds in \(B\) itself. Set \(y=\mu^{Q-1}\).
Then

\[
 y^2-y=\mu^{Q-2}(\mu^Q-\mu)=0,\qquad y\mu=\mu^Q=\mu.
\]

Since \(E=(Q-1)p\), \(f=\mu^E=y^p=y\) (as \(y\) is idempotent), so
\(f^2=f\) and \(v=(1-f)\mu=0\). In the checked composition interface this
is exactly \(q=1\), with \(1\le2^0\). The nilpotent square-root iteration
starts at \(x_0=1\) with \(\delta_0=v=0\), hence has no nontrivial step.
This closes the source/proof-only selector gap for the fixed odd-field
core even before the general \(p\)-adic lift is formalized.

## Explicit canonical \(U\), Newton, and three-C conclusion

Write \(F(X)=\Phi_\alpha(X)\), \(c_\alpha=F(1)\), and
\(H(X)=(F(X)-c_\alpha)/(X-1)\). For squarefree \(\alpha\),
\(c_\alpha=\ell\) if \(\alpha\) is prime \(\ell\), and \(c_\alpha=1\)
otherwise. The prime \(\ell\) cannot be \(p\), so \(c_\alpha\) is a unit in
\(R\). In \(B\),

\[
 U^\alpha=1,\qquad
 U^{-1}=U^{\alpha-1},\qquad
 (U-1)^{-1}=-c_\alpha^{-1}H(U).
\]

For \(j=1,2\), since \(\alpha\) is odd,

\[
 (1+U^j)\sum_{a=0}^{\alpha-1}(-U^j)^a
 =1-(-U^j)^\alpha=2.
\]

With \(h=2^{-1}\in R\), this gives the explicit inverses

\[
 (U+1)^{-1}=h\sum_{a<\alpha}(-U)^a,\qquad
 (U^2+1)^{-1}=h\sum_{a<\alpha}(-U^2)^a.
\]

Thus \(u_P=U\) is admissible for every odd \(p\), including \(p=3,5\),
with no auxiliary square root of two.

For each \(\mu\), use the selector above and \(v=(1-f)\mu\). Choose
\(k_0\) with \(\nu\le2^{k_0}\), set \(a=1-v\), \(x_0=1\),
\(\delta_j=1-a x_j^2\), and

\[
 x_{j+1}=x_j(1+h\delta_j),\qquad
 \delta_{j+1}=h^2\delta_j^2(3+\delta_j).
\]

Then \(\delta_0=v\), \(\delta_j\in(v^{2^j})\), and \(\delta_{k_0}=0\).
With \(w=ax_{k_0}\), \(w^2=1-v\) and \(wx_{k_0}=1\). The existing
idempotent patch combines the shared admissible \(u_P=U\) on the \(f\)
part with \(u_N=Iw\) on the \(1-f\) part. It constructs units
\(u,u^{-1},(u-1)^{-1},(u+1)^{-1},c,c^{-1}\) with
\(u^2+1=c\mu\).

Set \(a_C=(1+I)h\), \(b_C=(1-I)h\), and
\(C=\begin{bmatrix}a_C&b_C\\b_C&a_C\end{bmatrix}\). For
\(b=(u-1)(u+1)^{-1}\), direct multiplication gives

\[
 C\operatorname{diag}(u,1)C\operatorname{diag}(b,1)C
 =\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\0&\delta\end{bmatrix},
\quad \alpha=-2Ia_Cu(u+1)^{-1},\quad
 \beta=a_C(u^2+1)(u+1)^{-1},\quad
 \delta=-Ia_C(u-1).
\]

Both pivots are units. Normalize them on the left by their explicit
inverses and conjugate the resulting shear by
\(z=-2Iu c^{-1}\), whose inverse is \(z^{-1}=Ih u^{-1}c\). Since
\(\alpha^{-1}\beta=Ih c\mu u^{-1}\), the exact word

\[
 \operatorname{diag}(z\alpha^{-1},\delta^{-1})\ C\
 \operatorname{diag}(u,1)\ C\
 \operatorname{diag}((u-1)(u+1)^{-1},1)\ C\
 \operatorname{diag}(z^{-1},1)
 =\begin{bmatrix}1&\mu\\0&1\end{bmatrix}
\]

has three positive \(C\) slots and only unit diagonals. In the current
checked interface, the exact generic bridge is
OddAdmissibleThreeCComposition.admissible_parameter_to_threeC; its
four canonical \(u_P\)-witnesses are inputs. The explicit diagonal inverse
products are
OddGeneralThreeCUnitWitnesses.explicit_word_diagonal_inverse_products.

## Formalization boundary and proof interfaces

The generic formal interface is conditional at this boundary. In OddCheckedThreeCBundle, principal_root_threeC_with_unit_diagonals assumes selector idempotence, complementary nilpotence, and a PrincipalRootWitness. It does not prove those selector premises for the finite quotient. The generic interface also does not instantiate the canonical quotient or its U-inverse identities. The theorem OddAdmissibleThreeCComposition.admissible_parameter_to_threeC is the appropriate bridge for canonical U: it accepts the four explicit inverse witnesses and requires neither a principal-root typeclass nor T^2=2.

For the fixed-field core \(\nu=1\), the smallest useful formal target is
the quotient Frobenius fact

    theorem pow_pD_eq_self_mod_p (x : Bbar) : x ^ (p ^ D) = x

Prove it by AdjoinRoot.induction_on or AdjoinRoot.algHom_ext, first for
\(\mathbb F_p[I]/(I^2+1)\) and then for the \(\Phi_\alpha\) quotient.
The pinned Mathlib 4.34.1 API supplies CharP.frobenius_def,
CharP.iterate_frobenius, AdjoinRoot.induction_on, and AdjoinRoot.algHom_ext.
Scalar fixedness is the finite-field power-card lemma; generator fixedness
is the two congruences above, with Euler's theorem for \(p^D\equiv1
\pmod\alpha\). The generic selector conclusion then follows by two ring
identities, and can be fed to the checked composition with \(q=1,k=0\).

For general \(\nu\), add a generic power-lift lemma of the form

    lemma idempotent_lift_p_power
        (h : z ^ 2 - z = (p : B) ^ (a + 1) * b) :
        exists c, (z ^ p) ^ 2 - z ^ p = (p : B) ^ (a + 2) * c

using OddPowerHensel.powerTaylor. This avoids prime-binomial divisibility:
with \(d=z^2-z=p^{a+1}b\), Taylor gives
\((z+d)^p-z^p=pz^{p-1}d+d^2R\), and both terms factor by
\(p^{a+2}\). Structural induction on the iterate yields
\(z_k^2-z_k=p^{k+1}c_k\); at \(k=\nu\) (or \(\nu-1\)) this vanishes
because \(p^\nu B=0\). The exact selector is then
\(f=(\mu^{p^D-1})^{p^\nu}=\mu^{(p^D-1)p^\nu}\).

For a formal local-factor theorem, the two standard-etale witnesses are explicit. With \(h=2^{-1}\),
the first is
\[
 (2X)(-hX)+(X^2+1)\cdot1=1.
\]
For \(F=\Phi_\alpha\) and \(G=(X^\alpha-1)/F\), differentiating \(FG=X^\alpha-1\)
gives the second identity
\[
 F'\,(\alpha^{-1}XG)+F\,(\alpha^{-1}XG'-G)=1.
\]
Here \(\alpha\) is a unit because \(p\nmid\alpha\). Thus each monic
AdjoinRoot quotient is the \(g=1\) case of StandardEtalePair and is finite
etale over its base. Construct successive AdjoinRoot quotients using
Polynomial.Monic.free_adjoinRoot / finite_adjoinRoot; then use
IsArtinianRing.quotNilradicalPowEquivPi,
FormallyUnramified.quotient, and
FormallyUnramified.map_maximalIdeal to identify each local factor's
maximal ideal with \(p\) times that factor. The pinned Mathlib API
includes Polynomial.natDegree_of_dvd_cyclotomic_of_irreducible, which
supplies the finite-field input for \(r_s\). That version has no built-in
IsLocalRing instance for ZMod (p^nu), so prove prime-power localness from
the unit criterion before using the local-ring API.

The written actual-quotient argument proves the selector claim under the stated hypotheses. The remaining formalization gap is concrete: prove the quotient Frobenius lemma, the generic power-lift lemma for nu>1, the four canonical U-inverse identities, and a specialization theorem that feeds them into the generic three-C module. For the fixed-field core nu=1, the generic power-lift and local-decomposition formalization are not blockers. This formalization boundary is separate from the native-machine dependency.
