\subsection{Odd-field kernels through grouped native matching layers}
\label{sec:oddgroupedtransfer}
This transfer reuses the existing odd-ring phase, native matching and
record-routing constructions in
\href{native_odd_source_snapshot.tex.txt}{the finite-ring manuscript}.
In particular its stage-two interchange import and mixed finite word
are antecedents, not new constructions here. The new step is to group
fixed tensor axes before adjoining dummy coordinates, while reserving
other original axes to meet the routing service's wide-record condition.
This avoids a mask-by-mask lift of that entire recursive word.
Broad fixed-matrix tensor acceleration is already prior work: Alman's
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2211.04643v1}{SOSA 2023 paper},
Sections 1.1--1.2, explicitly covers fixed Kronecker powers using
width-charged RAM lookup tables. The proposed distinction here is the
power saving in tensor height on a fixed number of sequential tapes,
with generated layouts and arbitrary opaque lanes. The general tensor
applicability and the earlier local dense-leaf reduction are not new.

Put
\[
 \tau_L=1-\frac{123879734039}{10^{15}},\qquad
 \eta=\frac{10^{15}}{10^{15}+123879734039}=\frac1{2-\tau_L},
 \qquad \beta_*=1-\frac1{10000}.
\]
The named external machine theorem is
\texttt{Stage2Machine.Bound.uniform\_bound}, with its ordinary-bit
adapter already recorded in
\href{PINNED_STAGE2_BINARY_MOVEMENT_BRIDGE_20261030.txt}{the pinned movement audit}.
The source-dependent phase theorem used below is
\texttt{thm:stage2phase} of the finite-ring manuscript. Its separate
global integer multiplier is not used.

\begin{paperlemma}[Native matching service with its actual record condition]
\label{lem:oddgroupedservice}
Fix a finite field $G$ of odd characteristic and $i\in G$ with $i^2=-1$.
Put $C=((1+i)I+(1-i)X)/2$. Let $n_j$ be even native radices,
$M=\prod_j n_j$, $\Lambda=\lceil\log_2(2M)\rceil$, and
$\Lambda^3\le n_j\le R$, with $\log R=\Oh(\log(\Lambda+2))$.
For each fixed family $\mathcal G$ of polynomial-time public generators
there is a fixed effective polynomial $P_{G,\mathcal G}$ such that,
on records of $W\ge P_{G,\mathcal G}(R,\Lambda)$ bits, the existing
constructions apply
\begin{enumerate}
\item a tensor of supplied local record permutations and its inverse;
\item a tensor which applies $C$ on the two-cycles of supplied native
involutions and identity on their fixed points,
\end{enumerate}
in $\Oh_{G,\mathcal G}(MW(\Lambda+1)^\eta\log^c(\Lambda+2))$ bit steps.
For the linear operator, a record is a serialized vector of $G$
coefficient lanes; permutations also accept arbitrary bit payloads.
The generators run in polynomial time in $R,\Lambda$.
Ordinary native order, setting generation, private
padding, record codecs and cleanup are included.
\end{paperlemma}
\begin{proof}
The relevant source results are \texttt{prop:nativephase},
\texttt{prop:nativecontrolled}, \texttt{lem:nativeselector}, and
\texttt{thm:stage2phase}. Substitute the stage-two interchange tariff
in their actual constructions. A native endpoint block is exchanged
with a separate binary field by three frozen-mask updates. Its integer
tile and movement dock are distinct. The complement of the safe native
quotient margins has fraction $\Oh(\Lambda^{-2})$; exact destination-key
repair pays all full-record sort passes. The untouched native suffix
provides the exponential index reservoir for address preparation.
The completed endpoint swap fixes all spectators. Binary phase on that
field, followed by the inverse swap, therefore gives the native endpoint
tensor without an interleaved-bit gather. There are only
$\Oh(\log R)$ completed blocks.

The source's ragged routing uses one global tensor shuffle per level,
target-only two-call phase switches and selective translations. The
shuffle is a flat doubling permutation followed by a charged endpoint
rotation. Every stage finishes its own temporary numerical padding.
Unconditional local permutations are the control-free case of that
construction. Its setting algorithm performs polynomial$(R,\Lambda)$
work on each record; this is precisely why the condition on $W$ is
retained. It is not implied by a one-symbol payload.
At exponent $\eta$ the unequal-field and binary-phase costs coincide;
keeping all fixed secondary logarithms prices the construction as stated.

For a matching, pair the fixed points in any generated order and move
all pairs to adjacent canonical positions. Use five endpoint calls,
with post-call diagonal lists
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 \text{genuine pair}&I&Z&-iZ&Z&-iZ\\
 \text{added pair}&Z&-iZ&S&S&-iS
 \end{array},\qquad Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),\quad
 S=\operatorname{diag}(1,i).
\]
The identities $CZC(-iZ)=I$ and $CSCSC(-iS)=I$ prove respectively
the identity and $C$ targets after five calls. Undo the pair ordering.
Product diagonals are generated by sequential prefix expansions in
linear coordinate volume. Added pairs may mix live data with dummy
coordinates inside the word; they return identity only at its endpoint.

For completeness the coefficient extension is harmless but not free.
If $\mathbb F_p(i)$ is quadratic, split $G$ into fixed-many lanes over
that field and use the source's Gaussian residue representation.
If $i\in\mathbb F_p$, encode each prime-field lane into the source's
formal Gaussian ring and evaluate its formal $i$ at the chosen root
after the complete call. This gives the required $G$-linear operator
with a fixed linear encoding/decoding cost. Opaque record permutations
instead use the source's fixed $A_3$ bit codec. These are complete-call
constant factors, not recursive branching factors. This reconstructs
the named service and preserves its source-theorem dependency.
\end{proof}

\begin{paperlemma}[A fixed two-coordinate generator in odd characteristic]
\label{lem:oddgroupedgenerator}
In a fixed extension $G$ containing $i$ and with $|G|>5$, every fixed
matrix is a finite word in two-coordinate $C$ matching factors and
diagonals. Singular matrices require one additional zero/one rank diagonal.
\end{paperlemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $u\notin\{0,1,-1,i,-i\}$ and set $a=(1+i)/2$,
$z=-(u+1)/(u-1)$. Direct multiplication gives the upper-triangular
matrix
\[
 T=C\operatorname{diag}(u,1)C^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(z,1)C,
 \quad T_{11}=\frac{-2au}{u-1},\quad T_{22}=a(u+1),\quad
 T_{12}=\frac{ia(u^2+1)}{u-1}.
\]
All three displayed entries are nonzero. Left normalization by
$\operatorname{diag}(T_{11}^{-1},T_{22}^{-1})$ gives
$I+\kappa E_{12}$, where $\kappa=-i(u^2+1)/(2u)\ne0$.
Unit diagonal conjugation supplies any nonzero coefficient; reversing
the ordered pair supplies the opposite transvection. Since $C^2=X$
and $C^4=I$, this uses five positive $C$ calls. Elimination now gives
a fixed word for every fixed invertible matrix; row swaps include their
diagonal sign. Row/column elimination gives the singular case with a
zero/one rank diagonal. All choices are finite field-dependent constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{paperlemma}[Grouped padding with wide lanes from original axes]
\label{lem:oddgroupedpadding}
Fix $d\ge2$ and write $k=5h+r_0$, $0\le r_0<5$. One $h$-axis block
can be represented by native even axes satisfying
Lemma~\ref{lem:oddgroupedservice}, with total padding ratio below two.
The other four original blocks provide the required wide records,
uniformly in an arbitrary external payload. Encoding and cropping cost
linear total volume; no whole-array pass per original axis is needed.
\end{paperlemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $c_d=\lceil\log_2d\rceil+3$ and take the least $t$ with
$d^t\ge(c_dh)^4$. For sufficiently large $h$, write
$r=\lfloor h/t\rfloor$, $v=h-rt<t\le r$. Use $v$ groups of size
$t+1$ and $r-v$ of size $t$, in original axis order. Their sizes
sum exactly to $h$. Let $D_j=d^{t_j}$ and take $n_j=D_j+1$ if $d$
is odd, $n_j=D_j$ otherwise. All $n_j$ are even. Moreover
\[
 \frac{M}{d^h}\le\prod_j(1+D_j^{-1})
 \le\frac1{1-\sum_jD_j^{-1}}<2,
 \quad \Lambda\le h\lceil\log_2d\rceil+2\le c_dh,
\]
and
\[
 n_j\ge(c_dh)^4\ge\Lambda^3,\qquad
 R\le d^2(c_dh)^4+1.
\]
The product bound follows by expanding the product and bounding its
elementary symmetric sums by powers of $\sum_jD_j^{-1}<1/2$.
Thus $\log R=\Oh_d(\log(\Lambda+2))$.

With a fixed $G$ encoding, a record has width proportional to
$W=d^{4h+r_0}L$ for arbitrary $L\ge1$. This is eventually larger
than $P_{G,\mathcal G}(R,\Lambda)$ for any fixed generators used below.
Their effective polynomial determines a fixed direct-branch cutoff.
To pad, enumerate the complete native product
in lexicographic order. At a valid tuple read the next whole original
record; at an invalid tuple emit a zero record. Polynomial address and
descriptor work per record is absorbed by the unchanged exponential
$W$, also uniformly when $L$ grows. The relative order of the valid
tuples is exactly the original grouped $d$-ary order. Selection gives
the inverse crop and the actual transpose of the insertion.

The finitely many smaller $h$ use fixed direct kernels, linear in all
external lanes. Bringing an original block first and restoring it uses
fixed-many whole-range interchanges with the stage-two tariff. Its
numerical padding is removed at each complete interchange. Original
dummy coordinates remain present throughout a native matching word.
They are cropped only after the extended target preserves the valid
coordinate subspace. No phase child is enlarged by this outer padding.
\end{proof}

\begin{papertheorem}[A stronger common exponent for odd-field fixed kernels]
\label{thm:oddgroupedkernel}
Using the named stage-two interchange theorem and the existing finite-ring
constructions above, fix any finite field $K$ of odd characteristic,
$d\ge2$, and $B\in\operatorname{Mat}_d(K)$. For all $k\ge0,L\ge1$,
the ordinary map $B^{\otimes k}\otimes I_L$ has fixed-tape bit cost
\[
 \Oh_{B,K}\bigl(d^kL(k+1)^\eta\log^{C_{B,K}}(k+2)\bigr)
   =\Oh_{B,K}\bigl(d^kL(k+1)^{\beta_*}\bigr).
\]
Its field transpose and ordinary prime-field coordinate transpose have
the same bounds. This is a source-dependent mathematical transfer;
it does not assert a new proof build of the imported machine theorem.
\end{papertheorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a fixed $G$ containing $K,i$ with $|G|>5$ and embed coefficients
once. For an active block use Lemma~\ref{lem:oddgroupedpadding}.
At original position $s<t+1$, apply the fixed word for $B$ simultaneously
in every group containing that position, using identity in shorter groups.
A two-coordinate $C$ factor becomes the native involution pairing those
two digit values for every context of the other original digits. It fixes
the dummy coordinate. Unit or rank diagonals are the corresponding local
digit functions, with dummy value one. Each is one tensor matching or
diagonal job over all groups, not one whole-volume call per group.
All pair orderings and diagonal tables have polynomial$(R,\Lambda)$
generators. Lemmas~\ref{lem:oddgroupedservice} and
\ref{lem:oddgroupedgenerator} price the $\Oh_{B,K}(\log(h+2))$ jobs.

The completed native target is
$\bigotimes_j(B^{\otimes t_j}\oplus I_{n_j-D_j})$.
It preserves every dummy pattern, so cropping gives exactly
$B^{\otimes h}$. Inside its five-call matching realization a dummy
may contain live data; no crop is inserted there. Tensor diagonals,
including zero entries, use prefix-product streams without scalar
division. To select an original block, exchange only whole aggregate
numerical ranges, pad each complete rectangle by a bounded factor, and
crop at the completed transpose boundary. A fixed number of these layouts
costs $\Oh(d^kL(k+1)^\eta\log^c(k+2))$ by the same stage-two supplier.
The five original block calls and the bounded residual axes therefore
give the stated bound in original order, uniformly in $L$.
Finite word lengths and encodings multiply complete calls only.

The integer inequality
$9999\cdot123879734039>10^{15}$ proves $\eta<\beta_*$.
It absorbs every fixed secondary logarithm. Apply the construction to
$B^T$ for the field transpose; the fixed $K/\mathbb F_p$ trace-Gram
wrappers price its ordinary coordinate transpose linearly.
\end{proof}
